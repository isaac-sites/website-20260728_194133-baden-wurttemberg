## Introduction

When an unidentified object appears over a naval base, energy terminal or government building in [Schleswig-Holstein]({{ 'what-is-really-seen-over-schleswig/' | relative_url }}), the central question is no longer simply “What was in the sky?” Authorities must decide whether the report concerns an aircraft, a lawful civilian drone, a mistaken observation, an airspace violation or deliberate reconnaissance.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Schleswig-Holstein_76916e_protected_site_respo_56543d-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Official Response" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Recent cases show both the reach and the limits of the official response. Police can secure a scene, record sightings, deploy detection equipment and investigate immediate danger. Prosecutors can open criminal proceedings when there is evidence of unlawful flying, security-sensitive photography, sabotage preparation or foreign intelligence activity. Yet identifying the operator is often much harder than confirming that a drone was present. The 2024 Brunsbüttel investigation, for example, established that some drone flights had occurred but ended without a suspect because investigators could not locate the launch points or operators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/Spionage-Drohnen-ueber-Brunsbuettel-Ermittlungen-eingestellt%2Cregionflensburgnews3032.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spionage-Drohnen über Brunsbüttel: Ermittlungen eingestellt &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spionage-Drohnen über Brunsbüttel: Ermittlungen eingestellt &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

This distinction is important within Schleswig-Holstein’s wider UFO history. An unresolved drone investigation is evidence of an identification or attribution gap, not evidence of an exotic craft. At the same time, flights near protected facilities cannot be dismissed merely because their origin remains unknown.

## Why protected sites receive special attention

Schleswig-Holstein contains an unusually concentrated mixture of maritime, military, industrial and energy infrastructure. Relevant locations include naval facilities around Kiel, military training and operational sites, the Kiel Canal, submarine construction, power installations, chemical industry and the Brunsbüttel energy complex.

German aviation rules place significant restrictions on drone operations over or close to sensitive locations. Under Section 21h of the Air Traffic Regulations, operations over and within 100 metres of facilities including military sites, industrial plants, prisons, central energy-generation installations and energy-distribution infrastructure generally require consent or another legal basis. Restrictions also apply around constitutional bodies, police properties and other security-sensitive sites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/luftvo_2015/__21h.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gesetze-im-internet.de">[Gesetze im Internet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gesetze-im-internet.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gesetze im Internet§ 21h Luft VO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">über und innerhalb eines seitlichen Abstands von 100 Metern von der Begrenzung von Industrieanlagen, Justizvollzugsanstalten, Einrichtung...</span></span></span>

The restriction does not mean that every light seen nearby is illegal. Police aircraft, emergency helicopters, authorised survey drones and ordinary aeroplanes may all be operating lawfully. Nor does the appearance of a drone automatically prove espionage. What changes at a protected location is the risk calculation: an unexplained flight may be capable of collecting detailed imagery, mapping security arrangements, monitoring ship movements or testing how quickly authorities respond.

That concern was visible in Brunsbüttel in August 2024. Repeated reports concerned the industrial area beside energy and chemical facilities, including the liquefied natural gas terminal and the former nuclear power station. The Flensburg public prosecutor’s office and state-security investigators examined possible aviation offences and sabotage-related activity. The eventual finding was narrower than some early headlines suggested: several drone flights were considered real, but investigators found neither their operators nor reliable evidence of their precise launch and landing locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.landtag.ltsh.de/nachrichten/24_09_04_i_r_drohnen_brunsbuettel/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: landtag.ltsh.de">[Landtag Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">landtag.ltsh.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Staatsanwaltschaft und Staatsschutz ermitteln schon länger</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Landtag Schleswig-HolsteinDrohnen über Brunsbüttel: Spionage nicht ausgeschlossen4 Sept 2024 — Wer lässt nachts Drohnen über einem Indust...</span></span></span>

The case therefore remained unresolved in an operational sense, but not wholly mysterious. Officials did not confirm the more dramatic public speculation about a particular foreign drone type or state operator. The investigation demonstrated a genuine security problem—unattributed flights near important infrastructure—without establishing who was responsible or what they intended.

## From a sighting report to a police operation

A report of a suspected drone normally begins as a police and public-safety matter. Officers must first determine whether an object is still present, whether it threatens people or air traffic and whether a protected facility is being overflown. They may collect witness descriptions, photographs and video, check known aviation activity and contact the site operator.

This initial verification stage is essential because many reports do not survive technical scrutiny. After a series of sightings over Schleswig-Holstein on the night of 25–26 September 2025, the state government said reports had been received from Dithmarschen, Rendsburg-Eckernförde and Kiel, including areas containing military and critical infrastructure. The objects were described as varying in type and size. Subsequent checks excluded illegal drone activity in a number of cases because the objects were identified as aeroplanes, helicopters or lawful drones. Other reports, including some connected with military infrastructure, could not initially be verified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

That mixed result is typical of investigations into unusual aerial activity. Authorities are rarely dealing with one clean body of evidence. Instead, they receive overlapping calls from witnesses at different locations, with uncertain times, viewing directions and estimates of altitude. Media attention can produce additional reports, some of which concern unrelated objects.

Schleswig-Holstein’s police response is organised around three broad tasks:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Detection:** determining whether an object is physically present, using observers and technical sensors where available.
* **Verification:** distinguishing a drone from conventional aircraft, lawful operations, birds, astronomical objects or other misidentifications.
* **Intervention:** deciding whether the object should be tracked, warned, electronically disrupted, intercepted or otherwise countered.

</div>

The state government said in October 2025 that this “detect, verify and intervene” approach formed the basis of the Landespolizei’s drone concept. Schleswig-Holstein had begun purchasing counter-drone equipment in 2024, allocated further funds in 2025 and created additional posts in the police and domestic intelligence service.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

Intervention is nevertheless more complicated than simply bringing an object down. Radio-frequency interference can affect other systems, while a falling drone may injure people or damage the very facility being protected. A suspected reconnaissance flight may therefore be tracked rather than immediately attacked, especially when its payload, route and level of danger are uncertain.

## When prosecutors become involved

The police deal with immediate danger and evidence collection, but prosecutors direct criminal investigations once there is an initial suspicion that an offence has been committed. In Schleswig-Holstein, prominent protected-site cases have frequently involved the Flensburg public prosecutor’s office, supported by the State Criminal Police Office.

Several possible offences may be considered, depending on what the evidence shows. An unauthorised flight in a restricted geographical area can lead to aviation-law enforcement. Conduct that seriously endangers air traffic may carry criminal liability, while lesser aviation violations can result in substantial fines. The federal government states that air-traffic offences can attract fines of up to €50,000, while a dangerous interference with air traffic can be prosecuted as a criminal offence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/aenderung-luftsicherheitsgesetz-2394310" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bundesregierung.de">[Bundesregierung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bundesregierung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Besserer Schutz gegen Drohnen &#124; Bundesregierung</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Besserer Schutz gegen Drohnen &#124; Bundesregierung...</span></span></span>

A more specialised provision is Section 109g of the German Criminal Code, concerning security-endangering images. It can apply when someone knowingly produces or transmits aerial imagery in circumstances that endanger Germany’s security or the operational effectiveness of the armed forces. The offence requires more than the mere existence of a photograph: investigators must consider what was recorded, the operator’s knowledge, the security consequences and whether the necessary intent can be proved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__109g.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gesetze-im-internet.de">[Gesetze im Internet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gesetze-im-internet.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gesetze im Internet§ 109g St GB</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2) Wer von einem Luftfahrzeug aus eine Lichtbildaufnahme von einem Gebiet oder Gegenstand im räumlichen Geltungsbereich dieses Gesetzes...</span></span></span>

More serious suspicions may involve intelligence activity or preparations connected with sabotage. Such classifications give investigators grounds to examine communications, data storage, travel patterns and links between an aircraft and possible controllers. They do not, by themselves, establish guilt. An investigation “against persons unknown” simply means that the available evidence creates sufficient reason to investigate but has not yet identified a responsible individual.

The September 2025 Schleswig-Holstein sightings illustrate this cautious threshold. The Flensburg prosecutors opened an investigation against an unknown operator on suspicion of security-endangering imaging. At the same time, state officials stressed that many reports had already been explained and that the available information did not establish a specifically heightened, concrete threat to the state.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/drohnen-ueber-schleswig-holstein-staatsanwaltschaft-ermittelt%2Cdrohnen-186.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Drohnen über SH: Staatsanwaltschaft ermittelt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 1, 2025 — 1 Oct 2025 — Drohnen über Einrichtungen kritischer Infrastruktur in Schleswig-Holstein geflogen sind, ermittelt die Sta...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## What investigators need to identify an operator

Confirming a drone is only the first step. A successful prosecution normally requires evidence connecting the aircraft, its flight and its operator. That may include several different strands.

**A reliable track.** Investigators need accurate times, positions, altitude estimates and direction of travel. Several synchronised sensors or witnesses are more useful than a single distant video.

**Technical identification.** Some civilian drones transmit identifying or control information that can help establish the model or operator. More sophisticated aircraft may fly pre-programmed routes, use modified communications or provide little useful radio data.

**A launch or recovery point.** Locating where a drone took off or landed can lead directly to a pilot, vehicle or ship. This becomes especially difficult on Schleswig-Holstein’s coast, where a drone may approach over water or be launched from a moving vessel.

**Usable digital evidence.** Seized phones, controllers, computers, memory cards, cameras and navigation records may reveal flight plans, stored images or communications. Investigators must still prove who controlled the equipment and whether the material relates to the incident under investigation.

**Evidence of purpose.** An unauthorised overflight does not automatically prove espionage. A stronger case may require repeated routes, systematic photography, attention to security features, links to other incidents or communications indicating an intelligence or sabotage objective.

The failure to secure these connections explains why apparently serious cases can end without charges. In Brunsbüttel, prosecutors said some overflights had been established, but no offender could be identified. They had no dependable information about launch and landing locations or the specific targets of the flights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/Spionage-Drohnen-ueber-Brunsbuettel-Ermittlungen-eingestellt%2Cregionflensburgnews3032.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spionage-Drohnen über Brunsbüttel: Ermittlungen eingestellt &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spionage-Drohnen über Brunsbüttel: Ermittlungen eingestellt &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

For readers assessing a dramatic report, this is the most useful distinction to retain. “Authorities could not identify the operator” is not the same as “authorities could not determine whether anything flew there”. Conversely, evidence of a drone does not establish its nationality, ownership or mission.

## Who can stop a suspicious drone?

Responsibility depends on location and the nature of the threat, but civilian police are normally the first authority for domestic danger prevention. The Landespolizei can respond to incidents within Schleswig-Holstein, while the Federal Police has responsibilities in areas including federal transport and border security. The Federal Criminal Police Office, domestic intelligence agencies, military counter-intelligence and the armed forces may contribute information or specialist support where national security or military installations are involved.

This division has repeatedly caused practical concern. A drone may travel rapidly between civilian airspace, military property, a port and waters under different jurisdictions. The authority that detects it may not possess the equipment or legal power needed for immediate intervention, while another authority may arrive too late.

Following the 2025 incidents, Schleswig-Holstein intensified cooperation with the Federal Police, Federal Criminal Police Office, armed forces, federal government, neighbouring German states and Denmark. The northern German states also agreed on joint procurement, exercises and operational support.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

## Why active defence remains difficult

Detection equipment does not remove the need for judgement. Authorities must distinguish an unlawful aircraft from a legal drone before using measures that could interfere with communications or bring an object down. That is especially difficult at night, in coastal weather or around busy ports where multiple legitimate aircraft may be present.

There are also competing risks. Allowing a drone to continue may expose a protected site to surveillance. Disrupting it may cause an uncontrolled crash. Shooting it down near fuel storage, chemical facilities, residential areas or crowded waterfronts could create a greater danger than the original flight.

Private operators of critical infrastructure have traditionally possessed fewer active counter-drone powers than the police. They can improve fencing, observation, lighting, camera coverage and sensor networks, and can provide evidence rapidly to investigators. Their ability to jam, seize control of or destroy an aircraft is much more restricted because such actions may interfere with radio systems, property rights and public safety. The 2026 federal reforms strengthened state capabilities, but did not simply give every power station, shipyard or industrial company an unrestricted right to neutralise aircraft independently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw09-de-luftsicherheitsgesetz-1140448" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bundestag.de">[Deutscher Bundestag]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bundestag.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bundestag.de.</span></span></span>

This is why protected-site response is best understood as a chain rather than a single power: the operator reports and preserves evidence; police assess and manage the immediate danger; specialist units attempt detection or intervention; intelligence bodies consider foreign-state links; and prosecutors decide whether the evidence supports a criminal case.

## What the recent cases establish

Schleswig-Holstein’s protected-site incidents have strengthened the case for better drone detection and coordination, but they have not validated every claim made during periods of intense reporting.

The Brunsbüttel case confirmed that at least some unauthorised drone activity occurred near important industrial infrastructure. It did not identify a pilot, launch platform, country of origin or intelligence service. The investigation was closed in May 2025 because no suspect could be established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/Spionage-Drohnen-ueber-Brunsbuettel-Ermittlungen-eingestellt%2Cregionflensburgnews3032.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spionage-Drohnen über Brunsbüttel: Ermittlungen eingestellt &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spionage-Drohnen über Brunsbüttel: Ermittlungen eingestellt &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

The September 2025 wave produced a similarly mixed picture. Some reports concerned ordinary aircraft or authorised drones; some could not be verified; and officials believed illegal drones had probably flown over parts of the state. The Landeskriminalamt continued assessing the remaining reports, while prosecutors examined possible security-sensitive imaging. Officials publicly rejected the idea that the available evidence demonstrated a new, specific and heightened threat situation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

For Schleswig-Holstein’s UFO record, these incidents mark a shift in official priorities. Earlier unexplained-light reports were often questions of witness perception and later identification. A modern object near a naval or energy facility can trigger a structured security investigation even when its appearance is not especially extraordinary.

The most defensible conclusion is therefore deliberately limited. Unidentified drones near protected sites are a real law-enforcement problem because small aircraft are difficult to detect, follow and attribute. Some Schleswig-Holstein reports have been explained, some flights have been confirmed without an identified operator, and some claims remain unverified. None of those categories should be confused with proof of an unknown technology. The unresolved element is usually who operated the aircraft and why—not whether the incident supports an extraterrestrial interpretation.
