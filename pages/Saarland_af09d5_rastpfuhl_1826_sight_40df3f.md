## Introduction

On 1 April 1826, shortly before 4 pm, brickworker Johannes Becker was carrying dry wood out of woodland at Rastpfuhl, near Saarbrücken, when he heard rapidly intensifying rolls and crashes like thunder. In clear, warm and almost windless weather, he then reported a grey-white form rushing towards him at low level, apparently rolling up and spreading out before disappearing. Other [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-b354a2/' | relative_url }}) confirmed the violent noise and the arrival of a powerful whirlwind, although Becker seems to have been the only person who clearly described the object itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GrewiSaarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen</span></span></span>

The incident is often presented as [Saarland]({{ 'saarlands-ufo-story-between-mystery-and/' | relative_url }})’s earliest UFO case, but that label needs care. The witnesses did not report a spacecraft, and the scientist who published the account considered a whirlwind-like atmospheric event more likely than a fireball. What makes Rastpfuhl important is not proof of an extraordinary craft, but the survival of an unusually detailed, named and partly investigated aerial mystery from more than a century before the flying-saucer era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GrewiSaarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen</span></span></span>

## The event and its witnesses

The reported site lay roughly half an hour’s journey from Saarbrücken, at the edge of a state-owned forest north of the town and about 400 paces from Heinrich König’s brickworks. In modern terms, the area belongs to Rastpfuhl in Saarbrücken-Malstatt, but in 1826 it was a much more open landscape of woodland, fields, tracks, a pond and scattered industrial premises. Local historical material places the episode near the forest edge and the road corresponding broadly to today’s Lebacher Landstraße.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[grewi.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GrewiSaarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen</span></span></span>

Johannes Becker, a brickworker employed by König, supplied the central testimony. He said he had emerged along a forest track carrying dry wood when he heard nearby cracking and rolling resembling an approaching storm. He dropped the wood and looked upwards, but saw only a few scattered light clouds in an otherwise clear sky. The air was still, the sunshine felt very warm, and the noise appeared to be approaching from south-west of Rastpfuhl.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GrewiSaarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen</span></span></span>

According to the published account, Becker then saw something emerge near a clear corner of the wood, perhaps 24 paces away. It travelled with extreme speed at an estimated height of six or seven feet above the ground. He compared its first appearance to two greyish pieces of sheet metal, each approximately one and a half feet wide and three and a half feet high, positioned parallel to one another. Crucially, he reported no flames, glow or surrounding light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GrewiSaarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen</span></span></span>

Becker believed the form rushed directly at him and came down about seven paces away, although [Chladni]({{ 'chladni/' | relative_url }}) later cautioned that this apparent distance might have been an optical error. As it descended, Becker thought it rolled into a cylinder-like shape and then spread out to approximately the size of a linen sheet. The accompanying crash was described in extravagant but unmistakably fearful terms—as though a vast number of thunderstorms were breaking beside him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GrewiSaarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen</span></span></span>

There was then an interval of roughly a minute in which everything became quiet. Noise resumed from the place where the appearance seemed to have come down and moved through the woodland towards König’s brickworks. Becker subsequently heard the roar of a severe whirlwind travelling north-east towards Rußhütte and apparently ending near the Fischbach valley. He estimated that the entire disturbance lasted three or four minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GrewiSaarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen</span></span></span>

### What the other witnesses confirmed

The supporting testimony is important, but it does not amount to several independent sightings of the same object. The surviving account names three principal corroborating witnesses: road keeper Hasse, elderly brickworker Christian Werner and brickworks owner Heinrich König. ## What the surviving account actually says

The most valuable document is a short article by physicist Ernst Florens Friedrich Chladni, published in the 1826 volume of *Annalen der Physik*. Chladni was not a nineteenth-century occultist collecting marvels. He was a major figure in acoustics and an early advocate of the extraterrestrial origin of meteorites, at a time when the idea that stones could fall from space had only recently gained scientific acceptance. His broader work makes it understandable that reports of unusual sounds, fireballs and falling material attracted his attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~loeb/UM1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lweb.cfa.harvard.edu">[Center for Astrophysics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lweb.cfa.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Astrophysics Marvin.fm</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Astrophysics Marvin.fm</span></span></span>

Yet Chladni was not the first investigator to question Becker directly. The evidence passed through several hands:



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Saarland_af09d5_rastpfuhl_1826_sight_40df3f-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Rastpfuhl 1826 illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Saarland_af09d5_rastpfuhl_1826_sight_40df3f-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Saarland_af09d5_rastpfuhl_1826_sight_40df3f-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Witnesses described the occurrence to the Reverend Johann Friedrich Köllner of Malstatt.
2. Köllner visited the scene, interviewed the named people and forwarded the collected information to the district authority.
3. District administrator Dern included the matter in a report to the royal government in Trier.
4. Trier physics and mathematics teacher Steininger extracted the passage and sent it to Chladni.
5. Chladni edited and published it in a scientific journal. [Grewi](https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/)

</div>

This chain gives the story considerably more weight than a legend recorded generations later. The witnesses were named, the site was inspected, and the incident entered an administrative reporting system within weeks or months of the event. It also creates uncertainty. The surviving article is an edited extract rather than a transcript of the original interviews. It is impossible to know which words came directly from Becker, which were introduced by Köllner or Dern, and which were condensed by Steininger or Chladni.

### The physical search found nothing

Köllner reportedly went to the supposed landing or explosion site before anyone else had walked over it. He found no burn marks, scorched vegetation, broken saplings or other disturbance. Grass and young trees appeared untouched. A search of the field also failed to produce meteorites or any other fallen material. [Grewi](https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/)

That negative result substantially weakens a literal reading in which a solid object struck the ground seven paces from Becker. An object large enough to resemble a sheet, moving at the speed he perceived and producing such a violent crash would normally be expected to leave some trace if it genuinely hit the surface. Chladni therefore suggested that Becker might have misjudged the distance, a common problem when a witness sees an unfamiliar phenomenon without reliable reference points. [Grewi](https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/)

The absence of physical evidence does not show that nothing occurred. It does mean the surviving case cannot establish a landing, impact or recovery. The safest reconstruction is that Becker perceived the phenomenon as coming very close to the ground, while its true position and distance remain unknown.

### The missing administrative file

Chladni explicitly stated that his material came from an extract of Dern’s April 1826 report to the government in Trier. That underlying administrative record has not been located. Research described by journalist Andreas Müller found no corresponding document among the limited surviving Dern papers in the Saarland State Archive or in the Federal Archives at Koblenz. One possibility is that Steininger removed or separately retained the relevant extract, breaking its connection with the original file. That remains speculation rather than a demonstrated history of the document. [Grewi](https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/)

Calling the lost papers “Germany’s oldest UFO file” is therefore convenient but slightly misleading. There evidently was an official report about an [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) aerial or meteorological occurrence. There is no evidence that the authorities classified it as anything resembling a modern UFO investigation, still less that they suspected an artificial craft. Its importance lies in official collection of witness statements, not in a nineteenth-century government interest in extraterrestrial visitors.

The [missing file]({{ 'missing-file/' | relative_url }}) also prevents several useful checks. Researchers cannot compare the witnesses’ statements line by line, establish whether they were questioned separately, recover a more exact map, or see whether later correspondence resolved the episode. In evidential terms, Rastpfuhl has a respectable contemporary publication but an incomplete case record.
