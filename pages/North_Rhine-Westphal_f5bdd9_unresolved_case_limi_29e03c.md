## Introduction

An unresolved UFO case in North Rhine-Westphalia does not mean investigators have found evidence of an extraordinary craft. It usually means something more modest: the report cannot be matched confidently to a particular aircraft, balloon, satellite, astronomical object or other ordinary source because essential information is missing. A distant light seen briefly, an uncertain time, a single photograph without original metadata or a witness estimate made after the event may leave several explanations possible but none provable.

That distinction matters in NRW, where dense cities, busy air routes and large numbers of potential observers generate many reports. Civilian investigators based in Lüdenscheid have accumulated decades of case material, including a small residue classified as anomalous or insufficiently explained. Their own classification system separates potentially interesting cases from reports weakened by inadequate observation, unreliable testimony or insufficient data. “Unresolved” is therefore an evidential status, not a finding about what the object was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/forschung/ufo-klassifikationen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ForschungKlassifikationenPROBLEMATIC UFO: UFO weist wesentliche anomale Merkmale auf, die auch unter extremen Bedingungen bei herkömm...</span></span></span>

## Why incomplete reports resist identification

Most UFO reports begin as observations rather than measurements. A witness may accurately remember that a light was bright, fast or strangely coloured, yet still be unable to supply the details needed to reconstruct its movement. Without a reliable time, viewing direction, angular height and duration, investigators may not be able to compare the report properly with flight records, satellite passes, planets, meteors or balloon trajectories.

Distance is an especially serious problem. A light against a dark sky provides few visual clues to scale. An apparently large object moving rapidly may instead be a small nearby object drifting slowly, or a distant aircraft whose movement has been exaggerated because the witness assumed it was closer. Claims of extreme speed or sudden acceleration are consequently weak unless range and position were measured independently.

The GEP’s classification system reflects this problem. It includes categories for reports that appear to contain substantial anomalous features, but also separate classifications for insufficient data, inadequate observation and other exceptions. Its “problematic” category covers cases in which unusual characteristics remain but could still occur under extreme or unfamiliar ordinary conditions. A “good” case is intended to represent stronger unexplained material, not proof of a non-human vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/forschung/ufo-klassifikationen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ForschungKlassifikationenPROBLEMATIC UFO: UFO weist wesentliche anomale Merkmale auf, die auch unter extremen Bedingungen bei herkömm...</span></span></span>

A case from Siegburg illustrates how little a striking description can establish by itself. In September 2010, a witness reported a pale, unlit triangular form crossing the entire sky in four or five seconds. The account contains memorable features—shape, speed and orientation—but a public summary cannot by itself determine range, physical size or whether the appearance resulted from a group of objects, a silhouette, atmospheric conditions or perception during a very brief event. The report is worth preserving precisely because it cannot be reconstructed confidently, but its unresolved quality does not demonstrate impossible flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/ufo-meldungen/dreieckfoermiges-licht-ueber-siegburg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gesellschaft zur Erforschung des UFO-Phänomens (GEP) e.V</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ForschungDreieckförmiges Licht über Siegburg – Gesellschaft zur Erforschung des UFO-Phänomens e.V.October 19, 2010 — Fall-Nr.: 201009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2010</span></span></span>

## Unresolved is not the same as anomalous

Three kinds of case are often blurred together in public discussion.

**Unresolved because data are missing.** The investigator cannot choose between several ordinary possibilities. A short phone video may show a genuine object but provide no usable scale, direction or distance.

**Unresolved despite reasonably good evidence.** Witness testimony, timing and imagery may be better documented, yet no single explanation fits comfortably. Such a case deserves further attention, but uncertainty remains broader than the extraordinary interpretation.

**Evidence of genuinely anomalous performance.** This would require measurements showing behaviour outside known natural or technological limits: for example, independently confirmed speed, acceleration or manoeuvring that cannot be produced by perspective, sensor effects or tracking errors.

Most residual UFO reports in NRW belong to the first two groups. Their labels describe the limits of investigation, not demonstrated aeronautical performance. The same distinction is now explicit in larger institutional studies. NASA’s independent UAP study found that analysis is frequently hampered by poor calibration, missing sensor metadata, a lack of multiple measurements and inadequate baseline data. It also noted that apparent anomalies have sometimes proved to be sensor artefacts once calibration and contextual information were examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Independent Study Team Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportSeptember 13, 2023 — At present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 13, 2023</span></span></span>

The United States All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office uses a similarly cautious meaning of “unresolved”. Its published European cases include recordings that appear to show physical objects with unremarkable behaviour but cannot be attributed to a specific source. Other footage remains open because it is too short or ambiguous to establish whether a signature came from a real object, a reflection, an environmental temperature difference or a display error. ## Ordinary answers can arrive late

A case may look strongest during the first hours of publicity, when witnesses and reporters know little about the surrounding [air traffic]({{ 'air-traffic/' | relative_url }}). Later enquiries can substantially weaken the mystery.

In March 2021, for example, an unusual aircraft seen near Massen and Dortmund Airport was initially described locally as a UFO travelling on an unexpected route. The airport reportedly had not treated it as an unidentified radar target, while information from the control tower connected the sighting to a police helicopter travelling to an operation in Hamm. The important point is not merely that this case was solved. It shows how an object can remain unidentified to the public while already being known within a separate operational system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hellwegeranzeiger.de/unna/ufo-am-flughafen-ist-identifiziert-w1612790-1000357632/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hellwegeranzeiger.de">[Hellweger Anzeiger]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hellwegeranzeiger.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hellweger Anzeiger UFO am Dortmunder Flughafen ist identifizier &#124; Unna</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hellweger Anzeiger UFO am Dortmunder Flughafen ist identifizier &#124; Unna</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 10, 2021</span></span></span>

A photographed object over Gütersloh in 2019 followed a similar pattern. Initial local reporting presented a puzzling object in the morning sky. Subsequent identification by a civilian investigator traced it to a gas balloon. The photograph had recorded something real, but “real object” and “unexplained craft” were never equivalent propositions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nw.de/lokal/kreis_guetersloh/guetersloh/22540449_Raetsel-um-unbekanntes-Flugobjekt-am-Guetersloher-Himmel-geloest.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nw.de">[Neue Westfälische]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nw.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nw.de.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2019</span></span></span>

These examples also expose a survival bias in UFO archives. Resolved cases usually lose public interest, while unresolved reports continue to circulate. Over time, readers encounter a curated residue of mysteries without seeing the much larger number that became aircraft, balloons, [satellites]({{ 'satellites-a19e68/' | relative_url }}), planets or photographic artefacts. The residue can therefore appear more impressive than the investigative record as a whole.

Late identification is not always possible. Flight-tracking histories may be incomplete, balloons may never have been registered and old photographs may survive only as compressed copies. An ordinary cause can become impossible to prove after records disappear. Time may make a weak case permanently unresolved without making it stronger.

## Witness sincerity cannot supply missing measurements

There is no need to accuse a witness of dishonesty simply because an account cannot be verified. People can report an experience sincerely while being mistaken about distance, speed, size or the sequence of events. Memory also changes as a sighting is retold, discussed with others or framed by newspaper coverage.

Multiple witnesses improve a case only when their observations are genuinely independent. Ten people watching the same ambiguous light may confirm that a light was present, but they do not automatically establish its range or nature. If witnesses spoke to one another before giving formal accounts, agreement about shape or movement may partly reflect shared interpretation. Reports gathered separately, with diagrams and precise positions recorded before publicity, carry more weight.

Photographs and videos have related limits. A digital image can confirm that the camera registered a luminous point or shape, but it may not show whether the source was metres away or kilometres away. Autofocus, digital zoom, stabilisation, rolling-shutter distortion, internal reflections and heavy compression can change an object’s appearance. Original files, metadata and the camera’s exact position are therefore more valuable than a dramatic enlargement circulated on social media.

Pilot, police or military testimony deserves careful attention because trained observers may describe aviation conditions better than the average witness. Training does not, however, create missing range data or make perception infallible. A compelling case requires the witness account to agree with independent instruments rather than relying on professional status alone.

## What would make an NRW case genuinely persuasive?

A strong case would not need to be spectacular in description. It would need to be difficult to explain after the ordinary possibilities had been tested with good data.

The most persuasive NRW report would include:

* an exact time synchronised to a reliable clock;
* the observers’ precise locations and unobstructed lines of sight;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* independently recorded bearings and elevations from two or more positions;
* original, unedited imagery with complete metadata;
* known camera specifications and calibration;
* continuous footage showing entry, movement and departure;
* corroborating radar or other sensor records that clearly refer to the same event;
* weather, astronomical, satellite and flight information preserved promptly;
* separate witness statements taken before discussion or media coverage;
* measurable behaviour that remains anomalous after perspective and sensor effects are modelled.

</div>

Triangulation would be particularly valuable. Two calibrated cameras at known locations could establish distance, altitude and three-dimensional movement rather than leaving these quantities to visual estimation. Multispectral imaging, radar-derived range and environmental sensors would help distinguish a solid object from a reflection, balloon, bird, aircraft light or optical artefact. Scientific proposals for purpose-built UAP observation systems emphasise precisely this combination of synchronised, independently calibrated instruments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2023</span></span></span>

A persuasive case would also have to survive attempted disproof. Investigators should actively test the strongest ordinary alternatives rather than merely state that none is obvious. An aircraft explanation should be compared with actual routes and viewing geometry; a satellite explanation with orbital data and illumination conditions; a balloon explanation with wind direction and apparent motion. Only after those comparisons fail does the residual uncertainty become meaningful.

Even then, the conclusion should remain proportional to the evidence. Good data might establish that an object followed an unusual path without revealing who made it. They might show a physical target without demonstrating technology beyond present capabilities. Identification, performance and origin are separate questions.

## The most honest reading of the open files

North Rhine-Westphalia’s unresolved cases are useful because they show where ordinary investigation reaches its limits. They preserve unusual experiences that cannot now be reconstructed completely and occasionally identify reports worthy of renewed analysis. They do not, as a group, establish a hidden class of vehicles displaying impossible performance.

The correct position is neither that every open case has already been explained nor that the unexplained residue proves something extraordinary. Some reports may eventually be matched to ordinary causes. Others will remain open because the necessary records were never created or have been lost. A very small number may contain genuinely unusual observations, but their significance depends on evidence quality rather than the persistence of the mystery.

For NRW’s UFO history, that is the central lesson: “unresolved” is the beginning of a careful evidential judgement, not the end of one.
