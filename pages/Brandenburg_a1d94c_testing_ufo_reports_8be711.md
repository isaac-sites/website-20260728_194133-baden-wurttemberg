## Introduction

A [Brandenburg]({{ 'why-brandenburgs-ufo-record-remains-so/' | relative_url }}) UFO report is tested by rebuilding the observation and then trying to match it against ordinary activity in the same part of the sky. Investigators need an exact time, viewing position, direction, elevation, duration and description of movement. They then compare the account with aircraft routes, satellite passes, planets and stars, meteors, weather, balloons, drones, photographs and reports from other witnesses.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Brandenburg_a1d94c_testing_ufo_reports_8be711-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Testing Reports" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This process matters more than how strange the object sounded. A dramatic account with no fixed time or direction may be impossible to test, while a brief sighting with accurate details can sometimes be solved within minutes. In Brandenburg, where observers share the sky with traffic approaching Berlin Brandenburg Airport, rural airfields, satellites and occasional military activity, several explanations may initially fit. “Unresolved” therefore means only that the surviving evidence did not support a reliable identification. It does not establish that an extraordinary craft was present.

## Reconstructing direction, duration and movement

The first task is to turn a witness’s memory into a testable sequence. German civilian investigation groups such as the Society for the Study of the UFO Phenomenon, or GEP, use standardised reporting and interview procedures rather than relying on a free-form story alone. Direction is especially important because witnesses often describe motion relative to themselves rather than to the landscape. A light may seem to follow a car because a distant planet remains in roughly the same place through the windscreen. An approaching aircraft can appear stationary for several minutes because it is flying almost directly towards the observer. Once it turns onto another heading, the same light may seem to accelerate sideways.

Investigators therefore ask witnesses to use fixed reference points. “It travelled from above the church tower towards the north end of the forest” is far more useful than “it crossed the whole sky”. A sketch made before the witness sees suggested explanations can also preserve details that might otherwise change during later discussion.

Duration provides another useful check. A meteor normally crosses the visible sky in seconds. A planet or bright star may remain in approximately the same direction for much longer. An aircraft can be visible for several minutes, while a newly launched satellite train may take several minutes to pass. An account describing all these behaviours at once may reflect inaccurate timing, more than one object, or a memory reconstructed after the event.

### Why witness certainty is not enough

Investigators do not have to assume that a sincere witness is unreliable. They separate honesty from observational accuracy. A person can accurately report what an object looked like while being mistaken about its distance, size or speed because the night sky provides few depth cues.

Apparent speed is particularly fragile. Without knowing distance, investigators cannot calculate true speed from visual movement alone. A nearby insect crossing a camera’s field of view, an aeroplane several kilometres away and a satellite hundreds of kilometres above Earth can all appear to move rapidly on a screen. Claims of extreme acceleration require either a known range, observations from separated locations, radar-quality tracking or another independent measurement.

This is why good interview practice avoids leading questions. GEP’s published research principles warn that investigators should gather information neutrally, obtain informed consent and distinguish collected material from later interpretation. They also treat deliberate alteration or invention of facts as research misconduct.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/forschung/grundsaetze-redlicher-wissenschaftlicher-praxis-in-der-erforschung-des-ufo-phaenomens" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">5) Betrug in der wissenschaftlichen Forschung umfasst bewusste Erfindungen oder Verfälschungen von Fakten, Forschungsdaten oder Untersuc...</span></span></span>

## Checking aircraft, satellites and astronomy

Once the sighting has been reconstructed, investigators work through possible matches. The order may vary, but the most productive checks are usually aircraft, satellites and astronomical objects because these account for a large share of modern reports.

### Aircraft and local flight activity

Brandenburg presents a demanding aviation environment. Aircraft approaching or departing Berlin Brandenburg Airport may be visible far beyond the airport boundary, particularly at night. Their landing lights can dominate the view while the red, green and white navigation lights remain too faint to distinguish. Survey flights, helicopters, light aircraft and authorised drones create additional possibilities.

Investigators compare the reported track with available aircraft-position data and known approach or departure patterns. Public flight-tracking records can be useful, but a missing track is not conclusive. Military, police and some private aircraft may be hidden, incomplete or absent from public services. Older data may also no longer be freely accessible.

Published airport notices provide another check. Berlin Brandenburg Airport, for example, announces some night-time calibration flights used to test its instrument landing systems. An unusual aircraft repeatedly following the same route near a runway may therefore be carrying out planned technical work rather than displaying inexplicable behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://corporate.berlin-airport.de/en/company-media/corporate-news/2025-05-12-vermessungsfluege.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: corporate.berlin-airport.de">[Berlin Airport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">corporate.berlin-airport.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This is a drone measurement that is to be carried out every 180 days at BER to supplement flight measurements (ILS).... Reports close to BER are handled differently when they could represent a real drone hazard. German air-traffic control uses established safety procedures after reported drone sightings near airports and may temporarily withhold take-off or landing clearance. DFS advises witnesses near airports or controlled airspace to contact the police or the airport security centre promptly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/de/drohnenflug/drohnen-an-flughaefen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dfs.de.</span></span></span>

An operational response does not itself prove that a drone was confirmed. Controllers must act conservatively because the consequences of ignoring a genuine object could be serious. The later evidential question remains separate: was an object detected, photographed, tracked or independently observed, and could an operator be identified?

### Satellites, rockets and re-entry

Satellites have transformed the appearance of Brandenburg’s night sky. A line of evenly spaced lights moving together strongly suggests a recently deployed satellite group, especially Starlink. A single satellite may brighten or fade as its angle to the Sun changes, while a rocket stage or fuel release can produce an expanding cloud that looks unlike an ordinary point of light.

The [Rathenow]({{ 'rathenow/' | relative_url }}) report publicised in 2021 illustrates why pattern description matters. The observer reported a chain of lights moving across the night sky, a configuration that immediately gives investigators a specific satellite hypothesis to test rather than an undefined “craft”. Brandenburg generated 47 reports to CENAP during 2020, but that total counts initial mysteries, not 47 objects that survived investigation as unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moz.de/lokales/rathenow/ufo-ueber-brandenburg_-unbekannte-flugobjekte-leonard-ist-naechster-kandidat-61196141.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moz.de">[Moz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moz.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on moz.de.</span></span></span>

A proper satellite check uses the witness’s coordinates and observation time to calculate where candidate objects should have appeared. Investigators compare direction, elevation, speed, spacing and brightness. Historical orbital elements are preferable to simply opening a current sky map, because a satellite’s orbit changes and the relevant question is where it was at the time of the sighting.

This method can solve cases that initially appear well corroborated. A published study of reports by pilots reconstructed the observers’ aircraft positions with flight data and modelled a newly launched Starlink group using orbital information. The simulated appearance matched the reported lights, showing that multiple trained witnesses and photographs can still document an unfamiliar presentation of known satellites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08155" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

Sunlight geometry is crucial. Satellites that are invisible for most of an orbit may flare brightly when their surfaces reflect sunlight towards the observer. Research into extreme Starlink flares has shown that these reflections can become bright enough to trigger reports from aviation professionals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13091" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Extreme Flaring of Starlink Satellites</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv Extreme Flaring of Starlink Satellites</span></span></span>

### Planets, stars and meteors

Astronomical checks begin with a sky simulation for the precise location and time. Venus and Jupiter are common sources because they can be exceptionally bright. Sirius can flash in different colours when seen low through turbulent air. CENAP has described a case in which even attending police officers were puzzled by a strongly scintillating light until it was identified as Sirius.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutschland.de/en/topic/knowledge/ufos-over-germany-hansjuergen-koehler-and-cenap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutschland.de">[Deutschland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutschland.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos over germany hansjuergen koehler and cenap</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs over Germany: An expert provides clarity29 Nov 2024 — UFOs over Germany: Read on to learn how Hansjürgen Köhler and the C...</span></span></span>

The test is not merely whether a bright planet was somewhere in the sky. Its calculated direction and elevation must agree with the witness’s line of sight. If the report says the object remained low in the west for 40 minutes and Venus occupied that position, the match is strong. If the witness consistently placed it in the north, Venus is not a satisfactory explanation.

Meteors and fireballs are checked against their short duration, direction of travel, fragmentation and any regional observation records. Independent reports from towns along a common path can be particularly valuable. A bright meteor may look low and nearby to every witness, although it is actually high in the atmosphere and visible across a wide area.

German CENAP statistics show why these tests are performed early. Its 2023 cases included large numbers attributed to Starlink, Venus, Jupiter, fireballs, aircraft, drones, Sirius, balloons, projected lights and camera reflections. The figures describe the reporting population rather than Brandenburg alone, but they identify the recurring candidates most likely to matter when a Brandenburg observation is assessed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.merkur.de/wissen/jupiter-starlink-astronomie-ufo-meldestelle-807-meldungen-himmel-objekte-stern-planet-venus-zr-92756211.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merkur.de">[Merkur]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merkur.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on merkur.de.</span></span></span>

## Weather, imagery and independent witnesses

Weather records help investigators decide not only what the object might have been, but what the witness could realistically have seen. Cloud height, visibility, rain, fog and wind can support or weaken competing explanations.

A light reported as moving within clouds may have been a searchlight projected from the ground. Thin or broken cloud can conceal an aircraft body while allowing its lights to show intermittently. Strong winds can explain balloons drifting in a consistent direction. Ice crystals, haze and atmospheric turbulence can enlarge, distort or colour lights close to the horizon.

The German Weather Service maintains observations of conditions including cloud, visibility, wind and precipitation. The nearest station will not reproduce conditions above every Brandenburg village exactly, but it provides an independent check against statements such as “the sky was completely clear” or “the object moved against the wind”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetterbeobachtung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Photographs and videos require their own reconstruction. Investigators look for the original file, exposure time, camera model, focal length, digital zoom, stabilisation and editing history. They also ask what the photographer saw with the naked eye. A bright disc in a heavily enlarged phone image may be an out-of-focus star, while a pale object that appears only in one frame may be a bird, insect, reflection or compression artefact.

Useful image tests include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* locating stars, buildings or horizon features that establish direction;
* checking whether apparent movement follows camera shake;
* comparing consecutive frames rather than one selected still;
* examining whether the object changes with focus or zoom;
* estimating angular size from known features in the image;
* preserving metadata and the untouched original file.

</div>

An image cannot normally establish extraordinary performance without scale and range. Even a sharp photograph may show only what an object looked like from one direction.

Independent testimony is strongest when witnesses were separated and recorded their accounts before discussing the incident. Several people standing together are valuable, but they share the same viewpoint and can influence one another. Reports from different locations may permit triangulation, in which intersecting lines of sight estimate position or altitude. Poorly timed or vague accounts cannot support this calculation.

Instrumental corroboration can greatly improve a case, although it remains unusual in civilian reports. Scientific proposals for investigating unidentified aerial phenomena emphasise combinations of wide-field cameras, narrow-field imaging, radar, acoustic sensors and environmental measurements. Multiple sensor types help distinguish a physical object from a lens effect, radio interference or a visual misperception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

## Separating unresolved cases from weak reports

The most important final step is classification. Investigators should not force every report into either “explained” or “extraordinary”. There are at least three practically different outcomes.

**Identified reports** have a candidate that fits the important details: time, direction, movement, duration and appearance. A satellite train predicted to cross the correct part of the sky at the correct minute is a stronger identification than a general statement that “satellites were visible that night”.

**Unresolved reports** contain enough information to deserve continued attention, but no tested explanation adequately fits. This category should be reserved for cases with reasonably reliable timing, geometry or corroboration. It records an evidential result, not a claim about extraterrestrial origin.

**Insufficient or weak reports** cannot be tested properly. The date may be uncertain, the witness may not remember the direction, the only image may be a copied screenshot, or the account may have emerged years later. Such a report can remain personally compelling while contributing little to a historical assessment of Brandenburg’s skies.

This distinction prevents a common statistical error. Removing all obvious aircraft, planets and satellites from a database does not make every remaining entry more mysterious. Poor information often survives filtering precisely because it cannot be compared with anything. A high-quality unresolved case and an uncheckable story must not be counted as equivalent.

Later evidence can move a report between categories. A recovered original photograph, a second independent witness or archived flight information may strengthen it. Conversely, a launch record, astronomical calculation or matching aircraft route may weaken the original interpretation. Responsible investigators record that change rather than preserving the first label indefinitely.

Brandenburg’s fragmented reporting system makes this discipline particularly important. Civilian groups, police, airport authorities and local media collect information for different purposes. A police incident may focus on immediate public safety; a newspaper may preserve only the witness’s most striking description; a civilian investigator may concentrate on identification. No single record should be treated as the complete case unless the underlying times, statements and supporting data can be inspected.

The fairest conclusion is therefore often narrower than either believers or sceptics expect. Many Brandenburg reports can be matched to familiar objects once their geometry is reconstructed. Some remain unresolved because a serious comparison found no satisfactory answer. Others remain “unknown” only because the basic information needed for an investigation was never collected.
