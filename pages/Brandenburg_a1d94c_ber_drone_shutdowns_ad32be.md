## Introduction

Berlin [Brandenburg]({{ 'why-brandenburgs-ufo-record-remains-so/' | relative_url }}) Airport’s drone shutdowns show why an object does not need to be technologically extraordinary to become an aviation emergency. On 31 October 2025, a reported drone stopped take-offs and landings for nearly two hours, diverted incoming aircraft and disrupted the airport’s night schedule. On 11 March 2026, a reported luminous object near a military helicopter hangar prompted another, shorter suspension, even though police could not confirm that any aircraft or drone had actually been present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/233028211d399bbbbbca72a08a767ef3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AP News A drone sighting temporarily suspends air travel at the Berlin airport</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">and 9:58 p.m. local time. Within Brandenburg’s wider history of unusual aerial reports, these incidents are valuable precisely because they are not strong evidence of exotic craft. They demonstrate the practical meaning of “unidentified”: controllers and police may have only a brief visual report, no recovered object and no identified operator, yet must still protect passenger aircraft before attempting a final explanation. At an airport, uncertainty itself can be operationally serious.

## The October 2025 suspension

The more disruptive incident began on the evening of Friday, 31 October 2025. Berlin Brandenburg Airport, commonly known by its code BER, stopped flight operations from 8.08 pm until 9.58 pm after reports of a drone close to the airport. A member of the public initially reported the object, and contemporary accounts said that a police patrol also observed what appeared to be a drone. Police deployed ground units and a helicopter but did not locate the aircraft or its operator.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/233028211d399bbbbbca72a08a767ef3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AP News A drone sighting temporarily suspends air travel at the Berlin airport</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">and 9:58 p.m. local time. The sighting was confirmed by local authorities after a witness reported seeing the drone; however, no drone wa...</span></span></span>

The operational consequences were clear even though the object itself remained poorly documented. Eleven arriving aircraft were reportedly diverted: four to Dresden, four to Leipzig and three to Hamburg. The affected services included flights arriving from Stockholm, Antalya and Helsinki, while other departures and connections were delayed or cancelled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.euronews.com/2025/11/01/drone-interrupts-flight-operations-at-berlin-airport-for-two-hours" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: euronews.com">[euronews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">euronews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Drone interrupts flight operations at Berlin Airport for two hours</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">All flight operations at BER were suspended between 8 pm until shortly before 10 pm. · &#x27;Wake-up call in terms of...</span></span></span>

The shutdown also created a secondary problem. BER normally operates under restrictions intended to limit night-time aircraft noise, but the interruption left aircraft and passengers displaced late in the evening. Authorities therefore allowed exceptional operations after the usual limits, with departures permitted until 1 am and arrivals until 4 am so that the airport could clear the backlog.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/article69053e32a6bc3e9242fc8874" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTDrohnen-Sichtung</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oktober 2025 wurde der Berliner Flughafen BER für etwa zwei Stunden gesperrt, nachdem eine Drohne in der Nähe des Flughafens gesichtet wo...</span></span></span>

This chain of events is more firmly established than the identity of the object. There is strong evidence that witnesses made a report, police treated it seriously and air traffic was suspended. There is much weaker public evidence about the reported drone’s size, model, flight path, duration or control signal. No device was recovered, no pilot was publicly identified and no clear photograph or sensor record emerged in the reporting reviewed here. The incident therefore remains an unresolved drone report, not a confirmed account of a particular aircraft or operator.

The distinction is important for UFO history. In everyday language, the object was literally unidentified after the search ended. That does not turn it into evidence of an exotic vehicle. The available facts are compatible with an unauthorised consumer or commercial drone, but they may also reflect the difficulty of judging a small illuminated object at night. The October case is operationally well documented but visually and technically incomplete.

## The March 2026 luminous-object report

A second closure occurred on Wednesday, 11 March 2026. An airport employee reported a suspicious luminous object near a helicopter hangar used by the German armed forces. BER halted take-offs and landings on both runways for roughly half an hour, with reports placing the interruption from about 6.40 pm until 7.18 pm. Passenger and baggage handling continued while the movement area was closed, and operations resumed after no further sightings were received.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tagesspiegel.de/berlin/leuchtendes-flugobjekt-gesichtet-betrieb-am-flughafen-ber-fur-30-minuten-eingestellt-15345496.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tagesspiegel.de">[tagesspiegel.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tagesspiegel.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">„Leuchtendes Flugobjekt“ gesichtet: Betrieb am Flughafen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">„Leuchtendes Flugobjekt“ gesichtet: Betrieb am Flughafen</span></span></span>

The language used in early reports deserves attention. The object was described as a “luminous flying object” or an unidentified flying object, wording that can sound more mysterious in isolation than it was in context. The phrase described an observation that had not yet been identified; it did not amount to an official claim that an extraordinary craft had been detected. Airport representatives and news agencies treated a drone as the relevant safety possibility because of the location and the danger that any small aircraft could pose near active runways.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tagesspiegel.de/berlin/leuchtendes-flugobjekt-gesichtet-betrieb-am-flughafen-ber-fur-30-minuten-eingestellt-15345496.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tagesspiegel.de">[Tagesspiegel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tagesspiegel.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">„Leuchtendes Flugobjekt“ gesichtet: Betrieb am Flughafen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">„Leuchtendes Flugobjekt“ gesichtet: Betrieb am Flughafen</span></span></span>

Later information weakened the idea that a drone had been positively established. The Federal Police said that the report could not be confirmed: officers found neither a drone nor another corresponding flying object. The incident therefore has a thinner evidential basis than the October shutdown, when reporting indicated that both a witness and a patrol had seen the suspected drone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutschlandfunk.de/vermeintliches-flugobjekt-gesichtet-hauptstadtflughafen-ber-kurzzeitig-geschlossen-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutschlandfunk.de">[Deutschlandfunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutschlandfunk.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Airport Berlin-Brandenburg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wie die Bundespolizei mitteilte...Read more...</span></span></span>

The location nevertheless raised the stakes. A light near a military helicopter facility beside a major civilian airport can suggest several different risks at once, including accidental intrusion, surveillance, deliberate disruption or simple misidentification. None of those possibilities was proved publicly in the March case. The proximity of the hangar explains the urgency of the response, but it should not be treated as evidence of hostile intent.

For Brandenburg’s UFO record, the March event is a useful example of how official action and evidential certainty can move in opposite directions. The airport’s decision was significant and verifiable; the underlying object remained unverified. A closure proves that a report crossed a safety threshold, not that investigators later confirmed the witness’s interpretation.

## Why precaution comes before identification

Air traffic controllers cannot wait for a complete investigation while an unknown light may be crossing an approach or departure path. Germany’s air navigation service provider, DFS, states that defined procedures apply when drones are reported near airports. Those procedures can require controllers to stop issuing take-off and landing clearances, leading to delays, diversions and cancellations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/en/drone-flight/drones-at-airports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dfs.de.</span></span></span>

The reason is not that every small drone would inevitably bring down an airliner. The problem is that controllers may not know its position, altitude, direction or operator. A drone can move unpredictably, may not carry equipment visible to conventional air-traffic surveillance and may be difficult for a flight crew to see in time. Until the airspace is assessed as clear, suspending movements separates passenger aircraft from the suspected hazard.

A shutdown is therefore a risk-control measure rather than an identification verdict. The basic sequence is:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A witness, employee, pilot or sensor reports a possible object.
2. Controllers and airport security assess whether it may affect an active runway or flight path.
3. Take-offs and landings may be stopped while police search the area.
4. Operations resume once the object is located, leaves the relevant airspace or is no longer being reported.

</div>

This procedure inevitably produces some closures in which no drone is found. Small objects can disappear before police arrive, and darkness, distance or weather can make visual confirmation difficult. Birds, conventional aircraft, helicopters, celestial objects and lights with no clear distance reference can also be mistaken for drones. Research into drone-detection systems recognises this identification problem: effective systems may need to distinguish drones from aircraft, birds and other targets by combining several kinds of sensor rather than relying on one observation alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01888" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv A dataset for multi-sensor drone detection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv A dataset for multi-sensor drone detection</span></span></span>

European aviation guidance treats drone incursions as a recurring airport-management problem rather than a rare curiosity. An EASA review noted hundreds of reported disturbances in German airspace from 2015 onwards and described earlier runway suspensions at Frankfurt following reported drones. It emphasised coordinated incident management because decisions must often be made before the object’s nature or intent is known.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/drone_incident_management_at_aerodromes_part1_website_suitable.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: easa.europa.eu">[EASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">easa.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EASADRONE INCIDENT MANAGEMENT AT AERODROMES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EASADRONE INCIDENT MANAGEMENT AT AERODROMES</span></span></span>

## A wider problem, not evidence of an exotic wave

The BER events occurred during a genuine rise in reported drone disruption. DFS recorded 225 drone-related disturbances in German airspace during 2025, compared with 161 in 2024. It also cautioned that part of the increase may reflect improved detection, as Federal Police units began using drone-detection systems in 2025. More reports do not necessarily mean that the same proportionate increase occurred in unauthorised flights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/en/media/press/2026/05-01-2026-air-traffic-in-germany-2025-more-flights-good-punctuality-level/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">05 01 2026 air traffic in germany 2025 more flights good punctuality level</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">05 01 2026 air traffic in germany 2025 more flights good punctuality level</span></span></span>

This wider setting helps explain why BER reacted so quickly. Other European airports had experienced disruptive reports in 2025, including two closures at Munich within 24 hours. The pattern prompted concern about airport resilience, deliberate interference and the ability of police to detect or stop small aircraft. However, the origins of individual incidents were often unknown, and public speculation about organised or state-sponsored activity generally ran ahead of the evidence available for specific sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/4c1fdfdb69372a58af9dee4ba12c028d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Authorities, including federal and local police, are investigating the origin of the drones, but no suspects have been identified. Uncert...</span></span></span>

German rules tightly restrict drone operations around airports. Flights within airport control zones require appropriate air-traffic-control clearance, and DFS instructs members of the public to report sightings near airports immediately to police or airport security. Unauthorised flights that endanger aviation may be investigated as dangerous interference with air traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/en/drone-flight/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dfs.de.</span></span></span>

The difficulty lies in enforcement. Detecting a possible drone is not the same as tracking it reliably, identifying its pilot or disabling it without creating another hazard. Radio-frequency detection may fail if a drone is autonomous or uses an unexpected link. Radar can struggle with small targets and clutter. Cameras depend on visibility, while attempts to jam or physically intercept an object can affect legitimate communications or cause debris to fall. That is why improved detection, police powers and coordination have featured prominently in debate following airport incidents.

## What the BER cases establish

The two shutdowns support several conclusions, but not an extraordinary interpretation.

**The closures were real and consequential.** Aircraft were held, diverted or delayed because authorities considered the reports potentially dangerous. The October event imposed the larger operational cost, while the March event was resolved more quickly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.euronews.com/2025/11/01/drone-interrupts-flight-operations-at-berlin-airport-for-two-hours" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: euronews.com">[euronews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">euronews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Drone interrupts flight operations at Berlin Airport for two hours</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">All flight operations at BER were suspended between 8 pm until shortly before 10 pm. · &#x27;Wake-up call in terms of...</span></span></span>

**The October report was stronger than the March report, but neither produced a publicly identified drone.** October involved multiple reported observations and a police search. In March, police explicitly said that the luminous object could not be confirmed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/article69057a77df9fa029228efb96" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTDrohne legt Hauptstadtflughafen BER lahm Am Abend des 31</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oktober 2025 wurde der Flugbetrieb am Berliner Hauptstadtflughafen BER wegen einer Drohnensichtung für knapp zwei Stunden von 20:08 bis 2...</span></span></span>

**Official caution is not official confirmation.** Closing a runway means that the risk could not safely be dismissed at that moment. It does not certify the witness’s description or settle what the object was.

**“Unidentified” remains the correct term only in a limited sense.** The reports were not fully explained in the public record. That evidential gap leaves room for ordinary possibilities, especially drones and mistaken observations, rather than providing positive evidence for an unknown advanced technology.

For Brandenburg’s aerial-phenomena history, BER supplies a modern counterpart to older reports of mysterious lights. The setting has changed from fields and village skylines to controlled airspace, police helicopters and airport procedures, but the central problem is familiar: a brief visual impression must be interpreted with incomplete information. What makes the BER incidents important is not proof of something extraordinary overhead. It is the fact that modern aviation must act decisively before it knows whether an unidentified light is a drone, a mistake or nothing that can be found at all.

    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
