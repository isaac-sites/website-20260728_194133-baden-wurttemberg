## Introduction

The September 2025 drone wave in [Schleswig-Holstein]({{ 'what-is-really-seen-over-schleswig/' | relative_url }}) was confusing because several different kinds of event were reported at almost the same time. During the night of 25–26 September, witnesses and police recorded drone-like objects over Kiel, Dithmarschen and Rendsburg-Eckernförde, including near military, medical, government, energy and transport infrastructure. Early reporting raised the possibility of coordinated reconnaissance. Later official assessment was more cautious: many reports were identified as aircraft, helicopters or lawful drones; some could not be verified at all; and investigators still believed that at least some illegal drone flights probably occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Schleswig-Holstein_76916e_drone_wave_2025_b164f4-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 2025 Drone Wave" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That mixed result is what makes the episode important in Schleswig-Holstein’s UFO history. It was not one cleanly documented incursion, but a reporting wave in which credible security concerns, incomplete observations and ordinary air traffic became entangled. The central mystery is therefore not whether every object was extraterrestrial or even whether every object was a drone. It is which sightings belonged to a real unlawful operation, and which were added by heightened vigilance.

## Sites named during the reporting wave

The first official statements were deliberately broad. On 26 September, Schleswig-Holstein’s Interior Ministry confirmed that several drone sightings from the previous night were under investigation for possible espionage or sabotage. Officials did not initially state how many objects had been reported or identify the locations. The timing increased concern because Denmark had experienced repeated reports near airports and military sites during the same week, including incidents that disrupted air traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/drohnensichtungen-jetzt-auch-in-sh-landespolizei-verstaerkt-abwehr%2Cdrohnen-180.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wieder Drohnen in SH gesichtet: Landespolizei verstärkt Abwehr &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wieder Drohnen in SH gesichtet: Landespolizei verstärkt Abwehr &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

According to these reports, two small drones were seen shortly after 9 p.m. above the naval shipyard. A formation described as including a larger “mother drone” and smaller objects was then reported near the university hospital. Shortly after 10 p.m., a similar formation was said to have appeared near the power station and the Kiel Canal. Later sightings included a large, apparently stationary object and several smaller lights over the Kiel Fjord.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zdfheute.de/politik/drohnen-schleswig-holstein-ausspaehen-infrastruktur-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zdfheute.de">[ZDFheute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zdfheute.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Schleswig-Holstein: Drohnen sollen Infrastruktur ausgespäht haben</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Schleswig-Holstein: Drohnen sollen Infrastruktur ausgespäht haben...</span></span></span>

The distribution of the named sites gave the story its apparent coherence. A submarine yard, parliament, refinery, power station, hospital and strategic waterway are not random landmarks. Together they looked like a list of facilities that a hostile intelligence service might wish to photograph, map or test for security weaknesses.

Reports that some objects flew in parallel tracks strengthened that interpretation. Security sources suggested that the pattern might have been consistent with systematic surveying. The reported passage along sections of the Kiel Canal also attracted attention because the canal is a major commercial route and a strategically significant connection between the North Sea and Baltic Sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/spiegel-drohnen-ueber-schleswig-holstein-sollen-kritische-infrastruktur-ausgespaeht-haben%2Cdrohnen-182.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Drohnen über SH und MV sollen Infrastruktur ausgespäht haben &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Drohnen über SH und MV sollen Infrastruktur ausgespäht haben &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

Yet geographical proximity alone does not prove that every report formed part of one operation. Kiel’s waterfront concentrates naval facilities, hospitals, government buildings, industrial premises, shipping, helicopters and conventional aviation within a relatively small visible area. Once observers had been alerted to possible hostile drones, lights seen from different locations could easily be joined into a single narrative even when their origins differed.

## Resolved, unresolved and unverified sightings

The clearest official account came from Interior Minister Sabine Sütterlin-Waack’s report to the State Parliament’s Interior and Legal Affairs Committee on 1 October. She confirmed that objects of different types and sizes had been reported over Kiel, Dithmarschen and Rendsburg-Eckernförde. The State Criminal Police Office was assessing the observations, but the evidence had already divided into three distinct categories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

**Resolved reports** were sightings for which investigators could exclude an illegal, security-sensitive drone flight. The minister specifically named conventional aircraft, helicopters and lawful drones. This was a substantial qualification to the earliest headlines, which had often treated the night’s reports collectively as a mysterious drone incursion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

**Unverified reports** included some claims involving military infrastructure. “Unverified” does not mean disproved. It means that investigators could not establish from the available observations, recordings or sensor data that the reported object had actually been present as described. A witness report may be sincere yet remain unverifiable because it lacks a photograph, radar return, precise direction, reliable timing or independent corroboration.

**Probable illegal flights** formed the third category. Despite resolving or failing to verify many reports, state authorities said they had to assume provisionally that illegal drones had flown over Schleswig-Holstein. The public evidence did not establish their number, launch point, model, operator or purpose.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

This distinction is essential. The official position was not that the entire wave had been explained, nor that a hostile swarm had been conclusively documented. It was that a mixed body of reports probably contained a smaller core of genuine unlawful drone activity.

The Flensburg public prosecutor’s office opened an investigation against unknown persons. The suspected offence concerned security-threatening image collection, prompted in part by reports that multiple drones had flown together over protected infrastructure. Opening an investigation indicates that the information met the threshold for further inquiry; it is not proof that deliberate espionage occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/spiegel-drohnen-ueber-schleswig-holstein-sollen-kritische-infrastruktur-ausgespaeht-haben%2Cdrohnen-182.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Drohnen über SH und MV sollen Infrastruktur ausgespäht haben &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Drohnen über SH und MV sollen Infrastruktur ausgespäht haben &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

Descriptions such as “mother drone” should be treated with particular care. The term suggests a larger aircraft controlling or transporting smaller drones, but the publicly available reporting did not provide imagery, recovered equipment or technical sensor evidence proving such a system. It may have been an observer’s description of one large light accompanied by smaller ones rather than a confirmed identification of a specific platform.

The same caution applies to the claim that objects were “measuring” facilities. Parallel flight paths can be used for aerial mapping, but they can also be inferred incorrectly from intermittent lights, separate aircraft or observations made from different positions. No publicly released flight tracks, high-resolution recordings or completed forensic assessment demonstrated that a survey had taken place.

## How heightened concern multiplied reports

The wave occurred in an unusually charged security atmosphere. Danish authorities had reported unidentified drones near airports and military facilities, while European governments were publicly debating espionage, sabotage and so-called hybrid operations: pressure conducted below the threshold of open warfare through intimidation, interference, cyberattacks or covert surveillance. Schleswig-Holstein’s position directly south of Denmark made the Danish incidents feel immediate rather than remote.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/drohnensichtungen-jetzt-auch-in-sh-landespolizei-verstaerkt-abwehr%2Cdrohnen-180.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wieder Drohnen in SH gesichtet: Landespolizei verstärkt Abwehr &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wieder Drohnen in SH gesichtet: Landespolizei verstärkt Abwehr &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

There were also earlier Schleswig-Holstein cases in recent memory. Drone reports near industrial infrastructure in Brunsbüttel during August 2024 had led to a criminal investigation. Authorities established that some flights had occurred but could not identify an operator, and the case was closed in May 2025. In January 2025, drones had reportedly been observed near a Patriot air-defence site at Schwesing. Shortly before the September wave, police searched the cargo ship *Scanlark* at the Kiel Canal over suspicions that a drone had been launched from the vessel to photograph a naval ship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/drohnensichtungen-jetzt-auch-in-sh-landespolizei-verstaerkt-abwehr%2Cdrohnen-180.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wieder Drohnen in SH gesichtet: Landespolizei verstärkt Abwehr &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wieder Drohnen in SH gesichtet: Landespolizei verstärkt Abwehr &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

These earlier incidents did not prove that the 25–26 September reports had the same source. They did, however, shape how witnesses, journalists and officials interpreted ambiguous lights. A person who had recently heard about ship-launched reconnaissance drones was more likely to interpret an unfamiliar object over the fjord as another hostile platform.

Heightened attention can increase both detection and error. More police officers, security personnel and members of the public watching the sky will notice genuine drones that might otherwise pass unseen. The same observers will also report more aircraft, stars, helicopters and lawful drones. The result is not necessarily panic or fabrication. It is a familiar problem in sighting waves: the reporting threshold falls, so stronger and weaker cases arrive together.

Kiel was particularly vulnerable to this effect. Rescue and police helicopters may operate near the university hospital. Aircraft approaching or receding at night can appear stationary when viewed head-on. Navigation lights can disappear as an aircraft changes direction. Small drones may be legal in one area but appear to be above a restricted site because the observer cannot judge distance accurately. Lights seen across the fjord lack reliable visual reference points, making height, speed and separation difficult to estimate.

Media sequencing added another layer. The first announcement confirmed suspicious sightings but withheld most locations and details. Several days later, reports named a shipyard, hospital, parliament, power station, refinery and canal and described possible coordinated surveying. Readers therefore encountered the story in two stages: first an official warning, then a more dramatic reconstruction. The later details were newsworthy, but some were drawn from internal notes and anonymous security sources rather than publicly released technical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/drohnensichtungen-jetzt-auch-in-sh-landespolizei-verstaerkt-abwehr%2Cdrohnen-180.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wieder Drohnen in SH gesichtet: Landespolizei verstärkt Abwehr &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wieder Drohnen in SH gesichtet: Landespolizei verstärkt Abwehr &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

## Why the “swarm” remained difficult to prove

A coordinated drone swarm should leave several kinds of evidence. Multiple sensors might record matching tracks. Photographs from separate locations could establish object size and direction. Radio-frequency detection might identify control links. Radar could help distinguish drones from aircraft, while recovered equipment or an identified operator could connect sightings at different sites.

Little of that material was released publicly in relation to the September wave. The available record consists mainly of official acknowledgements, witness reports, accounts of an internal note and the later statement that many observations had mundane explanations. This does not show that authorities possessed no stronger classified or operational information. It does mean that the public case for one large, coordinated swarm remained incomplete.

The number of reported objects was also unstable. Accounts referred variously to “several drones”, two small objects, a larger object with smaller companions and formations at multiple sites. Without continuous tracking, it is impossible to tell whether the same craft moved between locations, whether several unrelated drones were operating, or whether conventional aircraft were counted more than once.

Even the most alarming named sites require separate assessment. TKMS confirmed that an incident had occurred over its Kiel premises, which gives that part of the story more weight than an anonymous visual report alone. The wider claims concerning the hospital, refinery, parliament, power station and canal were publicly discussed by authorities and reputable news organisations, but not every sighting was independently confirmed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/spiegel-drohnen-ueber-schleswig-holstein-sollen-kritische-infrastruktur-ausgespaeht-haben%2Cdrohnen-182.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Drohnen über SH und MV sollen Infrastruktur ausgespäht haben &#124; ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Drohnen über SH und MV sollen Infrastruktur ausgespäht haben &#124; ndr.de</span></span></span>

Nor did the official threat assessment match the strongest public speculation. The Interior Ministry said that the information available at the time did not justify declaring a qualitatively increased, concrete threat to Schleswig-Holstein. That judgement sat alongside the belief that some illegal flights had probably occurred. Both statements can be true: unlawful surveillance may be serious enough to investigate without evidence of an imminent attack or a single large-scale hostile operation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

Claims of Russian involvement were therefore hypotheses, not established findings. Russia was widely suspected in political and media discussion because of the European security situation and earlier concerns about reconnaissance near military infrastructure. No public evidence identified a Russian operator, command system, launch vessel or intelligence unit behind the Schleswig-Holstein sightings.

## Investigation and later assessment

The immediate response involved the State Criminal Police Office, the Flensburg public prosecutor, federal police and criminal agencies, the German armed forces and contacts with Danish authorities. Schleswig-Holstein had already purchased counter-drone equipment in 2024 and allocated further money and staff for detection and defence during 2025. The incident intensified calls for clearer responsibilities and faster coordination between state police, federal authorities and the military.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

The investigation produced an early correction but no comparable public breakthrough. By 1 October, officials had reduced the apparent scale of the wave by excluding many reports as aircraft, helicopters or lawful drones. Some supposed sightings over military locations remained unverified. At the same time, the criminal investigation preserved the possibility that a smaller number of coordinated illegal flights had occurred.

Later public reporting did not disclose an identified pilot, recovered drone or confirmed foreign sponsor. It also did not overturn the minister’s three-part assessment. The strongest defensible conclusion remained that the original wave combined explained sightings, unsupported reports and a residue of probable illegal drone activity.

That pattern resembles the earlier Brunsbüttel investigation. In that case, authorities eventually accepted that some drone flights were real but could not trace the operators. The comparison does not establish a common perpetrator. It shows why such cases often remain unresolved: confirming that a small aircraft crossed restricted airspace is much easier than identifying who controlled it, particularly near coasts, ports and shipping routes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ndr.de/nachrichten/schleswig-holstein/Spionage-Drohnen-ueber-Brunsbuettel-Ermittlungen-eingestellt%2Cregionflensburgnews3032.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ndr.de">[ndr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ndr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ndr.de.</span></span></span>

## The wave’s place in Schleswig-Holstein’s UFO history

The September 2025 episode belongs in the state’s UFO record because “unidentified” describes an evidential condition, not an exotic origin. Witnesses reported objects they could not identify. Investigators then separated ordinary traffic from doubtful claims and apparently unlawful flights whose operators remained unknown.

Unlike older UFO stories built around a single witness or photograph, this wave involved security agencies, protected infrastructure and a criminal investigation. That gives it greater institutional importance. It does not automatically make every sighting more reliable. Official attention was driven partly by the sensitivity of the locations and the possible consequences of overlooking a real incursion.

The event also demonstrates why modern drone waves can be harder to interpret than traditional UFO sightings. Consumer and professional drones are common, military drone technology is rapidly developing, and authorities have valid reasons to take incursions seriously. At the same time, distant aircraft and helicopters remain difficult to identify at night, while public warnings encourage more people to look upwards and report uncertain observations.

The most balanced classification is therefore **partly resolved, partly unverified and partly unresolved**. There is good evidence that numerous reports were made and that authorities treated some of them as potentially serious. There is official confirmation that many were subsequently explained. There is also an official judgement that some illegal drone flights probably occurred. What remains missing is public proof that all the named sites were surveyed by one coordinated swarm, or that a foreign state directed the operation.

The confusion was not a side issue; it was the defining feature of the wave. Schleswig-Holstein may have experienced a genuine security breach during the night of 25–26 September 2025, but the larger story grew from the merging of that possibility with legal aviation, mistaken identifications and reports that could never be verified.
