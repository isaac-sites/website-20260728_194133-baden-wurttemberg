## Introduction

Before the famous [Greifswald lights]({{ 'greifswald-case/' | relative_url }}) appeared on 24 August 1990, north-eastern Germany had already experienced a smaller run of unusual-light reports. Between mid-June and mid-July, witnesses around Güstrow, Rostock, Laage and the Salzhaff described bright orange or yellow balls, intense illumination and silent objects moving through the night sky. Local newspapers treated the accounts as a developing UFO wave, encouraging further witnesses to come forward.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Mecklenburg-Vorpomme_d975e6_gustrow_rostock_ufo_593232-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1990 Flap" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

These earlier cases matter because they helped prepare the public and press to interpret the later Greifswald formations as part of a regional mystery. They are not, however, equally strong evidence. Unlike the Greifswald event, the Güstrow and Rostock reports produced no known photographs, video, radar data or surviving official investigation. Most are available only through newspaper testimony later reproduced by a sceptical UFO investigator. The result is a genuine reporting cluster, but not a securely documented single phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

## June and July sighting reports

The first widely publicised episode reportedly occurred during the night of 16–17 June 1990. According to a 14 July article in the *Norddeutsche Neueste Nachrichten*, eight pupils aged about 13 or 14 were attending a garden party in the Güstrow district when an intense light drew their attention shortly after midnight. They described a roughly spherical object surrounded by a strong halo, apparently hovering at a height of only about ten metres before departing towards the north-east.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

That account sounds dramatic, particularly because it placed the object unusually close to the witnesses. Yet nearly every important measurement was an unaided estimate made at night: height, size, distance and direction. Without a visible structure, a known background reference or physical traces at the supposed position, a bright light farther away could easily have appeared much lower and larger than it really was. The surviving narrative also does not say whether all eight pupils independently described the same details before discussing the experience together.

A second report connected the same night with Breesen, near Laage in the Güstrow district. A Rostock resident and his wife said they had noticed the unusual appearance after watching a World Cup football match. Their account included the striking claim that they were enveloped by a large circle of light. The published material does not establish whether they saw the same object as the pupils, how far apart the groups were or whether their times and directions matched closely enough to support a common source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

The geographical label “Güstrow flap” therefore gives the reports more unity than the surviving evidence necessarily supports. What is documented is a cluster of recollections associated with one night and one broad district. What is missing is the kind of structured comparison that could turn separate stories into a reconstructed flight path: exact observation times, bearings, angular elevations, weather conditions and statements recorded independently before press coverage.

## The Güstrow, Rostock and Salzhaff accounts

Local interest widened after the Güstrow story appeared in print. On 21 July, the *Ostsee-Zeitung* published further testimony under a headline asking whether an unknown flying object had appeared over the Salzhaff and noting an orange-coloured light over Rostock. The article explicitly presented the new accounts as readers breaking their silence after earlier UFO coverage. That sequence is important: the newspaper was not simply recording a simultaneous mass sighting but collecting retrospective reports after the subject had become news.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

One of the responses came from the Wietzke family in Rostock-Lichtenhagen. They recalled seeing a glaring orange circle shortly after 1 a.m. on 17 June, viewed from their flat towards the district of Groß Klein. It reportedly moved with a slight rocking motion, continued eastwards and eventually disappeared. The date and general time overlap with the Güstrow-area accounts, making this the most useful potential link in the June cluster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

Even so, the connection remains circumstantial. Rostock and the Güstrow district cover a large area, and descriptions such as “orange circle” or “bright ball” are not distinctive enough by themselves to prove that distant observers were watching one object. The apparent rocking could indicate a suspended or wind-driven light, but it could also reflect atmospheric turbulence, observer movement or difficulty judging a point source against a dark, featureless sky.

A separate report came from Marianne Teichmann, who was staying at a weekend property in Rerik beside the Salzhaff. She said that, after waking sometime after midnight, she saw a large, bright yellow ball pass rapidly over the house. It made no sound and was bright enough to illuminate the room. She specifically rejected the aurora as an explanation, noting that she had seen that natural phenomenon before.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

Teichmann’s description differs significantly from the slower Rostock light. A rapidly moving luminous object that briefly lights its surroundings could suggest a meteor or fireball, although the wording “over the house” may simply express the witness’s visual impression rather than a measurable low-altitude passage. No precise date, duration, direction or astronomical correlation survives in the accessible account, so a meteor explanation cannot now be confirmed.

The reports consequently fall into at least two descriptive groups:

* **Slow or apparently hovering lights:** the garden-party object, the Rostock orange circle and possibly the Breesen appearance.
* **A fast luminous passage:** the large yellow ball reported at Rerik.

Treating both groups as one craft or one type of event requires assumptions unsupported by the available evidence.

## Why the earlier cases remain weak

The central weakness is the documentary chain. The most detailed surviving online account was assembled by Werner Walter of the Central Research Network for Extraordinary Sky Phenomena, or CENAP, and reproduces portions of contemporary regional newspaper stories. It is valuable because it preserves names, dates and quotations, but it is not a complete case file. The original witness statements, journalists’ notes, photographs of the locations and any police enquiries are not presented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

There is also no known instrumental evidence. None of the June or July witnesses is reported to have produced a photograph, film, radar return, physical trace or corroborating aviation record. That sharply separates the flap from the Greifswald incident, for which several recordings from different coastal positions later became available. The Greifswald films at least demonstrate that an external luminous event occurred; the earlier reports establish only that people sincerely described unusual experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[GWUP e. Timing is another problem. Some testimony was reported nearly a month after the night of 16–17 June. Human memory does not become worthless over that interval, but details such as exact time, apparent movement and perceived distance can shift, especially after witnesses encounter similar stories in the press. The *Ostsee-Zeitung* article’s language about readers finally speaking out shows how publicity was actively building the cluster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

Nor is there evidence that investigators conducted independent interviews before witnesses could compare accounts. Group testimony can strengthen confidence that something was noticed, but it does not automatically provide eight independent confirmations of an object’s height, form or behaviour. At a social gathering, observers influence one another’s attention and language almost immediately.

Finally, the reports do not describe a consistently unusual performance. No object was recorded accelerating abruptly, reversing direction, interacting with an aircraft or leaving a physical effect that resisted ordinary explanation. The dominant features—orange or yellow colour, silence, slow drift, apparent hovering and occasional rapid passage—also occur in reports caused by small illuminated balloons, distant aircraft lights, pyrotechnics and bright meteors.

## Plausible explanations without a firm identification

Walter later judged that the slow-moving descriptions resembled miniature hot-air balloons. A small paper balloon carrying a flame can appear as a vivid orange or yellow sphere, drift silently, rock in air currents and disappear when its flame goes out or the object passes into cloud. That is a plausible class of explanation for the Rostock report and perhaps for parts of the Güstrow testimony, but no balloon was recovered and no launch was identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

The apparent ten-metre height reported by the pupils does not rule out a more distant light. At night, observers cannot reliably estimate the distance of an isolated luminous object unless its true size or surroundings are known. A small balloon hundreds of metres away and a much larger object close by may produce a similar visual angle.

The Rerik sighting is less compatible with a slowly drifting balloon because the witness described a rapid overhead passage and powerful brief illumination. A bright meteor is a reasonable possibility, but the accessible account lacks the exact information needed for verification against astronomical records. It should therefore be classified as insufficiently documented rather than retrospectively “solved”.

Military activity is another possibility in a coastal region with air and naval facilities, particularly during the final months of the German Democratic Republic. The later Greifswald formations were eventually linked by sceptical investigators to parachute-supported military lights used in a Baltic training area. That explanation fits the August event’s repeated formations and slow descent, but it should not simply be projected backwards onto every orange light reported around Güstrow and Rostock. The June and July cases lack the formation pattern, multiple recordings and testimony about offshore launches that support the flare interpretation at Greifswald.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[GWUP e. ## How the flap shaped the Greifswald story

The earlier wave’s historical importance lies less in the individual sightings than in the atmosphere it created. By late July, local readers had already encountered headlines linking unexplained lights with UFOs. When groups of luminous objects appeared over the Baltic coast on 24 August and were filmed from several locations, the public did not meet the event as an isolated puzzle. It arrived as the apparent climax of a summer-long regional pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[Alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

That continuity was psychologically persuasive but evidentially uncertain. The earlier accounts involved different dates, locations, speeds and colours, whereas Greifswald featured repeated clusters of several lights descending over the maritime horizon. Similar vocabulary—balls, circles, silent lights—may have encouraged the incidents to be combined even though such language is common in unrelated night-sky reports.

The political transition of 1990 also complicated investigation. Communications and official responsibilities were unsettled, and later Greifswald investigators described serious difficulty contacting local witnesses or obtaining useful answers from the Rostock police. Those conditions may partly explain why the Güstrow and Rostock reports never developed into properly documented files. They also allowed uncertainty to survive while newspaper stories circulated more freely than technical findings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[GWUP e. ## A reporting wave, not a proven aerial mystery

The Güstrow and Rostock flap is best understood as a short-lived wave of unusual-light reports amplified by regional newspaper coverage. Several named witnesses probably did see striking lights, and the overlapping reports from the night of 16–17 June justify treating the episode as part of [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern]({{ 'mecklenburg-vorpommern/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history. There is no sound basis, however, for claiming that all the witnesses observed one structured craft or a single unexplained operation.

The evidence supports a cautious distinction. The flap itself—the growth of reports and public interest—is well attested through contemporary press material. The objects behind it are not. Slow orange lights may have been illuminated balloons or other drifting sources; the fast Salzhaff light may have been a meteor; and some reports remain too incomplete for meaningful identification.

Later research did not substantially strengthen these early cases. Instead, the better-documented Greifswald event overshadowed them and demonstrated why recordings, multiple observation points and military context matter. The Güstrow and Rostock accounts remain valuable chiefly as a lesson in how separate, weakly documented sightings can accumulate into a regional UFO narrative before investigators have established whether the reports share a common cause.
