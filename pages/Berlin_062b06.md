## Introduction

The best-documented Berlin incident occurred near Tempelhof in August 1952, when several air-base personnel reported luminous objects south of the field. It received a formal intelligence investigation but no decisive explanation. Other Berlin cases were identified as astronomical events or left unresolved simply because the files contained too little information. That distinction—between unexplained and inadequately documented—is central to understanding the city’s UFO record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/05/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1952-10-8773352-Berlin-Germany.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

## Tempelhof’s 1952 sightings

Berlin’s most substantial surviving UFO file concerns observations made near Tempelhof Air Base on the night of 11 August 1952. Tempelhof was then not merely a civil airport but a strategically important Western airfield inside divided Berlin. Any unidentified object near it could therefore be treated as an [air-security]({{ 'air-security/' | relative_url }}) matter rather than a curiosity.

According to an Air Intelligence Information Report, personnel in Tempelhof’s control tower received a call from the air-base fire department shortly before 11pm. Three luminous objects were then seen directly south of the field. Witnesses described them as cloud-like in texture, elliptical in shape and bright enough to stand out against the dark sky. They appeared to hover for roughly 30 seconds. One object, reportedly brighter and apparently closer than the others, then moved rapidly eastwards and disappeared; no sound or visible trail was reported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/05/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1952-10-8773352-Berlin-Germany.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

The report is stronger than the average anecdotal UFO account in several respects. More than one member of the base staff was involved, witnesses were interviewed collectively within a day, and the observation was documented through a military intelligence system. The file also preserves uncertainty rather than embellishing the event: distances and speeds could not be established, and the witnesses could not give a precise physical description beyond luminous, cloud-like forms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/05/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1952-10-8773352-Berlin-Germany.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

Yet the case has important weaknesses. There was no photograph, radar confirmation or recovered material. The apparent hovering lasted only about half a minute, and the objects were observed at night with no reliable distance reference. Members of the fire department suggested that [searchlights]({{ 'searchlights/' | relative_url }}) illuminating clouds might have created the effect, although one witness said the forms resembled searchlights without any visible beams rising from the ground. That leaves the file genuinely uncertain, but not evidentially strong enough to establish an extraordinary craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/05/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1952-10-8773352-Berlin-Germany.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

A second report preserved in the same file describes four white objects seen from a building near the base. They reportedly crossed the sky in formation, disappeared and later returned moving in the opposite direction. One rose upwards while the others continued west. The observer compared their appearance to the Moon, heard no sound and could not estimate their altitude or speed. He contacted the control tower after the second appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/05/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1952-10-8773352-Berlin-Germany.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

This second account adds interest but not decisive corroboration. Its formation drawings and directional description make it more specific than a vague report of “lights”, yet it still lacks objective measurements. Birds catching light, balloons, aircraft viewed under unusual conditions and atmospheric effects remain plausible categories, even where no single explanation can now be demonstrated. The surviving paperwork shows that something puzzling was reported; it does not show that the objects possessed extraordinary technology.

## Why Cold War Berlin generated official files

The American reports are inseparable from Berlin’s political and military setting. During the early 1950s, Western bases in the city operated within Soviet-controlled East Germany, only a few years after the Berlin Airlift. Unidentified movements near Tempelhof could potentially indicate aircraft, balloons, surveillance activity or unfamiliar military technology. That helps explain why witness statements travelled into the United States Air Force’s UFO-investigation system.

Project [Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book/' | relative_url }}), which formally operated from 1952 to 1969, was tasked with evaluating whether UFO reports represented a national-security threat and with analysing their [likely causes]({{ 'likely-causes/' | relative_url }}). Its records eventually included 12,618 reports from many locations, not only the United States. The presence of Berlin files therefore demonstrates official attention, but it should not be mistaken for evidence that investigators accepted an extraterrestrial explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unwritten-record.blogs.archives.gov/2013/09/30/project-blue-book-looking-to-the-film-record/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unwritten-record.blogs.archives.gov">[The Unwritten Record]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unwritten-record.blogs.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Unwritten Record Project Blue Book: Spotting UFOs in the Film Record</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Unwritten RecordProject Blue Book: Spotting UFOs in the Film RecordOctober 1, 2013 — 30 Sept 2013 — Project Blue Book was actually th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1, 2013</span></span></span>

At least three Blue Book files connected with Berlin survive in public digital collections: the extensive 1952 material, a January 1954 report and a brief July 1954 West Berlin entry. Their outcomes illustrate three very different meanings of the word “UFO”: a investigated but uncertain observation, a sighting given an ordinary explanation, and a report that could not be evaluated because the data were inadequate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category%3AUFO_sightings_in_Germany" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons Category:UFO sightings in Germany</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsCategory:UFO sightings in Germany - Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

The January 1954 case was reported by a civilian who saw a bright object move from the north-west towards the south-east for five to ten seconds. It was described as approximately as bright as the full Moon, travelling in a straight line without sound or trail. Blue Book classified it as astronomical and specifically as a meteor sighting, judging the apparent speed and angular movement consistent with that explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b5/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1954-01-6779499-BerlinGermany.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

The West Berlin entry dated 5 July 1954 is much weaker. Its record card contains little more than a reference to another status report, and the assessment states “[insufficient data]({{ 'insufficient-data/' | relative_url }})”. That is not a finding that the event defied conventional explanation. It means investigators lacked enough usable information to make a reasoned judgement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1d/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1954-07-8714556-WestBerlin.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

This distinction is frequently lost in UFO retellings. A case can remain “unidentified” because the observation was extraordinary, but it can also remain unidentified because the witness report was too brief, late, imprecise or incomplete. Berlin’s 1954 file is a clear example of the latter.

## The Stasi’s very small UFO file

East German intelligence also recorded unidentified aerial observations, although the surviving material does not support claims of a large secret UFO programme. A local report on the archival file describes it as only 12 pages long and containing two principal incidents handled by the Ministry for State Security.

The Berlin-related event occurred at about 3am on 27 November 1983. East German border guards reportedly saw an unlit, silent “flying body” travelling towards West Berlin and Spandau. A second incident, over Halle in February 1985, was independently reported by five members of the East German police. The file also included a West Berlin newspaper cutting about a UFO observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bz-berlin.de/archiv-artikel/stasi-dokumentierte-ufos-ueber-west-berlin" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bz-berlin.de">[B.Z. The bureaucratic setting is more revealing than the descriptions themselves. In a divided city, a silent object crossing towards West Berlin could represent a surveillance platform, balloon, illicit flight or other security concern. Intelligence officers did not need to believe in alien spacecraft to preserve such a report. Their job was to notice anomalous movement around a highly controlled border.

A later sceptical assessment attributed the Berlin and Halle observations to meteors. That explanation is plausible for a brief luminous transit, but the available press summary does not reproduce enough original observational detail to test it closely. The responsible conclusion is therefore modest: the incidents entered a state-security file, but the surviving public account supplies neither strong evidence for an exotic object nor enough detail for an independent reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bz-berlin.de/archiv-artikel/stasi-dokumentierte-ufos-ueber-west-berlin" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bz-berlin.de">[B.Z. The Stasi material also corrects a common myth about official UFO documents. A government file proves that an observation was reported and considered relevant enough to retain. It does not, by itself, validate the witness’s interpretation or reveal what the object was.

## Berlin’s reported “flaps” were often technology-driven

Berlin does not appear to have experienced a well-documented, state-wide UFO wave comparable with the famous Belgian triangular-sighting period of 1989–90. Instead, its reporting history seems to contain smaller surges shaped by whatever unfamiliar objects were then becoming common in the sky.

One striking example is the year 2000. Sceptical investigator Werner Walter said Berlin produced an unusually large number of reports because a local business sold sky lanterns, which were launched frequently but were unfamiliar to many observers. Seen at night, such lanterns can appear as silent orange or reddish lights, drift in groups, change apparent formation with the wind and disappear when their flames go out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bz-berlin.de/archiv-artikel/stasi-dokumentierte-ufos-ueber-west-berlin" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bz-berlin.de">[B.Z. Berlin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bz-berlin.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">B.Z. Berlin Stasi dokumentierte UFOs über West-Berlin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">B.Z. BerlinStasi dokumentierte UFOs über West-Berlin - B.Z. – Die Stimme Berlins...</span></span></span>

The modern equivalent is the satellite train. CENAP, a private German reporting and investigation network, received a record 1,084 reports from Germany, Austria and Switzerland in 2024. Its investigators said many involved newly launched Starlink satellites, which can resemble a procession of evenly spaced lights. Other common identifications included bright planets, Sirius, balloons, drones, aircraft, helicopters, event lighting, meteors and optical effects in phone images.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tagesspiegel.de/wissen/ratselhafte-lichter-am-himmel-ungewohnliche-viele-ufo-sichtungen-12993025.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tagesspiegel.de">[Tagesspiegel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tagesspiegel.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on tagesspiegel.de.</span></span></span>

This national pattern is directly relevant to Berlin because the city combines dense population, extensive artificial lighting, heavy air traffic and widespread smartphone use. More observers and more cameras produce more reports, but not necessarily better evidence. Phone footage often removes the horizon, magnifies shake, loses focus and turns ordinary points of light into apparently structured “orbs”. CENAP says it checks the time and location of reports against astronomical positions, satellite passes, aircraft movements and a long-running reference archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tagesspiegel.de/wissen/ratselhafte-lichter-am-himmel-ungewohnliche-viele-ufo-sichtungen-12993025.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tagesspiegel.de">[Tagesspiegel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tagesspiegel.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on tagesspiegel.de.</span></span></span>

Berlin’s strong skyglow also complicates observation. Measurements near Potsdam-Babelsberg found that artificial light and cloud cover could make the night sky far brighter than natural conditions, with clouds greatly amplifying urban illumination. In practical terms, this can hide stars, create bright patches and make distant lights harder to judge against a clear background.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.2038" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv The night sky brightness at Potsdam-Babelsberg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv The night sky brightness at Potsdam-Babelsberg</span></span></span>

## UFO reports and drone incidents are not the same thing

The word “UFO” simply means that an object has not yet been identified by the observer. In present-day Berlin, however, an unknown object near an airport is likely to be handled first as a drone or aviation-safety incident, not as an astronomical mystery.

In October 2025, a reported drone near Berlin Brandenburg Airport led to the suspension of flights for nearly two hours. A witness reported the object, police confirmed a sighting but did not recover the drone, and operations later resumed. The event remained uncertain at the level of ownership and exact aircraft identification, yet its practical meaning was clear: an unverified object in protected airspace was treated as a potential collision and security hazard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/233028211d399bbbbbca72a08a767ef3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AP News A drone sighting temporarily suspends air travel at the Berlin airport</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AP News A drone sighting temporarily suspends air travel at the Berlin airport</span></span></span>

Such incidents belong to Berlin’s broader history of unidentified aerial reports, but they should not be folded casually into claims about anomalous craft. Small drones can be difficult to locate after a sighting, may not appear clearly on conventional airport radar and can disappear before police arrive. Failure to recover one is not evidence that it was technologically inexplicable.

This modern category also changes the burden of investigation. The first questions are now whether airspace was breached, whether pilots or passengers were endangered, whether images or radar tracks exist, and whether an operator can be identified. The extraterrestrial question is remote unless conventional aviation, astronomy, balloons, satellites, atmospheric effects and deliberate hoaxes have first been excluded.

## The Bundestag paper was not a hidden Berlin UFO investigation

Berlin also features in German UFO history because it is the seat of the federal parliament and the location of a notable transparency dispute. In 2010, Berlin resident Frank Reitemeyer sought access to a Bundestag research paper concerning the search for extraterrestrial life and the implementation of a 1978 United Nations decision relating to unidentified flying objects. Access was initially refused, leading to legal action and press claims about a “secret UFO paper”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.morgenpost.de/politik/article105342687/Berliner-erklagt-Einsicht-in-geheimes-Ufo-Papier.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: morgenpost.de">[Morgenpost]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">morgenpost.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berliner erklagt Einsicht in geheimes Ufo-Papier</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berliner erklagt Einsicht in geheimes Ufo-Papier</span></span></span>

The document was eventually published by the Bundestag. It is a parliamentary research service paper, not the findings of a covert German UFO investigation. Its subject is the political and institutional treatment of UFO questions, including international approaches and the argument that open reporting can improve airspace safety.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/406336/741fdc9b7e96b9346e4e3414225b2835/wd-8-104-09-pdf-data.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bundestag.de">[Deutscher Bundestag]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bundestag.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">wd 8 104 09 pdf data</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">wd 8 104 09 pdf data</span></span></span>

The episode matters because it shows how secrecy can be inferred from routine restrictions on internal parliamentary research. The initial refusal undoubtedly encouraged suspicion, but the released document did not reveal recovered spacecraft, alien contact or a hidden Berlin incident. Later disclosure weakened the more dramatic interpretation while strengthening a narrower point: official bodies had considered how governments and international institutions deal with UFO reports.

## How strong is Berlin’s UFO evidence?

Berlin’s historical record can be divided into three broad levels.

**The Tempelhof case is genuinely unresolved but limited.** It has multiple military witnesses, prompt interviews and a surviving intelligence report. Against that stand the brief duration, lack of radar or photographic confirmation, uncertain distance and plausible possibility of illuminated cloud or another conventional aerial phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/05/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1952-10-8773352-Berlin-Germany.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

**Several cases are plausibly or officially explained.** Blue Book assessed the January 1954 observation as a meteor. The 1983 and 1985 East German incidents were later interpreted sceptically as meteors, while the Berlin reporting surge around 2000 was linked to sky lanterns. Modern investigators repeatedly identify satellites, planets, aircraft, balloons and camera artefacts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b5/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1954-01-6779499-BerlinGermany.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

**Other cases are too weak to judge.** The July 1954 West Berlin card is the clearest archival example: “insufficient data” is an evidence problem, not a mystery verdict. Recent isolated photographs and videos in the press generally suffer from the same difficulty when they lack precise time, direction, duration, original files and independent witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1d/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1954-07-8714556-WestBerlin.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

Taken as a whole, Berlin’s UFO history is historically valuable but scientifically inconclusive. The city’s archives show that military personnel, border guards, police, civilians and intelligence officials sometimes encountered aerial observations they could not immediately identify. They also show that later checking often reduced the mystery. No Berlin case currently combines a clear instrument record, independently calibrated imagery, multiple separated viewpoints and physical evidence strong enough to establish an extraordinary vehicle.

## Berlin’s lasting place in German UFO history

Berlin matters chiefly as a city where uncertain aerial observations repeatedly crossed institutional boundaries. At Tempelhof they became American air-intelligence reports. At the inner-German border they became Stasi paperwork. In reunified Germany, questions about official openness became a Bundestag access dispute. Around modern airports, unidentified objects have become an immediate drone-safety problem.

That history discourages two easy conclusions. It is wrong to say that every Berlin UFO report was imaginary: witnesses saw real lights or objects and, in several instances, officials recorded them seriously. It is equally wrong to treat official interest or an unresolved label as proof of alien technology. The surviving evidence is strongest when it documents how observations were reported and investigated, and weakest when later retellings inflate uncertainty into certainty.

Berlin therefore offers a useful model for reading UFO history elsewhere in Germany. The crucial questions are not simply whether a file exists or whether a witness seemed sincere. They are whether the time and direction were recorded, whether independent observers were separated, whether radar or photographs corroborated the account, whether astronomical and aviation data were checked, and whether “unresolved” means anomalous or merely under-documented. By those standards, Berlin has several worthwhile historical cases, one notably intriguing Tempelhof incident, many ordinary explanations and no confirmed evidence of an extraterrestrial craft.

    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
