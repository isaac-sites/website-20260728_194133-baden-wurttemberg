## Introduction

Two other strands complete the regional picture. A celebrated account from Stralsund in 1665 reveals how unusual sky phenomena were transformed by early newspapers, religious expectations and visual [storytelling]({{ 'storytelling/' | relative_url }}). Taken together, the record is interesting but not strongly anomalous. ## The Greifswald lights of 1990

On the evening of 24 August 1990, groups of bright reddish-white lights appeared over the Baltic region between Greifswald, Rügen and the wider coast. Contemporary accounts described seven luminous objects arranged like a bunch of grapes, with further lights or flashes appearing nearby. Witnesses said the formations seemed almost stationary or descended slowly and silently. The case became famous because it produced more than testimony. A couple in Greifswald recorded the lights on video, while other photographs and films were reportedly made from different positions along the coast. The footage shows multiple points of light maintaining a loose formation while shifting slightly relative to one another. That combination — prolonged duration, multiple witnesses and visual recordings — gave the incident a credibility that most UFO reports lack. It also made the event highly reusable in television programmes and UFO documentaries, where the lights could be shown without the less dramatic work of reconstructing their location, distance and likely source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[gwup.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">e. The timing added to the mystery. The sighting occurred during the final months of East Germany, when military structures were changing, archives were difficult to access and official responsibilities were unclear. Initial enquiries reportedly produced little useful information, while police and military contacts either lacked records or did not acknowledge relevant activity. ### Why flares fit the observations

The leading explanation is that the formations were parachute-supported military illumination flares released over a Baltic air-training zone. Such flares burn intensely while descending slowly beneath parachutes. Seen from many kilometres away, they can appear suspended in the sky, move only slightly and remain silent. A package containing seven separate pyrotechnic lights would also account for the repeated seven-point formations visible in the Greifswald images.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.openpr.de/news/865485/UFO-Phaenomen-Untersucher-vom-CENAPUFO-Lichter-von-Greifswald-Ostsee-24-August-1990.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openpr.de">[openpr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openpr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">open PR.de UF O-Phänomen-Untersucher vom CENAP:UFO-Lichter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">open PR.de UF O-Phänomen-Untersucher vom CENAP:UFO-Lichter</span></span></span>

Later research linked the event to activity in the former East German air-firing zone over the Baltic and proposed that Czechoslovak military aircraft had dropped Soviet-designed SAB illumination devices during an exercise. Former military personnel reportedly confirmed that comparable equipment had been used in the area. Earlier enquiries also produced a 1994 response from Germany’s Federal Office of Defence Technology and Procurement stating that relevant trials involving illumination targets had taken place on the date in question, although critics have noted that the surviving official correspondence is brief and not wholly consistent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archiv.mufon-ces.org/text/deutsch/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archiv.mufon-ces.org">[mufon-ces.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archiv.mufon-ces.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on mufon-ces.org.</span></span></span>

### What remains uncertain

Calling the [Greifswald case]({{ 'greifswald-case/' | relative_url }}) explained does not mean every detail has been conclusively documented. No complete military operations file identifying the exact aircraft, crew, release coordinates and individual flare type has become a widely available primary record. Some UFO investigators have therefore disputed whether the military evidence is sufficient, particularly where witness descriptions suggest lights entering or leaving the formation or remaining visible longer than a single flare would normally burn.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.openpr.de/news/581015/Unidentifizierte-Flugobjekte-ueber-der-Ostsee-DEGUFO-e-V-bittet-um-Ihre-Mithilfe.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openpr.de">[openpr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openpr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">open PR.de Unidentifizierte Flugobjekte über der Ostsee</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">open PR.de Unidentifizierte Flugobjekte über der Ostsee</span></span></span>

Those objections justify caution about the reconstruction, but they do not create positive evidence for an extraordinary craft. A succession of flare drops can also make a formation seem to persist longer than any one flare. ## The smaller flap before Greifswald

The August spectacle did not emerge in isolation. Local newspapers had already reported unusual lights in the Güstrow and Rostock areas during June and July 1990. One account involved pupils who said a bright spherical object appeared close to a garden gathering before moving away. Other witnesses around Rostock, Laage and the Salzhaff described orange or yellow balls of light travelling silently across the night sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

These reports are much weaker than the Greifswald case. They rely mainly on recollection and newspaper summaries, with no comparable set of images, measurements or independent technical records. Contemporary sceptical investigators suggested that at least some resembled small hot-air lanterns or balloons. The reports may also have primed journalists and residents to interpret the later Baltic flare display as part of an ongoing regional wave rather than as a separate military event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

This is a familiar feature of UFO flaps: once one report receives publicity, people pay closer attention to the sky, previously unremarkable observations are reconsidered and newspapers gather separate events under a single mysterious label. That does not mean witnesses invented what they saw. It means the apparent “wave” may consist of unrelated balloons, astronomical objects, aircraft and unusual lighting conditions joined together by publicity rather than by a common physical cause. The 1990 Mecklenburg-Vorpommern material fits that pattern better than it supports repeated visits by one unknown phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alien.de/cenap/greifswald/greifswald.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alien.de">[alien.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alien.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alien.de.</span></span></span>

## Stralsund’s air battle of 1665

The state’s oldest famous sky story predates modern UFO culture by almost three centuries. On 8 April 1665, six fishermen off Stralsund reportedly watched what first appeared to be flocks of birds transform into warships fighting in the sky. Contemporary versions described noise, smoke, figures on the vessels and, later, a flat grey disc or plate-like form above St Nicholas’ Church. The witnesses were said to have fled and to have suffered trembling or pain the next day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smb.museum/en/exhibitions/detail/a-ufo-in-1665/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smb.museum">[Staatliche Museen zu Berlin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smb.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a ufo in 1665</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staatliche Museen zu BerlinA UFO in 1665: The Air Battle of Stralsund...</span></span></span>

The value of the episode lies less in proving what physically appeared than in showing how reports were shaped. News sheets and illustrated publications competed to produce dramatic versions, while religious culture encouraged readers to treat strange events as divine warnings. The story accumulated imagery drawn from naval warfare, biblical omens and seventeenth-century designs for imaginary flying ships. Crucially, the surviving contemporary accounts did not interpret the event as extraterrestrial visitation; that association was applied much later by modern UFO culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smb.museum/en/exhibitions/detail/a-ufo-in-1665/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smb.museum">[Staatliche Museen zu Berlin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smb.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a ufo in 1665</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staatliche Museen zu BerlinA UFO in 1665: The Air Battle of Stralsund...</span></span></span>

The Berlin Kunstbibliothek’s 2023 exhibition proposed that the original stimulus may have been an atmospheric mirage of a naval engagement occurring beyond the horizon. A superior mirage can lift and distort distant ships, making them appear elevated, stretched or detached from the sea. Whether that precisely explains every detail cannot now be verified, but it is historically and physically more plausible than treating baroque reports as literal descriptions of spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smb.museum/en/exhibitions/detail/a-ufo-in-1665/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smb.museum">[Staatliche Museen zu Berlin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smb.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a ufo in 1665</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staatliche Museen zu BerlinA UFO in 1665: The Air Battle of Stralsund...</span></span></span>

The Stralsund case therefore belongs in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern’s UFO history as a study in transmission. There was an unusual report, but the evidence reaches modern readers through competitive publishers, symbolic illustrations and later retellings. It demonstrates why old chronicles cannot be read as if they were instrument logs: they reveal how people described and understood the sky in their own period, not an unfiltered record of an object’s shape or performance.

## Later sightings and recurring explanations

Later reports from the state have generally been smaller and easier to explain. In the Ludwigslust district in July 2008, witnesses described a large, silent, single-coloured light that did not blink like an aircraft. Investigator Werner Walter attributed it to the sky lanterns that had recently become popular. These small paper balloons carry a flame and can look surprisingly bright, hover in gentle winds and vanish when the flame goes out or the lantern enters cloud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://katapult-mv.de/artikel/e-t-in-mv/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: katapult-mv.de">[KATAPULT MV]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">katapult-mv.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">KATAPULT MVE.T. in MV &#124; KATAPULT MV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">KATAPULT MVE.T. in MV &#124; KATAPULT MV</span></span></span>

Reports elsewhere in Germany that evening described a bright meteor with a green trail, and local coverage likewise treated a small incoming space rock as the probable explanation. The wide geographic visibility and brief, fast-moving appearance strongly favour a fireball rather than a hovering vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.zeit.de/news/2015-11/01/buntes-mehrere-menschen-sichten-angebliches-ufo-in-der-halloween-nacht-01113404" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zeit.de">[zeit.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zeit.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">buntes mehrere menschen sichten angebliches ufo in der halloween nacht 01113404</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Neubrandenburg (dpa) - Mehrere Menschen haben unabhängig voneinander in der Halloween-Nacht eine ungewöhnliche Lichterscheinung am Himmel...</span></span></span>

Contemporary reporting also requires a distinction between traditional UFO claims and unidentified drones. Mecklenburg-Vorpommern’s Baltic coastline contains ports, military sites and other sensitive infrastructure, and recent unidentified-drone reports have prompted genuine security enquiries. An aircraft may be unidentified to a witness or even temporarily unidentified to the police without being aerodynamically mysterious. Drone cases should therefore be assessed through flight restrictions, radar, radio detection and criminal investigation rather than automatically folded into extraterrestrial folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/article68dce72aeb7e531160b3f6ab" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTErmittlungen: Teil der Drohnensichtungen im Norden geklärt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sütterlin-Waack betont, es sei keine akute Gefährdungslage erkennbar, es handele sich eher um Versuch...</span></span></span>

Astronomical sources remain another recurring cause. Bright planets, satellite trains and meteors can all look unfamiliar when viewed without a clear horizon or sense of scale. ## Records, investigations and evidence limits

Germany does not have a state UFO investigation programme comparable to a police service or aviation accident bureau dedicated to every civilian sighting. Much of the usable record is maintained by private organisations. The Society for the Study of the UFO Phenomenon, known as GEP, accepts witness reports, publishes a searchable statistical database and makes research data available for further study. Its records include cases from Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, but the database should not be read as a count of unexplained craft: it includes reports later attributed to conventional causes as well as cases that remain open because information is incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/GEP_UAP_Statistik.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufo-forschung.de.</span></span></span>

CENAP, another private German reporting centre, played the central role in investigating Greifswald and has generally taken a strongly sceptical approach. Its work usefully preserves press reports and witness descriptions that might otherwise have been lost, but some conclusions rely on privately gathered correspondence, retrospective interviews and material that is not always presented as a complete public case file. The disagreement around Greifswald is therefore partly about standards of documentation, not simply belief versus disbelief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[gwup.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">e. For readers assessing any Mecklenburg-Vorpommern sighting, the most valuable evidence would be simultaneous recordings from known locations, original files with timestamps, clear viewing directions, weather data, aircraft and satellite records, and radar or other sensor confirmation. Multiple witnesses strengthen the fact that something was visible, but they do not by themselves establish its distance, size or identity. The Greifswald case is the state’s clearest demonstration of that distinction: many people almost certainly saw the same real lights, while their nature was misjudged because the source was distant and poorly documented at the time.

## The state’s UFO history in perspective

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern does not present a strong pattern of persistently unexplained craft, repeated close encounters or well-corroborated aviation anomalies. Its reputation rests chiefly on the Greifswald lights, whose impressive witness and video record is counterbalanced by a persuasive military-flare explanation. The earlier Stralsund story is culturally important but inseparable from baroque media, religious interpretation and the possibility of atmospheric distortion. Later cases mostly illustrate familiar sources of confusion such as sky lanterns, meteors, planets and drones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.gwup.net/2012/11/25/greifswald-ufo-case-closed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.gwup.net">[gwup.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.gwup.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald ufo case closed</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Leuchtbomben des Typs SAB“ zurückführt, „bestückt mit jeweils sieben... That does not make the regional history empty or uninteresting. It makes it a particularly useful example of how UFO cases should be classified. Some reports are **resolved** because a conventional source closely matches the timing and appearance. Others are **weakly sourced**, meaning too little information survives to reach a firm conclusion. A smaller number may remain **unresolved**, but unresolved simply means that the available evidence does not permit identification. In Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, no well-documented case currently requires an extraterrestrial explanation, and later research has weakened rather than strengthened the state’s most famous modern claim.
