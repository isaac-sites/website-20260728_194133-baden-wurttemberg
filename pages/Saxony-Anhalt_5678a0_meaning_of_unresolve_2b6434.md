## Introduction

In modern [Saxony-Anhalt]({{ 'why-saxony-anhalts-ufo-mysteries-usually/' | relative_url }}) UFO records, an “unresolved” or “not conclusively classified” entry usually means that investigators lacked enough reliable information to identify the cause. It does not mean that they established the presence of an extraordinary craft. A brief sighting, an incomplete video or a report submitted long after the event may leave several ordinary explanations possible without allowing any one of them to be proved.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Saxony-Anhalt_5678a0_meaning_of_unresolve_2b6434-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Unresolved" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Cases from Haldensleben, Halberstadt and [Magdeburg]({{ 'magdeburg/' | relative_url }}) illustrate this distinction. Some contain little more than a witness description or a short recording. Others occurred amid clouds, fireworks or busy skies where aircraft, satellites, drones, insects and camera effects are difficult to separate retrospectively. They remain open because the evidence cannot support a firm decision, not because conventional explanations have been eliminated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/fall/haldensleben-20231210-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">haldensleben 20231210 c</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Haldensleben 2023 — Sichtung &amp; GEP-Bewertung — Ufokarte.de...</span></span></span>

## Unidentified is not the same as inexplicable

The most important distinction is between a phenomenon that has not been identified and one that has resisted a thorough investigation despite strong evidence. Those categories are often blurred in popular accounts, where any unresolved database label can be presented as a surviving mystery.

France’s official GEIPAN investigation service makes the difference explicit. Its category C covers phenomena that remain unidentified because information is missing, while category D is reserved for reports that remain unidentified after investigation. GEIPAN also allows cases to be reconsidered if new evidence appears. The classification therefore records the state of the evidence, not a permanent judgement about the nature of the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/faq-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The German records relevant to Saxony-Anhalt require an additional caution. The widely used [case data]({{ 'case-data/' | relative_url }}) were assembled by the Gesellschaft zur Erforschung des UFO-Phänomens, or GEP, a private research association rather than a Saxony-Anhalt government investigation office. Its published dataset contains dates, locations, witness summaries, classifications and investigative results from submitted reports. An “unclassified” entry is consequently a documented research status, not an official declaration by the state that an unknown aircraft entered its [airspace]({{ 'airspace/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/10547073" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on zenodo.org.</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because databases preserve reports for different reasons. A case may be retained to document what a witness experienced, even when the available material is too poor for meaningful testing. Archiving a report does not give every detail equal evidential weight.

## Haldensleben shows what missing detail looks like

A Haldensleben report dated 10 December 2023 describes a 23-year-old witness seeing a flying chain of lights before clouds obscured it. The entry is listed as not conclusively classified. The published summary gives no precise time, duration, direction, angular spacing, photographs or independent [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-9fc6b4/' | relative_url }}). Those omissions make it difficult to compare the sighting confidently with satellite passes, aircraft traffic, drones or other moving lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/fall/haldensleben-20231210-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">haldensleben 20231210 c</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Haldensleben 2023 — Sichtung &amp; GEP-Bewertung — Ufokarte.de...</span></span></span>

A chain of lights immediately suggests possibilities such as a [Starlink]({{ 'starlink-f2efa7/' | relative_url }}) formation, several aircraft, illuminated balloons or drones. Yet an investigator should not select one merely because it sounds plausible. Satellite identification normally requires a reliable observation time and direction. Aircraft comparison needs similar timing and positional information. Without those details, the responsible conclusion is that several explanations remain available but unverified.

The contrast with another Haldensleben case is revealing. On 10 April 2015, a witness reported a silent round-to-angular object travelling from east to north-west for about two minutes. That report was classified as identified, with a quadcopter given as the probable explanation. The cases are not separated by how strange the witness descriptions sound; they are separated by whether investigators considered the available circumstances sufficient to support a likely cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/fall/haldensleben-20150410-a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufokarte.de.</span></span></span>

This comparison also shows that “identified” need not mean demonstrated beyond all doubt. Case investigators often work with a best-fitting conventional explanation. Conversely, “not classified” may mean that even a likely explanation cannot be supported adequately from the record.

## Halberstadt: video does not guarantee an answer

A modern camera recording can feel more objective than testimony, but it may create its own uncertainties. In Halberstadt, a video made at 00:05 on 1 January 2024 during New Year fireworks contained a bright, elongated object apparently moving vertically downwards through the northern part of the frame. The witness noticed it only when reviewing the footage later, and the report remained not conclusively classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/fall/halberstadt-20240101-a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Halberstadt 2024 — Sichtung &amp; GEP-Bewertung — Ufokarte.de...</span></span></span>

The setting makes the case difficult rather than necessarily extraordinary. Fireworks produce sparks, fragments, smoke, changing illumination and objects at very different distances. A nearby particle or insect crossing the lens can appear elongated because of motion blur or exposure settings. A distant firework fragment may seem to move in an unexpected direction when the camera is tilted. Without the original file, camera settings, frame-by-frame analysis, exact lens orientation and an independent view, apparent speed and size cannot be trusted.

This is a common weakness in single-camera cases. A video records movement across an image sensor, not automatically the object’s true distance, dimensions or velocity. A small nearby object and a large distant one can occupy the same number of pixels. Unless distance can be established through triangulation, focus, shadows, radar or another calibrated measurement, claims that an object moved at exceptional speed are usually unsupported.

The Halberstadt database contains eight documented reports, but only some are unclassified. Others have been attributed to ordinary causes. The mixture is important: the presence of open entries does not demonstrate a persistent local anomaly. It shows that reports from the same area can have very different evidential quality and outcomes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/ort/halberstadt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O-Sichtungen in Halberstadt (Sachsen-Anhalt) — Ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UF O-Sichtungen in Halberstadt (Sachsen-Anhalt) — Ufokarte.de</span></span></span>

## Strange testimony can still be weak evidence

An unusual 2017 Halberstadt report describes a slender, roughly two-metre figure of white light appearing after a crackling noise and disappearing vertically after about 15 seconds. It was submitted by email and remains not conclusively classified. The description is striking, but the published entry contains no corroborating recording, second witness, environmental measurement or physical trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/fall/halberstadt-20170621-a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufokarte.de.</span></span></span>

Such a case cannot be responsibly converted into a firm claim about an aerial object. The witness may have described the experience sincerely, yet sincerity does not determine whether the cause was external, accurately perceived or correctly remembered. Reflections, nearby electrical effects, unusual lighting, visual disturbances and errors in estimating scale can all produce sincere but mistaken interpretations.

Nor can investigators safely impose an explanation without adequate evidence. Labelling the experience a hallucination, prank or electrical phenomenon would require information that the public summary does not provide. The most cautious position is therefore limited: the report exists, its cause is unknown, and its evidential strength is low.

This is why unresolved and compelling are separate judgements. A poorly documented claim can be impossible to solve precisely because it supplies too little information. Its resistance to identification does not make it a stronger mystery.

## Magdeburg and the problem of apparent extraordinary motion

A Magdeburg report from 7 August 2022 describes a glowing, ball-like object circling, changing colour and apparently accelerating at “impossible” speed before disappearing and reappearing elsewhere. The case remains not conclusively classified. No supporting sensor record or triangulated distance is included in the public summary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/fall/magdeburg-20220807-a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Magdeburg 2022 — Sichtung &amp; GEP-Bewertung — Ufokarte.de...</span></span></span>

Descriptions of rapid acceleration sound impressive, but speed cannot be calculated from angular movement alone. A light moving several degrees across the sky may be a nearby insect, a drone at moderate distance or a distant object whose movement is partly produced by the observer’s own head and eye motion. A point of light can also seem to wander when stared at against a dark, featureless background, an effect that complicates reports of stars or planets moving erratically.

The state’s records provide a useful control example. In April 2020, a Magdeburg witness and his father saw more than 30 evenly spaced objects crossing the sky. That formation was identified as SpaceX Starlink satellites. Before the orbital source was checked, the same observation could easily have been described as an organised fleet of unexplained lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/fall/magdeburg-20200418-a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufokarte.de.</span></span></span>

The difference was not that the Starlink witnesses were less credible. Their report happened to contain a recognisable pattern that could be matched with a known technological event. The 2022 report lacks an equivalent independent reference, so the dramatic interpretation cannot be confirmed or decisively replaced.

## How missing data blocks an investigation

Modern cases tend to remain open when investigators cannot reconstruct the basic geometry and circumstances of the observation. The most damaging gaps are usually mundane:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **No exact time:** Satellite passes, aircraft tracks and astronomical positions cannot be checked reliably.
* **No direction or elevation:** Investigators cannot establish where in the sky the phenomenon appeared.
* **No original media file:** Compression, cropping and social-media processing can hide metadata and create artefacts.
* **No reference points:** Apparent movement and size cannot be converted into distance or speed.
* **No independent witness:** A perceptual or camera effect cannot be compared with another viewpoint.
* **Delayed reporting:** Flight records, weather detail and the witness’s immediate memory may no longer be available.
* **Short duration:** A few seconds may be insufficient to note shape, sound, trajectory or environmental context.

</div>

NASA’s independent UAP work reached a similar general conclusion: most sightings provide very limited data, and the shortage of calibrated, high-quality observations prevents strong scientific findings. NASA states that it has found no evidence supporting extraterrestrial technology as the explanation for UAP reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/faqs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP FAQs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science UAP FAQs</span></span></span>

This is not an argument that every report has an ordinary cause already known. It is an argument about what the evidence permits. A conclusion must be weaker when the measurements are weak.

## Why open classifications should remain cautious

An investigator faced with incomplete evidence has three broad choices: force an explanation, declare a profound mystery or leave the case open. The third is often the most honest. Premature identification can conceal genuine uncertainty, while dramatic language can turn an ordinary documentation failure into an unsupported claim.

For Saxony-Anhalt, the surviving modern unidentified entries are best read as a mixed decision cluster rather than a collection of equivalent mysteries. The Haldensleben light chain lacks enough timing and trajectory information for a confident match. The Halberstadt New Year video was recorded in an environment rich in visual interference. The more unusual Halberstadt testimony lacks corroboration. The Magdeburg motion report contains extraordinary apparent behaviour but no measurements capable of establishing extraordinary physical performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufokarte.de/fall/haldensleben-20231210-c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufokarte.de">[Ufokarte.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufokarte.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">haldensleben 20231210 c</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Haldensleben 2023 — Sichtung &amp; GEP-Bewertung — Ufokarte.de...</span></span></span>

Later reporting has therefore neither proved these cases extraordinary nor fully removed them from the record. Their open status mainly preserves uncertainty. Better evidence—a second camera, an exact timestamp, original footage, aircraft data, satellite calculations or several independent witnesses—could strengthen an unusual interpretation, but it could just as readily identify a conventional source.

The correct public-facing reading is consequently modest. “Unresolved” means that no defensible final classification has been reached from the available material. It does not mean “inexplicable”, and it certainly does not mean that investigators have ruled out aircraft, satellites, drones, astronomical objects, atmospheric effects or recording artefacts. In Saxony-Anhalt’s modern UFO history, the label often tells us more about the limits of the investigation than about the object in the sky.
