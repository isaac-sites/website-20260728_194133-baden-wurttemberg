## Introduction

A UFO report in [Hesse]({{ 'why-hesses-ufo-story-is-mostly-about/' | relative_url }}) is usually investigated as a problem of reconstruction, not as a hunt for extraterrestrial craft. The first task is to establish exactly **when and where the witness was standing, which direction they faced, how long the event lasted and how the light moved**. Investigators can then compare the account with the positions of planets and stars, satellite passes, aircraft routes, meteor activity, rocket launches, weather conditions and familiar camera effects.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Hesse_19bb21_hesse_ufo_investigat_9709fd-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Investigation" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This work is most closely associated with the privately operated [CENAP]({{ 'cenap/' | relative_url }}) reporting centre in Lützelbach, southern Hesse. CENAP is neither a government agency nor an air-accident authority; it is a small, volunteer-led organisation with a strongly sceptical outlook. Its importance lies in the volume of reports it receives and the large archive of recurring explanations it has accumulated. Most cases are resolved through comparison rather than fieldwork, while a smaller number remain open because the original information is too incomplete to test.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutschland.de/en/topic/knowledge/ufos-over-germany-hansjuergen-koehler-and-cenap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutschland.de">[deutschland.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutschland.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs over Germany: An expert provides clarity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A hotline for UFO sightings. ## Recording the witness account

The quality of an investigation is often decided before any photograph is examined. CENAP’s Hansjürgen Köhler has said that a useful report should include the date, exact time, observation point and viewing direction. Investigators also need to know whether the witness was indoors, in a moving car or standing outside; whether clouds or buildings obscured the horizon; and whether the object appeared, disappeared or changed course relative to fixed landmarks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swr.de/swraktuell-radio/ufo-meldestelle-cenap-in-den-meisten-faellen-sind-es-drohnen-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swr.de">[swr.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO-Meldestelle CENAP: &quot;In den meisten Fällen sind es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO-Meldestelle CENAP: &quot;In den meisten Fällen sind es...September 18, 2025 — Hansjürgen Köhler leitet die UFO-Meldestelle CENAP. These details matter because descriptions such as “very high”, “close”, “fast” or “as large as a football” are not measurements. A solitary light against a dark sky gives the eye few reliable clues to distance. A bright planet may therefore appear to hover nearby, while a lantern or aircraft seen almost head-on may seem motionless before suddenly moving sideways as its angle changes.

Investigators try to replace these impressions with information that can be checked:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the start and end time, preferably to the minute;
* the precise location, including street, district or recognisable viewpoint;
* the direction and elevation of the object;
* its path relative to roofs, trees, hills or stars;
* colour, brightness, flashing pattern and apparent shape;
* weather and visibility;
* the number and position of witnesses;
* the original photograph or video rather than a compressed social-media copy.

</div>

Prompt reporting is especially valuable. Satellite predictions, flight tracks and weather records are easier to retrieve while the time is still certain, and investigators can ask follow-up questions before memory has been reshaped by discussion or news coverage. CENAP case material repeatedly stresses the need for an exact time, date, location and direction because an otherwise striking image may be impossible to identify without them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hjkc.de/_blog/4681-ufo-forschung---aus-der-fotoabteilung-der-ufo-meldestelle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hjkc.de">[CENAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hjkc.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CENAPRaumfahrt+Astronomie-Blog von CENAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wir brauchen im Ideal-Fall einfach kurzfristige Meldungen, die genaue Uhrzeit und Datum sowie Beobachtungsort mit Himmelsrichtung angeben...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 2015</span></span></span>

A careful interview also separates what the witness directly observed from what was inferred afterwards. “Three lights moved in a line” is an observation; “they were attached to a triangular craft” may be an interpretation produced by the mind joining separate points of light. This distinction does not imply dishonesty. German UFO research organisations generally recognise that sincere witnesses can accurately report an unusual experience while being mistaken about its cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/forschung/grundkonsens-der-ufo-forschung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufo-forschung.de.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2004</span></span></span>

## Testing astronomical and flight data

Once the basic account is fixed, the investigation becomes a process of eliminating candidates. The strongest explanation is not simply something that could have been visible. It should match the reported time, direction, movement, duration and appearance together.

Astronomical objects are checked first in many stationary-light cases. Venus, Jupiter and the bright star Sirius frequently generate reports because atmospheric turbulence can make them shimmer, change colour or appear to move. In August 2025, for example, observers in Hesse and neighbouring states described two bright morning lights in terms resembling paired drones or vehicle headlights. CENAP identified the display as the close appearance of Venus and Jupiter.

[Satellites]({{ 'satellites/' | relative_url }}) require a different comparison. A newly launched Starlink group may appear as a procession of evenly spaced lights, while older satellites can brighten briefly when sunlight reflects at a favourable angle. More than 120 reports received by CENAP in 2025 concerned Starlink, including some from pilots on night flights. A well-supported identification should reproduce the satellites’ expected position from the witness’s location rather than relying only on a general resemblance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/article695becd2fb77630dac278675" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTUFO-Meldestelle verzeichnet Rekordzahl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DIE WELTUFO-Meldestelle verzeichnet Rekordzahl</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2026</span></span></span>

The value of that reconstruction has been demonstrated in aviation research outside Hesse. In one study, researchers combined aircraft position data with orbital elements for a recently launched Starlink group and recreated what pilots would have seen from their cockpit. The model matched photographs and video from an incident initially reported by several pilots as an unusual aerial phenomenon. The lesson for Hessian cases is straightforward: independent positional data can turn an apparently corroborated sighting into a testable geometry problem.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08155" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2024</span></span></span>

Aircraft are checked through known routes, airport approaches and publicly available tracking records. This is particularly important around the [Frankfurt]({{ 'frankfurt/' | relative_url }}) region, where heavy traffic produces landing lights, holding patterns and distant aircraft seen nearly head-on. Public tracking data are useful but not complete: coverage depends on ground receivers and the aircraft’s transmitted data, so the absence of a visible track does not prove that no aircraft was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06254" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2025</span></span></span>

Rocket stages, fuel releases and re-entering space debris can create more dramatic reports. These events may produce expanding clouds, strings of fragments or slow-moving [fireballs]({{ 'fireballs/' | relative_url }}) visible across several German states. When dozens of reports share the same minute and direction, investigators can compare them as a single regional event rather than treating each testimony as a separate object. Modern meteor-camera and radar networks can sometimes reconstruct such events independently; a 2025 Falcon 9 upper-stage re-entry over central Europe was later traced using observations from 43 cameras and multistatic radar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/article695becd2fb77630dac278675" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTUFO-Meldestelle verzeichnet Rekordzahl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DIE WELTUFO-Meldestelle verzeichnet Rekordzahl</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 2026</span></span></span>

## Reading photographs without overvaluing them

A photograph feels more objective than memory, but many UFO images contain less information than the witness’s account. A bright point may be out of focus, digitally enlarged or distorted by automatic exposure. Internal lens reflections can create coloured discs or duplicate lights, while insects and birds close to the camera can appear as blurred objects against a distant sky.

Investigators therefore ask for the original file and examine practical questions before interpreting shape. Was the object visible to the naked eye, or discovered only later in the image? Does it appear in more than one frame? Does it move consistently against the background? Is there another bright source positioned where a lens reflection would be expected? Were the image and its metadata altered during editing or messaging?

Video can help when it contains fixed landmarks and sustained movement, but a shaky clip of a featureless light may make identification harder. Digital zoom exaggerates motion from the photographer’s hands, and autofocus can make a star or planet repeatedly expand and contract. CENAP’s published case material shows why the surrounding circumstances—time, direction and what the witness saw independently of the camera—remain essential even when an image exists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hjkc.de/_blog/4681-ufo-forschung---aus-der-fotoabteilung-der-ufo-meldestelle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hjkc.de">[CENAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hjkc.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CENAPRaumfahrt+Astronomie-Blog von CENAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wir brauchen im Ideal-Fall einfach kurzfristige Meldungen, die genaue Uhrzeit und Datum sowie Beobachtungsort mit Himmelsrichtung angeben...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 2015</span></span></span>

Multiple photographs do not automatically provide independent confirmation. Ten recordings of the same Starlink passage from different towns are strong evidence that a common event occurred, but they do not support ten separate unknown objects. Conversely, genuinely independent images from well-separated positions can be valuable because they may allow investigators to compare angles, estimate a trajectory or identify a shared astronomical or aerospace source.

## Matching reports to repeatable patterns

Hesse’s reporting centre benefits from repetition. A new witness may feel that a light formation is unprecedented, while the archive contains many earlier descriptions with the same timing and visual behaviour. Starlink trains, Venus near the horizon, illuminated balloons, [searchlights]({{ 'searchlights-ad50fc/' | relative_url }}) on low cloud and aircraft approaching an observer tend to generate recognisable families of reports.

Pattern matching is useful because different witnesses rarely use identical language. One may describe a “boomerang”, another a “row of windows” and a third a “silent formation”. Investigators look beneath those labels for common features: number of lights, spacing, direction, angular speed, fading sequence and whether the apparent shape depended on the observer mentally connecting separate points.

The Starlink display of 25 May 2019 illustrates this approach. CENAP received more than 50 reports describing a chain of small, star-like lights crossing the sky. The number of simultaneous witnesses strengthened the evidence for the sighting itself, but the shared time, direction and linear arrangement also made the satellite explanation much stronger. Corroboration proved that many people saw the phenomenon; it did not make the phenomenon anomalous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hjkc.de/_blog/12681-ufo-forschung-spektakulres-himmelsphnomen-ber-deutschland-055-mesz-update/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hjkc.de">[CENAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hjkc.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CENAPRaumfahrt+Astronomie-Blog von CENAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In welcher Richtung tauchte das „Phänomen” auf und in welche Richtung verschwand es (Himmelsrichtungen falls bekannt, ansonsten Richtung...</span></span></span>

This is one reason reporting surges need careful interpretation. CENAP received 1,084 submissions in 2024 and 1,348 in 2025 from Germany, Austria, Switzerland and occasional other countries. The increase reflected conspicuous satellite formations, planets, meteors, rocket events and growing attention to drones, not a corresponding rise in well-supported unexplained craft. According to the centre, none of its 2025 reports remained genuinely unidentified after examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hessenschau.de/panorama/wegen-starlink-und-drohnen-rekordzahl-an-ufo-sichtungen-in-deutschland-eingeschickt-v1%2Cufo-sichtungen-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hessenschau.de">[hessenschau.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hessenschau.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rekordzahl an Ufo-Sichtungen in Deutschland eingeschickt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rekordzahl an Ufo-Sichtungen in Deutschland eingeschickt</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

## Reaching an explanation or leaving it open

A convincing identification should explain the main features of the report without discarding inconvenient details. It is not enough to say that an aircraft was in the general region or that Venus was visible somewhere in the sky. The proposed cause should occupy the correct direction at the correct time and behave in a way compatible with the account.

In practice, cases tend to fall into three broad outcomes:

**Explained:** Independent information supports a specific source, such as a planet, aircraft, satellite train, balloon, meteor or rocket event.

**Probable:** One ordinary explanation fits most of the evidence, but the data are not precise enough for a definitive match.

**[Unresolved]({{ 'unresolved/' | relative_url }}) because of insufficient information:** No reliable identification can be made because the report lacks an exact time, direction, duration, original image or other testable detail.

That final category is often misunderstood. “Unresolved” does not mean investigators have confirmed extraordinary performance. It may mean only that a distant light reported weeks later cannot be reconstructed. A case with poor data can remain unidentified while being much weaker than a well-documented case that receives an ordinary explanation.

Other German investigators use more formalised procedures than CENAP, providing a useful standard against which the Hessian centre’s work can be judged. The Society for the Study of the UFO Phenomenon, or GEP, uses questionnaires to standardise reports and may conduct interviews, data analysis and field inquiries. Its published principles emphasise transparent methods, informed consent, source criticism and communication of findings to witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/ufo-sichtung-melden" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufo-forschung.de.</span></span></span>

CENAP’s quicker, explanation-led approach has also attracted criticism. Rival researchers have argued that investigations should use more consistent questionnaires, document uncertainty more formally and avoid settling on an answer before all testimony is assessed. Some witnesses have complained that a strongly sceptical manner can feel dismissive. Those objections do not invalidate successful matches with planets, satellites or aircraft, but they matter when the evidence is ambiguous and the proposed explanation depends heavily on judgement rather than a reproducible positional match.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrales_Erforschungsnetz_au%C3%9Fergew%C3%B6hnlicher_Himmelsph%C3%A4nomene" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## What makes a Hessian case persuasive

The best-investigated report is not necessarily the strangest one. It is the one with the strongest chain from observation to verification: a prompt account, exact time and location, a measured direction, original files, independent witnesses and records that can confirm or challenge the proposed explanation.

A serious case would become more compelling if several separated witnesses recorded the same object from known positions; if photographs preserved stars or landmarks that allowed accurate orientation; if aircraft, satellite, astronomical and weather checks failed; and if the reported motion could be reconstructed rather than merely described. Modern scientific proposals for investigating unusual aerial events place the same emphasis on multiple sensors, triangulation and independently verifiable measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2023</span></span></span>

Most Hessian reports never reach that evidential level. They begin as brief encounters with a light and are solved—or rendered permanently uncertain—by the amount of information captured in the first few minutes. That is the central lesson of Hesse’s civilian reporting history: sincere testimony starts the investigation, but precise timing, geometry and independent records determine how far it can go.
