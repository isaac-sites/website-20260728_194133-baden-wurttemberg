## Introduction

Many “hovering lights” reported over North Rhine-Westphalia are likely to be ordinary aircraft viewed from an awkward angle. An airliner approaching almost directly towards an observer produces little sideways movement against the sky. Its powerful landing lights may remain visible for several minutes, appearing as a single bright point that hangs in place, grows brighter and then suddenly turns or fades. When the aircraft changes heading, its red, green and flashing white lights can create the impression that one object has split, changed shape or accelerated.

This explanation matters especially in North Rhine-Westphalia because the state combines a very large urban population with several commercial airports and intersecting air routes. Düsseldorf and Cologne/Bonn alone handle hundreds of thousands of aircraft movements each year, while Dortmund, Münster/Osnabrück, Weeze and Paderborn/Lippstadt add further approach and departure traffic. ## Why an approaching aircraft can look stationary

The key illusion is caused by **bearing**, meaning the direction of an object relative to the observer. A plane crossing from left to right changes bearing quickly and is easy to recognise as moving. A plane travelling nearly towards the observer may cover several kilometres while remaining in almost the same part of the sky. Aviation researchers treat this lack of apparent movement seriously because an aircraft on a collision course can appear nearly stationary against the background until it becomes dangerously close.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02735" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Dataset of Stationary, Fixed-wing Aircraft on a Collision Course for Vision-Based Sense and AvoidDecember 6, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2021</span></span></span>

At night, the illusion becomes stronger. The observer usually has no clouds, buildings or landscape features beside the light with which to judge distance. A large airliner tens of kilometres away may therefore be mistaken for a much smaller object hovering nearby. Its speed is concealed because most of its motion is towards the observer rather than across the observer’s field of view.

Landing lights add to the effect. They are extremely bright, forward-facing lights intended to make an aircraft visible and illuminate the runway environment. Pilots are commonly encouraged to use them near airports and below 10,000 feet, including in daylight and reduced visibility. Seen nearly head-on, these lights can overwhelm the dimmer navigation lights and obscure the shape of the aircraft itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook/12_afh_ch11.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationAirplane Flying Handbook (3C) Chapter 11September 24, 2021 — Pilots are encouraged to turn on their landin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 24, 2021</span></span></span>

A typical sequence can consequently sound more unusual than it is:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A brilliant white light appears to remain motionless for several minutes.
* It becomes brighter without seeming to approach.
* It then moves sideways when the aircraft turns onto final approach.
* Red, green or flashing white lights become visible as the viewing angle changes.
* The light vanishes abruptly when the aircraft turns away, enters cloud or passes behind a building.

</div>

The apparent disappearance is particularly misleading. Witnesses sometimes interpret it as extreme acceleration, although a relatively small turn may simply direct the landing lights away from them.

Atmospheric conditions can complicate the view further. Haze enlarges and softens bright lights, thin clouds make them pulse, and turbulent air can cause colour changes or rapid flickering. Several lights from aircraft separated by many kilometres may also appear to form a triangle or line because the night sky provides no reliable indication of their different distances.

## NRW’s airport network creates repeated sighting conditions

North Rhine-Westphalia is unusually well suited to aircraft misidentification because its airports are embedded in or close to densely inhabited regions. Düsseldorf Airport lies within the Rhine-Ruhr urban area and operates parallel runways capable of handling concentrated arrival and departure periods. It recorded about 155,600 aircraft movements in 2024 and more than 159,900 in 2025. DFS, Germany’s air-navigation provider, describes its Düsseldorf tower as coordinating roughly 150,000 movements annually.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dus.com/de-de/konzern/presse/medieninformationen/geschaeftsergebnis-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dus.com">[Dus Airport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">geschaeftsergebnis 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Die Zahl der Flugbewegungen stieg von rund 155.600 im Jahr 2024 auf über 159.900 Starts und Landungen im vergangenen Jahr.... „Wir wachs...</span></span></span>

This places residents of Düsseldorf, Duisburg, Essen, Krefeld, Meerbusch, Neuss, Ratingen and surrounding districts beneath or near recurring traffic patterns. Düsseldorf Airport’s own noise-monitoring information reflects how widely its operations affect neighbouring municipalities. A witness does not need to live beside the runway to see an aircraft aligned for approach; a bright forward-facing light may be noticeable long before the plane reaches the airport.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dus.com/de-de/konzern/nachbarn/transparenz/umweltauswirkungen/fluglaerm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dus.com">[Dus Airport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dus.com.</span></span></span>

Cologne/Bonn creates a different but equally important context. DFS reports about 140,000 annual movements there, and the airport has three runways. Unlike airports with a general overnight closure, Cologne/Bonn has 24-hour operations and an indefinite night-flight permit, supporting substantial passenger and freight activity. It therefore supplies plausible aircraft candidates at hours when witnesses may assume that scheduled flying has ended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://karriere.dfs.de/de/arbeitgeber/standorte" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karriere.dfs.de">[dfs.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karriere.dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Karriere Standorte der DFS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DFS KarriereStandorte der DFS - Karriere bei der DFSvon Nordrhein-Westfalen, Mit etwa 140.000 Flugbewegungen jährlich stellt der Flughafe...</span></span></span>

That overnight activity is highly relevant to UFO reports. A bright light seen after midnight may seem less likely to be an airliner simply because roads are quiet and few people are outside. Around Cologne, Bonn, Troisdorf, Siegburg and the eastern side of the Rhine, however, cargo arrivals and departures can remain part of the normal background throughout the night.

Smaller airports add overlapping regional traffic. Dortmund recorded 36,080 movements in its recent annual reporting, while Münster/Osnabrück handles about 40,000 movements a year under both instrument and visual flight rules. Weeze and Paderborn/Lippstadt contribute further flights over less densely built districts, where an isolated light may appear especially conspicuous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dortmund-airport.com/en/dortmund-airport-continues-its-growth-trajectory-e8bf552f71f3537a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dortmund-airport.com">[Dortmund Airport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dortmund-airport.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The summer season marked the turning point: A significant upward trend, driven by strong demand during the summer holidays (.5 percent pa...</span></span></span>

Not every aircraft seen over NRW is taking off from or landing within the state. DFS says it manages roughly three million flights annually in German airspace, which contains a large share of Europe’s traffic within a comparatively small area. High-altitude overflights can cross above local airport traffic, producing separate lights that appear to converge, overtake one another or travel in formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/de/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dfs.de.</span></span></span>

## Flight paths do not remain visually identical

One common source of confusion is the assumption that aircraft always follow one fixed line. Published routes and familiar neighbourhood patterns are useful guides, but the track seen on a particular evening can vary with wind, runway selection, traffic spacing, weather and instructions from air-traffic control.

Aircraft normally take off and land into the wind where practical. A change in wind direction may therefore reverse the active runway direction, shifting arrivals from one side of an airport to the other. Residents who normally see aircraft moving away may suddenly see intense landing lights pointing towards them. A light that seems unprecedented may be normal traffic using the less familiar operating direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aircraftnoise.airservicesaustralia.com/category/what-are-the-flight-paths-in-my-area/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aircraftnoise.airservicesaustralia.com">[Aircraft Noise]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aircraftnoise.airservicesaustralia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on airservicesaustralia.com.</span></span></span>

Arriving aircraft also have to be sequenced. They may receive headings that place them on curved or stepped approaches before they join the runway centreline. From the ground, one aircraft may appear stationary while another crosses underneath it, even though the two are separated safely by distance and altitude. A turn can transform a single white point into an apparently triangular arrangement as the wingtip and tail lights become visible.

Departure paths can be deceptive in another way. An aircraft climbing away may turn soon after take-off, causing its lights to dim or disappear. Depending on the observer’s position, the turn may look like an abrupt stop followed by motion in a new direction. Engine noise is not always a reliable check: wind can carry sound away, urban noise can mask it, and sound reaches the observer after the visual position has already changed.

Military, police, medical and general-aviation flights further reduce the value of assumptions based only on passenger timetables. Düsseldorf’s published monthly movement figures, for example, explicitly include ambulance and military flights among the traffic counted. Helicopters can genuinely hover, while training flights may circle or repeat approaches. These remain conventional aircraft even when their route differs from the pattern expected by a casual observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dus.com/de-de/business/nachbarn/transparenz/flugbetrieb/betriebszeitn" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dus.com">[Dus Airport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dus.com.</span></span></span>

## What NRW reports reveal about witness interpretation

The public summaries issued by the Lüdenscheid-based Gesellschaft zur Erforschung des UFO-Phänomens, or GEP, demonstrate how frequently witnesses describe lights and shapes in aviation-like terms without initially being able to identify them. Its records include reports from Düsseldorf of triangular formations, pulsating lights, dark elongated objects and apparently stationary lights. These entries are valuable as records of what witnesses perceived, but the GEP warns that newly submitted accounts are “UFOs” only in the broad sense until investigation has been completed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/ufo-meldungen/5-neue-ufo-meldungen-1-alien-meldung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufo-forschung.de.</span></span></span>

A 2015 Düsseldorf report, for example, described a lit triangular object moving relatively quickly from west to east. A 2022 report from Düsseldorf-Vennhausen concerned a triangular craft noticed because of an engine sound and photographed as it passed overhead. In 2023, a witness in Düsseldorf-Golzheim reported three luminous spheres in a triangular formation over the Rhine. The short public summaries do not establish that these cases were aircraft, but they illustrate recurring features that require aviation checks: direction of travel, engine noise, formation geometry, proximity to approach sectors and whether separate lights were wrongly perceived as one body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/ufo-meldungen/5-neue-ufo-meldungen-1-alien-meldung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufo-forschung.de.</span></span></span>

The GEP’s wider database provides an important caution against selecting only dramatic reports. As of June 2026, it listed 6,570 reports, with 4,531 classified as identified objects. Among the 5,520 cases for which classification statistics were available, 82.1 per cent were identified and another 4.3 per cent were nearly identified; only 2.4 per cent fell within its grouped categories for stronger [unresolved]({{ 'unresolved-843c8a/' | relative_url }}) cases. These figures cover reports beyond North Rhine-Westphalia and do not mean that every identified case was an aircraft. They do show that investigation usually reduces, rather than enlarges, the unexplained residue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/GEP_UAP_Statistik.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufo-forschung.de.</span></span></span>

Aircraft explanations are most convincing when they reproduce several details at once: the correct time, viewing direction, brightness change, apparent hovering period, turn and disappearance. Merely noting that an airport is nearby is not enough. Conversely, a witness’s statement that an object was motionless, silent or shaped like a triangle does not rule out aviation, because those impressions can emerge naturally from viewing geometry and lighting.

## How flight records test a sighting

Aircraft reports are among the more testable UFO claims because aviation produces independent records. DFS offers STANLY Track, a radar-data service that displays aircraft movements and allows users to select airports and examine tracks over a chosen period. Airport arrival and departure records can provide scheduled or actual times, while weather data can establish wind direction, cloud level and the likely runway configuration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/en/environment/aircraft-noise/flight-paths-and-flight-procedures/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dfs.de.</span></span></span>

A useful reconstruction begins with the witness account rather than with a preferred explanation. Investigators need the exact time, location, viewing direction, elevation above the horizon, duration and sequence of changes. Even an error of ten minutes can attach the report to the wrong flight, while phrases such as “towards Cologne” may be too imprecise without a street-level position.

The strongest checks proceed in stages:

1. **Fix the observation window.** Establish whether the reported time came from a clock, a photograph, a message or later memory.
2. **Map the line of sight.** Determine the compass direction and identify any airport approach or departure sectors along it.
3. **Examine recorded tracks.** Look for aircraft whose bearing and altitude match the observation, not merely any aircraft in the wider area.
4. **Reconstruct the lighting angle.** A plane viewed from ahead should show strong landing lights; after turning, navigation and strobe lights should become more obvious.
5. **Compare the disappearance.** Check whether the candidate turned, descended behind terrain or buildings, entered cloud or switched off its landing lights.
6. **Test competing explanations.** Planets, [satellites]({{ 'satellites-a19e68/' | relative_url }}), [drones]({{ 'drones-d4ad56/' | relative_url }}) and reflections should still be considered where their timing and behaviour fit better.

Public flight-tracking websites are helpful but not infallible. They commonly use Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast, or ADS-B, in which equipped aircraft transmit position, altitude, speed and identity data. Some positions are also calculated by multilateration from signals received at several stations. Coverage can vary, and a small number of flights may have information limited or blocked. The absence of an aircraft from one consumer application therefore does not prove that no aircraft was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flightradar24.com/how-it-works/ads-b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flightradar24.com">[Flightradar24]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flightradar24.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Automatic Dependent Surveillance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Automatic Dependent Surveillance</span></span></span>

Photographs rarely settle the matter alone. Phone cameras enlarge bright points, introduce flare and lose faint structural detail. A row of lights may be stretched by camera movement, while digital zoom can turn an aircraft into a pulsing disc. Images become much more useful when their original timestamp, exposure information and unedited sequence survive.

## When the aircraft explanation is weak

Not every report near an airport should be dismissed automatically. An aircraft identification is weak when the proposed flight lies well outside the viewing direction, when its timing requires a large unexplained error, or when the recorded movement contradicts the witness’s account. Multiple observers at separated locations can also allow triangulation that rules out a distant approach light.

A genuinely strong unresolved aviation case would need more than an unusual description. It would ideally combine precise witness positions, original imagery, reliable timestamps, radar or air-traffic information and evidence that known aircraft, astronomical objects and other ordinary causes had been properly excluded. Publicly accessible NRW material rarely reaches that standard. Most online case notices are brief intake summaries rather than complete investigation files, and the full photographs, videos and analytical records are not generally available through the GEP’s public database.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-forschung.de/GEP_UAP_Statistik.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-forschung.de">[UFO Forschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-forschung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufo-forschung.de.</span></span></span>

That evidential gap should not be mistaken for proof of either an extraordinary craft or a definite misidentification. It usually means the case remains too poorly documented for a confident conclusion. Within North Rhine-Westphalia’s UFO history, the airport and flight-path pattern is therefore most valuable as a mechanism: it explains why sincere witnesses repeatedly report hovering, shape-changing or suddenly vanishing lights, and it supplies independent records capable of turning many initially puzzling observations into testable aviation events.
