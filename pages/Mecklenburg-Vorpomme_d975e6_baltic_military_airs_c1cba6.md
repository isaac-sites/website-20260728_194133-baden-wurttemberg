## Introduction

The [Greifswald lights]({{ 'greifswald-case/' | relative_url }}) of 24 August 1990 were seen above a coastline that was still an active military landscape. Fighter aircraft operated from Peenemünde, target-presentation units supported air-defence training, naval forces worked in the Baltic, and parts of the coast had only recently become more accessible to ordinary visitors. This setting does not prove that the lights were military flares, but it makes an exercise-related explanation far more plausible than accounts that treat the sky above the Greifswalder Bodden as an ordinary piece of civilian airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/YLKKDP3N34MD76KW3O2D4HUKDNU26VOM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de">[deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek Jagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Deutsche Digitale BibliothekJagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand) - Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek...</span></span></span>

The central difficulty is that no widely available operational document has been produced that straightforwardly states which unit released which devices at the relevant time. ## The former Baltic firing zone

The coast around Greifswald, Rügen and Usedom lay beside one of East Germany’s most militarised stretches of the Baltic. Peenemünde airfield, on northern Usedom, had housed Fighter Wing 9 of the East German National People’s Army since 1961. The unit operated MiG-21 and MiG-23 aircraft and worked within the East German air-defence system alongside Soviet forces stationed in the country. Its associated target-presentation unit provided airborne targets and training support, showing that the local military system involved more than routine departures and landings by fighters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/YLKKDP3N34MD76KW3O2D4HUKDNU26VOM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de">[Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek Jagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Deutsche Digitale BibliothekJagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand) - Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek...</span></span></span>

This matters because the witnesses were looking towards a region used for air-defence, aviation and maritime activity, not simply towards an empty horizon. Military operations over water are especially difficult for distant civilian observers to judge. A light several kilometres offshore may appear to hang above a town, power station or shoreline landmark when its actual position is much farther away. With no visible ground reference beneath it, estimates of height, speed and distance can vary enormously.

The term “firing zone” should not be understood as one permanently active, neatly bounded patch of sea whose every use was advertised publicly. The wider Baltic operating environment included naval training areas, air routes, coastal bases and restricted facilities controlled by different East German and Soviet organisations. East Germany’s navy was integrated into the Warsaw Pact’s combined Baltic forces, while its air-defence units operated in cooperation with the Soviet military presence. That institutional overlap complicates later attempts to identify a single responsible unit from civilian reports alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksmarine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Die Volksmarine war als Teil der Vereinigten Ostseeflotten in die Organisation des Warschauer Pakt es eingebunden.Ihr Operationsgebiet wa...</span></span></span>

Peenemünde’s history has sometimes encouraged more exotic speculation because of its earlier role in German rocket development. For the 1990 sighting, however, its relevant importance is more immediate: it remained a functioning military airfield until the final days of East Germany. Fighter Wing 9 held its last formal assembly on 2 October 1990, only five weeks after the Greifswald lights appeared, and was transferred into the Bundeswehr structure following reunification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/YLKKDP3N34MD76KW3O2D4HUKDNU26VOM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de">[Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek Jagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Deutsche Digitale BibliothekJagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand) - Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek...</span></span></span>

## Possible aircraft and illumination devices

The leading conventional explanation is that aircraft, ships or another military platform released groups of parachute-supported illumination devices during an exercise. These devices produce very bright points of light and descend slowly after deployment. At long range, the supporting parachutes, smoke and launching platform may be invisible, leaving only apparently stationary lights against the evening sky.

That mechanism fits several recurring details in the Greifswald recordings. Witnesses described groups of approximately seven lights, often in a grape-like arrangement. The lights were not rigidly fixed relative to one another but shifted gradually, as separate suspended objects exposed to wind would do. Some appeared to sink or fade rather than accelerate away, and the principal formations were accompanied by separate flashes that could be consistent with further launches or other pyrotechnic activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[GWUP e. Silence is not strong evidence against a military explanation. Aircraft or ships operating many kilometres away can be inaudible, particularly near a coast where wind, surf and ordinary background noise mask distant engines. Light travels to an observer effectively at once, whereas weak engine noise may dissipate or be carried away by the wind. The brightness of an illumination flare can therefore create a misleading impression that the object is nearby even when its source is beyond normal hearing range.

The repeated groupings also weaken the idea of a single structured craft. Video descriptions indicate that individual lights changed their spacing and formed new arrangements. That behaviour is easier to reconcile with several independently descending objects than with lamps mounted on a solid vehicle. There are still limits to this interpretation. Public accounts disagree over whether the lights moved horizontally, remained stationary or appeared in more than one place. Later retellings sometimes add rapid manoeuvres, radar detections or precise distances that are not equally well supported by the earliest accessible reports. The surviving videos document lights, but without reliable range, bearing and simultaneous radar data they cannot by themselves identify the launching platform or reconstruct a complete flight path.

The most defensible reading is therefore narrower than some debunking claims. The visual behaviour is highly compatible with military illumination devices, and the regional military setting makes their presence credible. What has not been publicly demonstrated beyond dispute is the exact aircraft, ship, unit, flare model and exercise order responsible for the display.

## Why the exercise was difficult to verify

The sighting occurred during an extraordinary collapse and transfer of authority. By August 1990, East Germany’s armed forces were still functioning, but their political future was unsettled. The Ministry for National Defence had become the Ministry for Disarmament and Defence, personnel were uncertain about their careers, and negotiations were under way over the fate of units, equipment and records. The Bundesarchiv notes that routine military service continued during this period despite deep institutional uncertainty; the army did not simply cease operating when the Berlin Wall fell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bundesarchiv.de/staat-militaer-und-gesellschaft/armee-im-umbruch/eine-zukunft-fuer-die-nva/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bundesarchiv.de">[Stasi-Unterlagen-Archiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bundesarchiv.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">eine zukunft fuer die nva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stasi-Unterlagen-ArchivEine Zukunft für die NVA?Oktober 1990 gab es Versuche, die NVA als Organisation. That combination helps explain why early enquiries could fail without implying a conspiracy. A local police office might not have been notified of an offshore exercise. An East German air unit might not have controlled a naval or Soviet operation. Military staff preparing for dissolution or transfer had little incentive to undertake a public UFO investigation, especially when responsibility crossed organisational boundaries.

The surviving archival picture is uneven. The Bundesarchiv holds a dedicated Fighter Wing 9 collection containing orders, training analyses, incident material and chronicles extending to 1990. Yet the catalogue also warns that some related political and administrative records are incomplete. The existence of a collection does not mean that every sortie, pyrotechnic issue or combined exercise can be located through a simple online search. Much of the material remains described at file level rather than digitised and searchable page by page.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/YLKKDP3N34MD76KW3O2D4HUKDNU26VOM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de">[Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek Jagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Deutsche Digitale BibliothekJagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand) - Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek...</span></span></span>

The sighting’s date also falls just before a sharp break in custody. Fighter Wing 9 ended as an East German unit on 2 October, and military sites, personnel and equipment passed into a new command structure on 3 October. The wider task of absorbing, securing or disposing of East German military assets was immense. In that process, a minor illumination exercise that had unexpectedly generated civilian reports would not necessarily have received special preservation or investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/YLKKDP3N34MD76KW3O2D4HUKDNU26VOM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de">[deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek Jagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Deutsche Digitale BibliothekJagdfliegergeschwader-9 (Bestand) - Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek...</span></span></span>

Investigators also faced a basic problem of geography. Witnesses were scattered along the coast and inland, and their statements used familiar landmarks rather than measured bearings. A light described as being “over Greifswald”, “over the Bodden” or “towards Usedom” could refer to the same distant event viewed along different sightlines. Without accurate observation positions and compass directions, attempts to ask a particular base or police district about activity could easily have been directed to the wrong authority.

## The information gap that sustained the mystery

The military-airspace explanation became more persuasive as investigators compared the footage with known illumination phenomena, but the delay allowed a different story to become established first. Newspaper coverage emphasised luminous discs, formation flight and the professional background of some witnesses. The image of seven lights floating above the Baltic was striking, simple and easy to reproduce; the institutional history of nearby military units was not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[GWUP e. V...</span></span></span>

This distinction is crucial. The witnesses almost certainly observed a real and unusual display. Calling it a probable flare event does not require assuming mass error, fabrication or poor eyesight. Their likely mistake concerned distance and interpretation: intensely bright, slowly descending objects released over a military operating area were perceived as nearby craft holding formation.

Later UFO-oriented reporting has continued to stress the number of witnesses, photographs and films, sometimes presenting the absence of an acknowledged exercise order as evidence that the case remains wholly unexplained. At least six films and several photographs have been claimed in later summaries, although exact totals and the independence of all recordings are not consistently documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.openpr.de/news/658020/Greifswald-UFO-Raetsel-seit-22-Jahren-ungeloest.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openpr.de">[openPR.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openpr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">open PR.de Greifswald UFO-Rätsel seit 22 Jahren ungelöst</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">open PR.de Greifswald UFO-Rätsel seit 22 Jahren ungelöst</span></span></span>

The lack of a definitive operational record is a legitimate evidential weakness, but it does not give every explanation equal weight. A hypothesis should be judged by how well it fits the observations and the local setting. Military illumination accounts for the brightness, duration, slow descent, loose formations and absence of audible engines while requiring only activities known to occur in the region. An extraordinary craft hypothesis has no comparable documentary, radar or physical evidence.

Within [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern]({{ 'mecklenburg-vorpommern/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history, the Baltic military setting is therefore not a side note to the Greifswald case. It is the main reason an impressive, widely recorded sighting could be both genuine and probably conventional. The final months of East Germany created a near-perfect information gap: military systems were still active, public access to their records remained poor, and the institutions capable of explaining the event were weeks away from reorganisation or disappearance. That gap strengthened the mystery at the time, even as the known character of the airspace later weakened the claim that the lights represented an unknown vehicle.
