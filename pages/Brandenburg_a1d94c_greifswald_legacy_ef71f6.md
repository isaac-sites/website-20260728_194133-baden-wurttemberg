## Introduction

The Greifswald lights were filmed on the Baltic coast on 24 August 1990, outside modern [Brandenburg]({{ 'why-brandenburgs-ufo-record-remains-so/' | relative_url }}), yet they became one of the most influential reference cases in eastern German UFO culture. Several groups of bright lights appeared in fixed-looking formations for roughly half an hour and were recorded from different locations. The footage proved that witnesses had seen a real luminous display, but not that they had filmed structured craft. Later investigations increasingly linked the spectacle to military illumination devices or aerial targets released during training over the Baltic Sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[GWUP e. V...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Brandenburg_a1d94c_greifswald_legacy_ef71f6-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Greifswald Lights" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

For Brandenburg, the incident matters less as a local sighting than as a model for interpreting later reports. It arrived at a moment when East German institutions were breaking apart, western newspapers and broadcasters were entering new audiences, and military activity was unusually difficult for civilians to reconstruct. The resulting combination — dramatic video, multiple witnesses, uncertain distances and incomplete records — helped establish the visual language through which unusual formations of lights would later be discussed across Brandenburg and the wider former East Germany.

## What the 1990 footage shows

On the evening of 24 August, witnesses along the coast between the Greifswald area, Usedom, Rügen and Rostock reported luminous points arranged in clusters. Accounts commonly describe one group as ring-like or grape-shaped and another as a cross, fork or Y formation. The lights appeared white, yellowish or reddish depending on the recording and viewing position. They remained visible long enough for witnesses to fetch cameras, stop vehicles and film several minutes of footage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[gwup.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">e. V.Das UFO-Phänomen von Greifswald</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GWUP e. V.Das UFO-Phänomen von Greifswald - Ein deutscher Klassiker &#124; Pseudo- und Parawissenschaften &#124; GWUP e. V...</span></span></span>

At least several recordings were eventually collected from different sites. This is the case’s strongest feature. It rules out a defect in one camera and makes a wholly invented story implausible. Some footage also shows individual lights fading gradually, while another recording appears to show the formation drifting with the wind. Later witnesses described smoke-like luminosity or small sparkling points near the main cluster. Those details are significant because they are compatible with pyrotechnic devices suspended beneath parachutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciences-faits-histoires.com/medias/files/ufo.-.ovni.-.the.greifswald.lights-.august.24-.1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciences-faits-histoires.com">[Sciences Faits Histoires]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciences-faits-histoires.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sciences Faits Histoires Untitled Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sciences Faits Histoires Untitled Document</span></span></span>

The films do **not**, however, provide reliable scale or range. They show bright points against a largely featureless sky, usually through consumer video cameras with limited resolution and zoom. Apparent circles, rings or dark edges can therefore reflect overexposure, defocus, atmospheric haze or the camera’s handling of an intense point source. Claims that the recordings demonstrate solid spheres many metres wide depend on disputed assumptions about distance, brightness and image processing rather than directly visible surfaces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciences-faits-histoires.com/medias/files/ufo.-.ovni.-.the.greifswald.lights-.august.24-.1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciences-faits-histoires.com">[Sciences Faits Histoires]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciences-faits-histoires.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sciences Faits Histoires Untitled Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sciences Faits Histoires Untitled Document</span></span></span>

Reports of rapid movement are similarly difficult to assess. Witnesses spoke of smaller lights approaching the formation, objects changing position and, in some retellings, extraordinary acceleration. Yet the best-known films mainly preserve slowly moving or nearly stationary lights. Without a fixed foreground, synchronised clocks and accurately surveyed camera positions, it is difficult to separate real motion from camera movement, perspective or successive pyrotechnic releases.

There is also no securely documented radar record in the publicly available case material. Later popular accounts sometimes add radar tracking, interceptions or official alarm, but the central published investigations are built around visual testimony, photographs and videotape. Treating Greifswald as a combined radar-and-visual incident therefore gives the evidence more weight than the surviving record warrants.

## Why military flares became the leading explanation

Early explanations included balloons, astronomical objects and atmospheric reflections. None fitted the complete pattern well. Stars and planets do not appear suddenly in multiple compact formations or fade one after another within minutes. Ordinary balloons would not naturally produce such intense, repeatable clusters. The coastal position and the signs of slow descent instead directed investigators towards military illumination devices.

The first widely circulated sceptical reconstruction proposed signal flares or illumination bombs associated with military activity over the Baltic. An appeal made during a 1994 television programme reportedly brought responses from viewers familiar with naval training lights, strengthening that line of inquiry. Werner Walter of the civilian investigation group CENAP subsequently argued that the display was produced by military pyrotechnics used as practice targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.daserste.de/information/wissen-kultur/w-wie-wissen/sendung/2008/von-ufos-und-ifos-102.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: daserste.de">[Das Erste]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">daserste.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Das Erste Von Ufos und Ifos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nach einem Aufruf im NDR 1994 wurde er für seine Hartnäckigkeit belohnt: Viele Zuschauer kannten die seltsamen Lichter: Es waren Leuchtra...</span></span></span>

A later investigation published by UFOinfo offered a more specific reconstruction. It identified Soviet-designed SAB illumination bombs, which could release a group of seven bright pyrotechnic units beneath parachutes, and connected the event to a Warsaw Pact training zone over the Baltic. The researchers attributed the operation to the Czechoslovak air force, which was permitted to use the offshore range. Seven released lights would naturally account for the repeated clustered appearance that made the footage seem technological or deliberately geometric.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinfo.de/index.php/themen/faelle/greifswald" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinfo.de">[UFO Info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinfo.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Info Die Greifswald UFO Lichter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO InfoDie Greifswald UFO Lichter - ufoinfo.de...</span></span></span>

This explanation also deals with the duration objection. Critics had argued that a single flare would not burn for the full period reported by witnesses. But the sighting need not represent one uninterrupted set of devices. Successive releases, overlapping burn times and two distinct groups could extend the overall display while individual lights faded much sooner. The films’ gradual extinctions and apparent reappearance in a nearby position fit repeated deployment more readily than a single formation of hovering craft.

The flare case is persuasive rather than forensically complete. No publicly accessible firing log has emerged that lists the precise aircraft, time, munition serial number and release coordinates. The identification was reconstructed from witness geography, military range information, the number and behaviour of the lights, testimony about training devices and later comparison footage. That leaves room to dispute particular operational details, but it does not place an extraterrestrial explanation on equal evidential footing.

## Why the case spread beyond Mecklenburg

East German agencies were being reorganised or dissolved, Soviet and allied forces remained present, and records or chains of responsibility were difficult for civilian investigators to trace. ## How distance confused the witness geography

The Greifswald case illustrates how a distant light display can appear to belong to several places at once. Witnesses were distributed across a broad coastal and inland area, yet they did not necessarily observe objects directly above their own towns. Bright lights released at altitude over the Baltic could be visible from widely separated viewpoints, especially in clear evening conditions.

Some case accounts claimed visibility from Neubrandenburg, roughly 100 kilometres from parts of the coastal observation area. Other recordings were made from beaches, islands and roads tens of kilometres apart.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciences-faits-histoires.com/medias/files/ufo.-.ovni.-.the.greifswald.lights-.august.24-.1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciences-faits-histoires.com">[Sciences Faits Histoires]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciences-faits-histoires.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sciences Faits Histoires Untitled Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sciences Faits Histoires Untitled Document</span></span></span> Such separation is useful only when directions, camera locations and timing are accurately known. Otherwise, witnesses may each describe the phenomenon as “over” the nearest familiar landmark — Greifswald, Usedom, Rügen, a nuclear plant or the sea — even though the actual source is much farther away.

This is particularly relevant to Brandenburg’s UFO record. A conspicuous light low in the northern or north-eastern sky can be assigned intuitively to a nearby town, airfield or forest when it may originate beyond the state boundary. Distance at night is hard to judge because lights provide no familiar measure of size. An object assumed to be a few kilometres away may instead be a powerful flare or aircraft light tens of kilometres distant.

The same error affects reports of movement. A descending flare viewed head-on can appear to hover. Wind drift can look like controlled sideways flight. When one light extinguishes and another is released nearby, the observer may perceive a single object making a sudden jump. A group suspended at slightly different ranges can also seem to rotate as the aircraft, wind and viewing angle change.

Greifswald therefore cautions against drawing a state boundary around a sighting too quickly. A Brandenburg witness report may have a cause in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, Berlin, Poland or a military training area farther away. Conversely, lights generated over Brandenburg can produce reports in neighbouring states. Regional analysis is strongest when it compares times and bearings across jurisdictions rather than counting every account according to the witness’s home district.

## The Brandenburg legacy

The Greifswald lights gave eastern German audiences a memorable template: several bright points, a geometric arrangement, prolonged visibility, multiple witnesses and video that looked impressive but lacked distance data. That template continues to influence how unusual light formations are described. Reports are more likely to be treated as one large craft when the lights maintain relative positions, even though separate flares, aircraft or lanterns can create the same impression.

Its legacy in Brandenburg is therefore methodological as much as cultural. The case demonstrates several questions that should be asked before declaring a regional light formation unexplained:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Were the lights independently moving objects, or separate sources released together?
* Did witnesses record compass directions, or merely say the objects were “over” a place?
* Can the accounts be matched with military exercises, aircraft routes or distant pyrotechnics?
* Do reported extraordinary movements appear in the original footage, or only in later testimony?
* Is a claimed radar confirmation supported by an actual record?
* Does the total duration describe one light, or a sequence of lights?

</div>

Greifswald also shows why video is not automatically conclusive evidence. Multiple recordings establish that a display occurred, but a bright point in a dark sky contains little information about shape, size or distance. Modern analysis would seek synchronised recordings, exact GPS locations, uncompressed files, weather data and triangulation from calibrated cameras. The absence of these measurements in 1990 allowed both advocates and sceptics to build very different reconstructions from the same images.

Later reporting has generally weakened the claim that Greifswald captured unknown craft. The identification of a suitable multi-flare military device, the offshore training-zone context and comparison with similar pyrotechnic displays provide a coherent conventional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinfo.de/index.php/themen/faelle/greifswald" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinfo.de">[UFO Info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinfo.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Info Die Greifswald UFO Lichter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO InfoDie Greifswald UFO Lichter - ufoinfo.de...</span></span></span> Continued disagreement mainly concerns whether investigators proved the exact exercise beyond all doubt, not whether the videos themselves demonstrate alien technology.

The most balanced classification is therefore **probably explained, with incomplete operational documentation**. The lights were real, widely observed and historically important. Their enduring value to Brandenburg’s UFO history lies not in evidence of extraordinary vehicles, but in showing how military activity, uncertain distance, transitional politics and powerful television images can turn a coastal illumination exercise into a defining eastern German UFO legend.
