## Introduction

In [Schleswig-Holstein]({{ 'what-is-really-seen-over-schleswig/' | relative_url }}), some of the most alarming reports of unknown objects in the sky have turned out to involve ordinary aircraft, helicopters or legally operated drones. The clearest official example came after the wave of suspected drone sightings on 26 September 2025. Police received reports from Dithmarschen, Rendsburg-Eckernförde and Kiel, including areas near military sites and critical infrastructure. Within days, the state government said that many reports had been explained and excluded as illegal drone incursions: the objects included aeroplanes, helicopters and lawful drones. Other reports remained unverified, while the authorities still believed that some genuinely unauthorised drone flights may have occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

## Approaching aircraft that appear stationary

An aircraft flying almost directly towards an observer can remain in roughly the same part of the sky for several minutes. Its landing lights may grow steadily brighter while its sideways movement remains too small to notice. To a witness on the ground, it can look like a brilliant object hovering at a fixed altitude before suddenly moving away when the aircraft turns.

The effect is strongest at night because the observer sees lights rather than the aircraft’s body. Distance becomes difficult to estimate, and there may be no nearby object against which to judge motion. Research into night approaches has shown how greatly reduced visual references weaken motion and height perception. Even trained pilots can misjudge angles when normal depth cues and relative motion are missing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/data_research/research/med_humanfacs/oamtechreports/1970s/1978/197815" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on faa.gov.</span></span></span>

A distant airliner can consequently be described as a silent white orb. When it turns, the witness may suddenly see red, green or flashing white navigation lights and interpret the change as a transformation or abrupt acceleration. In reality, the viewing angle has changed and revealed lighting that was previously hidden.

Aircraft can also seem to move in ways that do not fit their apparent size. A witness who assumes that a bright light is small and nearby will judge its speed very differently from someone who recognises it as a large aircraft many kilometres away. The initial distance estimate shapes the whole account: a slow turn by a distant aeroplane may be remembered as a small object making an extraordinary high-speed manoeuvre.

The September 2025 reports show why investigators must check conventional traffic before treating a cluster as evidence of a single incursion. ## Helicopters hidden by haze and wind

Helicopters are especially effective UFO lookalikes because they can hover, move sideways, circle repeatedly and operate at comparatively low altitude. Their searchlights and landing lights may dominate the view while the cabin and rotor disc remain invisible. Depending on the observer’s position, the result can resemble a stationary luminous object rather than a recognisable aircraft.

Sound is less reliable than many witnesses assume. Helicopter noise is directional and changes with the aircraft’s flight condition. Tests by the German Aerospace Center found that a helicopter in forward flight tends to radiate more noise ahead of itself. An observer behind or to one side may therefore see the helicopter before hearing it clearly, or may experience large changes in volume as the aircraft alters course.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dlr.de/en/latest/news/2022/01/20220120_quieter-helicopters-for-greater-operational-safety" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dlr.de">[DLR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dlr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">– Quieter helicopters for greater operational safety</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DLR – Quieter helicopters for greater operational safety...</span></span></span>

Wind further separates apparent position from audible position. Sound may be carried towards one neighbourhood and away from another. Coastal wind, sea haze, cloud layers and background noise from roads, ships or industry can make a nearby helicopter seem faint or completely silent. Conversely, a helicopter that is no longer visible may remain audible, encouraging the impression that the sound and light came from separate objects.

Schleswig-Holstein has several reasons for legitimate helicopter activity. Rescue aircraft conduct emergency missions, patient transfers and cross-border operations, while police, military and maritime services may fly routes unfamiliar to the general public. Weather services provide specialist forecasts for police, rescue and offshore helicopter operations, reflecting the region’s mixture of emergency aviation, coastal activity and offshore industry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dwd.de/EN/ourservices/aviation_lf_27_police_and_emergency_services/lf_27_police_and_emergency%20services.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dwd.de">[DWD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dwd.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wetter und Klima</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">* Strong and gusty wind (turbulence)... Special products, such as night-flight forecasts for night-vision goggles, convection forecasts...</span></span></span>

Rescue coverage has also expanded. The Christoph 67 air-rescue base at the Itzehoe/Hungriger Wolf airfield entered service in July 2024 to serve south-western Schleswig-Holstein and support intensive-care transfers. Such flights may involve direct routes, hospital approaches and operations at times or places where residents do not normally expect air traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.airmedandrescue.com/latest/news/adac-luftrettung-opens-new-air-rescue-base-schleswig-holstein" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airmedandrescue.com">[AirMed&amp;Rescue]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airmedandrescue.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">adac luftrettung opens new air rescue base schleswig holstein</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">adac luftrettung opens new air rescue base schleswig holstein</span></span></span>

None of this means that every unexplained hovering light is a helicopter. It does mean that the absence of an obvious rotor shape or immediate engine noise is weak evidence against one. A useful investigation requires the exact time, direction, duration, weather and movement pattern, followed by checks against known rescue, police, military and civilian activity.

## Lawful drones with unfamiliar lighting

A drone can be both correctly recognised as a drone and wrongly treated as an illegal or hostile aircraft. The category includes recreational models, commercial survey platforms, photography drones, inspection systems, research aircraft and public-service equipment. Their presence near an industrial landscape or transport corridor may look suspicious even when the operator has permission.

German airspace rules do not impose a universal ban on drone flying. Instead, operations depend on the aircraft, location, category of flight and applicable geographical zone. Airports, aerodromes, control zones, residential property, industrial sites, hospitals and nature reserves can have restrictions or approval requirements. A flight that would be unlawful without permission may be legitimate when the operator has obtained the necessary consent or clearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/en/drone-flight/faq-for-drone-flights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dfs.de.</span></span></span>

Schleswig-Holstein’s aviation authority handles permissions, exemptions and enquiries concerning unmanned aircraft within the state. This administrative framework matters because a witness cannot normally tell from the ground whether a visible drone has been authorised.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/LBVSH/Aufgaben/Luftfahrtbehoerde/Unbemannte_Luftfahrtgeraete/03_Drohnen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">03 Drohnen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">03 Drohnen</span></span></span>

Night lighting can add to the confusion. European rules require drones operating at night in relevant categories to carry a green flashing light so that a person on the ground can distinguish them from crewed aircraft. Other manufacturer-installed position lights, status indicators and powerful forward lights can produce combinations unfamiliar to observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/123576/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: easa.europa.eu">[EASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">easa.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on europa.eu.</span></span></span>

A lawful drone may therefore display several characteristics often treated as suspicious in UFO reports:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* hovering for long periods;
* repeated movement along the same line;
* sudden stops and direction changes;
* green, red, white or flashing illumination;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/CRD%202018-10%20.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: easa.europa.eu">[easa.europa.eu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">easa.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CRD</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CRD</span></span></span>
* little audible noise at a distance;
* operation near a work site, field, harbour or public event;
* disappearance when its lights turn away from the observer or when it descends behind buildings.

</div>

Small drones are particularly difficult to judge against a dark sky. Their apparent speed depends heavily on estimated distance. A nearby drone moving at modest speed can look like a distant object crossing an enormous section of sky. A larger industrial drone farther away may be mistaken for a helicopter or aeroplane.

## Why one night can produce several explanations

Sightings reported close together in time are often grouped into a single “wave”. That can be misleading. Once news of unusual drones or aircraft spreads, more people begin watching the sky. They notice ordinary traffic that would otherwise have passed without comment, and reports multiply around the same narrative.

This does not require witnesses to be careless or dishonest. Expectation affects classification. A person who has just heard that drones are flying over critical infrastructure is more likely to interpret a distant light as another drone. Social-media descriptions can also create an informal template: hovering, silent, flashing, moving in formation or vanishing suddenly. Later witnesses may use the same language for objects that are unrelated.

The late-September 2025 reports illustrate this mixed-event problem unusually well. Objects of different types and sizes were reported across several areas. Police investigations identified conventional aircraft, helicopters and authorised drones among them. Some reports could not be verified, yet officials also considered illegal flights plausible. The most defensible conclusion is not that the entire episode was either imaginary or hostile. It was an untidy combination of explained observations, weak observations and potentially genuine incursions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

The earlier Brunsbüttel incidents of August 2024 should be kept distinct. Those reports concerned repeated suspected drone flights around an industrial park, a planned liquefied natural gas terminal and a former nuclear power station. The state mounted a substantial police operation and involved specialist authorities because the location and repeated nature of the reports raised real security questions. The fact that conventional aircraft explained some sightings in 2025 does not retroactively settle the Brunsbüttel investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2024/240904_IuR_Drohnen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">240904 IuR Drohnen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">240904 IuR Drohnen</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to balanced UFO analysis. Demonstrating that misidentifications occur frequently does not prove that every report is mistaken. Equally, finding that one unauthorised drone probably flew in an area does not validate every light reported that night.

## How investigators separate a mix-up from a real incursion

Visual appearance alone is rarely enough. Investigators reconstruct the circumstances and compare independent records. The most useful questions are practical rather than speculative.

**Did the object follow a recognisable approach or circuit?** A light that remains stationary, grows brighter and then reveals navigation lights is a strong candidate for an approaching aircraft.

**Was hovering followed by a slow turn or repeated orbit?** That pattern may fit a rescue, police or military helicopter, especially near an emergency scene, hospital, coast or training area.

**Did the object show a regular green flash?** That can support identification as a drone operating under night-lighting requirements, although the light alone cannot establish whether the flight was authorised.

**Was the observer looking across water or open farmland?** Missing distance references greatly reduce confidence in claims about size, speed and altitude.

**Were several witnesses genuinely independent?** Ten accounts copied from one social-media post are not equivalent to ten people who recorded the same object from separated locations.

**Do photographs preserve useful detail?** A bright point enlarged digitally may reveal only camera shake, autofocus changes and sensor artefacts. Original files, exposure information and a known viewing direction are much more valuable than compressed online clips.

**What aviation and airspace records exist?** DFS, Germany’s air-navigation service provider, makes radar-based aircraft movement information available through its STANLY Track system. Drone operators are also expected to consult official geographical-zone information before flying, and operations near airports or within control zones can require permission and air-traffic-control clearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/en/environment/aircraft-noise/flight-paths-and-flight-procedures/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dfs.de.</span></span></span>

Public flight-tracking applications can be useful, but a missing symbol on a consumer app is not proof that no aircraft was present. Police, rescue, military and some general-aviation flights may be absent, delayed, filtered or incompletely displayed. The absence of a match should therefore keep a case open for further checking rather than convert it into evidence of extraordinary technology.

## What the mix-ups change in Schleswig-Holstein’s UFO record

Aircraft, helicopters and lawful drones are not merely generic alternatives added at the end of an investigation. In Schleswig-Holstein, they are demonstrated components of at least one major modern sighting cluster. The state government’s 2025 assessment established that reports initially discussed in the context of espionage, sabotage and critical infrastructure included several forms of ordinary aviation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/DE/landesregierung/ministerien-behoerden/IV/Presse/PI/2025/4_Quartal/251001_Rede_InnenRechtsausschuss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: schleswig-holstein.de">[Schleswig-Holstein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">schleswig-holstein.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">251001 Rede InnenRechtsausschuss</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministerium für Inneres, Kommunales, Wohnen und Sport - Bericht von Schleswig-Holsteins Innenministerin Sabine Sütterlin-Waack im Innen...</span></span></span>

That finding weakens claims that every object in the cluster represented a coordinated unidentified operation. It also shows why early news coverage should be read cautiously. Initial reports record what witnesses and authorities feared might be happening; later statements may separate the reports into very different categories.

The wider lesson is more precise than “people mistake planes for UFOs”. Identification fails through particular mechanisms:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an approaching aircraft loses obvious sideways motion;
* forward lights hide the aircraft’s structure;
* wind and directional noise make a helicopter seem silent;
* haze obscures rotors and fuselages;
* drone lighting looks unlike familiar aircraft lighting;
* distance errors exaggerate speed and manoeuvrability;
* publicity causes unrelated objects to be reported as part of one event.

</div>

Cases resolved through these mechanisms belong in Schleswig-Holstein’s UFO history because they reveal how the record is formed. They demonstrate that a serious investigation can begin with an alarming report and end with several different answers. The unexplained remainder must then be judged on its own evidence, not strengthened by counting the aeroplanes, helicopters and lawful drones that have already been removed from the case.
