## Introduction

North Rhine-Westphalia produces Germany’s highest **raw** total of UFO reports mainly because it has the country’s largest population, a dense network of cities and heavily used airspace. Millions of people can observe the same satellite pass, bright planet, aircraft approach or rocket-related display from different towns, then submit separate accounts of what was physically one event. Modern phones and rapid local-media coverage make reporting easier and can prompt additional witnesses to check the sky or review photographs.

The figures therefore describe the number of reports received, not the number of extraordinary objects proved to exist. In 2024, CENAP, a private German UFO reporting centre, attributed 177 reports to North Rhine-Westphalia, ahead of Baden-Württemberg, Hesse and Bavaria. Yet most reports nationally were identified as [satellites]({{ 'satellites-a19e68/' | relative_url }}), astronomical objects, aircraft, balloons, [drones]({{ 'drones-d4ad56/' | relative_url }}), meteors or camera artefacts. NRW’s lead is best understood as the product of exposure, technology and reporting behaviour rather than evidence that the state experiences uniquely anomalous activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tvo.de/ufo-alarm-in-oberfranken-acht-mysterioese-sichtungen-in-2024-was-steckt-dahinter-747248/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tvo.de">[tvo.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tvo.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O-Alarm in Oberfranken: Acht mysteriöse Sichtungen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO-Alarm in Oberfranken: Acht mysteriöse Sichtungen...March 5, 2025 — 5 Mar 2025 — 130 UFO-Sichtungen in 2024 … sten gab es unerklärlic...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 5, 2025</span></span></span>

## Population and shared urban skies

North Rhine-Westphalia had about 18.2 million residents at the end of 2023, substantially more than Bavaria’s 13.4 million and Baden-Württemberg’s 11.3 million. More residents mean more potential observers, more phones pointed towards the sky and more opportunities for an unfamiliar light to be reported. The state’s population is also concentrated in adjoining urban areas rather than dispersed evenly across a large rural territory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.destatis.de/EN/Themes/Society-Environment/Population/Current-Population/Tables/population-by-laender.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: destatis.de">[Destatis]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">destatis.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Population by nationaly and federal states</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Germany &#124; 84,669,326 &#124; 71,761,258 &#124; 12,908,068 &#124; 15.2 &#124; 4,623,824 &#124; 35.8...</span></span></span>

This geography matters. The Rhine-Ruhr region contains a near-continuous chain of cities including Cologne, Düsseldorf, Duisburg, Essen, Bochum and Dortmund. A bright object visible over a wide area may generate accounts from several municipalities, each written from a different viewing angle. Witnesses may describe different directions, colours or apparent movements even when they are watching the same satellite formation, meteor or distant aircraft.

That multiplication effect can make a routine aerospace event look like a regional “flap”. Ten reports do not necessarily mean ten separate objects. They may represent ten people independently reacting to one conspicuous event. Investigators therefore compare timestamps, locations and directions before treating reports as separate cases.

The 2024 figures also show why raw totals should be interpreted cautiously. NRW recorded approximately 9.7 reports per million residents. Using the reported state totals and official population figures, Baden-Württemberg’s rate was about 13.4 per million and Hesse’s about 21.8 per million. Bavaria’s rate, at roughly 9.7 per million, was similar to NRW’s. NRW led because of its overall volume, but it was not the clear leader after population was taken into account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tvo.de/ufo-alarm-in-oberfranken-acht-mysterioese-sichtungen-in-2024-was-steckt-dahinter-747248/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tvo.de">[tvo.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tvo.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O-Alarm in Oberfranken: Acht mysteriöse Sichtungen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO-Alarm in Oberfranken: Acht mysteriöse Sichtungen...March 5, 2025 — 5 Mar 2025 — 130 UFO-Sichtungen in 2024 … sten gab es unerklärlic...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 5, 2025</span></span></span>

These comparisons are only approximate. CENAP is a voluntary reporting service rather than a compulsory national register, so the numbers depend on who knows about it, who chooses to make contact and how multiple reports are grouped. Even so, the per-capita comparison weakens the idea that the raw NRW total identifies an exceptional concentration of unexplained phenomena.

## A crowded and easily misread airspace

Residents of NRW look into a sky containing frequent civil aviation. Düsseldorf and Cologne Bonn are major airports, while Dortmund, Münster/Osnabrück, Paderborn/Lippstadt and airports just beyond the state add further traffic. Germany as a whole handled nearly three million controlled flights in 2024, and Düsseldorf and Cologne Bonn lie within one of Europe’s most densely connected population and transport regions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dfs.de/homepage/en/media/press/2025/13-01-2025-dfs-looks-back-positively-on-2024-punctuality-improves/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">13 01 2025 dfs looks back positively on 2024 punctuality improves</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The busiest day in German airspace in 2024 was 15 July, the day after the UEFA European Football Championship.... Aircraft are not always recognisable as aircraft. A jet flying towards an observer may appear almost stationary because its forward movement produces little visible sideways motion. Landing lights can dominate the view while the navigation lights and outline remain invisible. Thin cloud, haze and atmospheric distortion can make the light pulse, change colour or appear to divide into several points.

Urban surroundings add further ambiguity. Buildings hide the horizon, leaving observers without a clear reference for distance or speed. Reflections in windows can appear superimposed on the sky. Searchlights, event lighting and illuminated balloons may be seen from kilometres away but not from their source. A person watching for a minute from a balcony may therefore have less information than the apparent clarity of the experience suggests.

Helicopters and drones create another expanding category. CENAP has reported a continuing increase in cases identified as drones, including private, industrial and police operations. Media attention surrounding suspected surveillance drones has also made observers more alert to unfamiliar lights, encouraging them to record and report objects that might previously have been ignored.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/article695becd2fb77630dac278675" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTUFO-Meldestelle verzeichnet Rekordzahl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Laut Cenap-Leiter Hansjürgen Köhler sind die jährlichen Meldungen seit 2019 stetig angestiegen. This does not mean every report near an airport can simply be labelled an aircraft or drone. A credible identification requires consistency between the reported time, bearing, appearance and known traffic. The point is that NRW offers many ordinary candidates to test before an unusual explanation becomes necessary.

## Satellites changed what witnesses see

The most important recent change in German UFO reporting has come from space rather than from alleged visitors. Large satellite constellations have placed unfamiliar, highly visible patterns into the evening and morning sky. Newly launched Starlink satellites can appear as a line of evenly spaced lights, often described as a glowing chain or formation moving silently in perfect order.

CENAP identified Starlink as a leading cause of Germany’s record reporting in 2024. In 2025 it received more than 120 Starlink-related reports, including some from pilots surprised by the appearance during night flights. Bright planets, stars, meteors, rocket stages and re-entering space debris produced many other submissions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/255086178" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Insgesamt wurden 1.084 Sichtungen aus Deutschland, Österreich und der Schweiz gemeldet, verglichen mit dem üblichen Durchschnitt von 600...</span></span></span>

NRW does not necessarily receive more satellite passes per person than every other state. Its advantage in raw reporting comes from the number of observers who may see each pass. A satellite train crossing the Rhine-Ruhr sky can be visible to residents across a wide urban belt. One launch can therefore create numerous independent reports within minutes.

Rocket activity can be even harder to recognise. Venting fuel at high altitude may form a luminous cloud or spiral because sunlight still reaches the object after the ground below has entered darkness. Re-entering hardware can break into several glowing fragments travelling together. Without advance knowledge of the launch or re-entry, such displays can look structured, controlled and unlike familiar aircraft.

Astronomical objects remain equally persistent sources of confusion. Venus, Jupiter and Sirius can appear exceptionally bright, particularly through broken cloud or near the horizon. Autocomplete and digital zoom on phones may then produce enlarged, unstable blobs that seem to change shape. CENAP’s investigators have also identified apparent objects in submitted images as lens reflections or nearby insects and birds rendered out of focus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/255086178" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Insgesamt wurden 1.084 Sichtungen aus Deutschland, Österreich und der Schweiz gemeldet, verglichen mit dem üblichen Durchschnitt von 600...</span></span></span>

## Phones increase reports, not necessarily evidence

Smartphones have made it easier to preserve a sighting, but they have also increased the number of misleading images. A phone camera designed for ordinary daylight photography struggles with a small light against a dark sky. Digital zoom enlarges pixels rather than revealing reliable detail, autofocus repeatedly hunts for a subject, and image stabilisation can make a stationary point seem to jump or wobble.

This creates a paradox. Modern reports are more likely to include photographs or video, yet the files may contain less measurable information than witnesses expect. A bright, featureless spot usually provides no dependable distance, size or speed. Without stars, buildings or a known horizon in the frame, investigators may be unable to establish even the direction of view.

Camera artefacts can also become reports in their own right. Internal lens reflections may reproduce the shape or motion of a bright streetlight outside the frame. Insects close to the lens may appear as blurred discs or rods. Movement caused by the observer’s hands can be mistaken for rapid manoeuvring by the object. CENAP says that many strange points in submitted photographs have been resolved through precisely these ordinary photographic effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/255086178" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Insgesamt wurden 1.084 Sichtungen aus Deutschland, Österreich und der Schweiz gemeldet, verglichen mit dem üblichen Durchschnitt von 600...</span></span></span>

Phone ownership nevertheless improves investigation when witnesses preserve the original file and provide an exact time and location. Metadata, surrounding landmarks and a continuous recording can help match an observation with a satellite pass, flight path or astronomical object. The technology produces both more evidence and more noise; its value depends on the quality and context of the recording.

## Media attention can turn one event into a wave

Reporting totals are influenced by attention as well as observation. When local news sites publish a striking photograph or ask whether readers saw the same object, people who would otherwise dismiss a light may submit their own accounts. Others search recent phone images and notice reflections or blurred objects only after the subject has entered the news.

This feedback effect can create a temporary cluster without any increase in genuinely unusual activity. One widely visible satellite train may generate initial calls, news coverage, social-media discussion and a second round of reports. Accounts posted online can also shape later descriptions: witnesses may begin using the same words for colour, shape or movement even when their original impressions differed.

The same process operates around drone scares. CENAP’s 2025 summary linked increased observer attention partly to press reports about suspected espionage flights. That does not prove that every drone report was mistaken, but it demonstrates how the public meaning attached to an object affects whether it is noticed, filmed and reported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/article695becd2fb77630dac278675" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTUFO-Meldestelle verzeichnet Rekordzahl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Laut Cenap-Leiter Hansjürgen Köhler sind die jährlichen Meldungen seit 2019 stetig angestiegen. ## Why totals do not equal stronger evidence

The central distinction is between a **report** and a **case supported by strong evidence**. A report records that someone saw or photographed something they could not identify. It does not establish that the object remained unidentified after examination, still less that it displayed technology beyond known human capabilities.

High-quality cases require more than volume. Investigators need exact times, reliable locations, directions of travel, duration, angular size and original recordings. Independent witnesses are valuable when their accounts genuinely add different information, but a hundred impressions of the same distant light do not automatically provide distance, speed or physical structure.

This is why many reports can be resolved by comparison with external data. CENAP checks astronomical positions, satellite and International Space Station passes, rocket activity and, where relevant, aircraft or helicopter movements. Its archive provides examples of recurring visual effects against which new material can be compared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/255086178" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Insgesamt wurden 1.084 Sichtungen aus Deutschland, Österreich und der Schweiz gemeldet, verglichen mit dem üblichen Durchschnitt von 600...</span></span></span>

A minority of reports may remain [unresolved]({{ 'unresolved-843c8a/' | relative_url }}), but “unresolved” commonly means that the available data are insufficient. CENAP told SWR that 108 cases accumulated over more than five decades remained on its cold-case list, while stressing that this did not amount to proof of extraterrestrial visitation. Missing times, uncertain directions or poor images can prevent both an ordinary identification and an extraordinary conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swr.de/swraktuell/baden-wuerttemberg/rekord-ufo-meldungen-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swr.de">[SWR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rekord ufo meldungen 100</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">rekord ufo meldungen 100</span></span></span>

There are also limits to the statistics themselves. CENAP is a private organisation with its own intake methods and classifications, not Germany’s official census of every unusual aerial observation. Other civilian groups may receive different reports or assess cases differently. Critics have challenged aspects of CENAP’s approach, so its conclusions should not be treated as government determinations or as the only possible interpretation of disputed cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sueddeutsche.de/wissen/cenap-rekordzahl-ufo-sichtungen-usa-musk-li.3180445" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sueddeutsche.de">[Süddeutsche.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sueddeutsche.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cenap rekordzahl ufo sichtungen usa musk li.3180445</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">cenap rekordzahl ufo sichtungen usa musk li.3180445</span></span></span>

Even with those qualifications, the overall pattern is consistent. NRW’s leading total is readily explained by its population, connected urban geography, busy visible airspace, widespread phone use and exposure to satellite and drone phenomena. The same conditions that generate large numbers of reports also supply investigators with many ordinary explanations.

## The more useful way to read NRW’s ranking

NRW’s position at the top of the table is significant, but not because it points to a proven hotspot of extraordinary craft. It shows how strongly UFO statistics are shaped by the number and distribution of observers. A populous state will usually produce more reports unless reporting rates are unusually low.

Three questions are therefore more useful than the raw ranking:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **How many reports were made per resident?** In 2024, NRW’s approximate rate was lower than those of Hesse and Baden-Württemberg despite having the largest total.
* **How many reports came from the same event?** A satellite train, meteor or rocket display can generate a multi-city cluster.
* **What remained after investigation?** Identified aircraft, satellites and camera artefacts should not be counted as continuing evidence of an unexplained phenomenon.

</div>

Read this way, NRW is not merely Germany’s most reported state. It is the clearest German example of how dense population, modern aerospace activity and rapid communication can turn an ordinary but unfamiliar sight into a large statistical footprint. The high total measures how often people encounter uncertainty in a crowded sky; it does not, by itself, show that the underlying objects are more unusual than those reported elsewhere.
