## Introduction

From Lützelbach in the Odenwald, the Central Research Network for Extraordinary Aerial Phenomena, better known as CENAP, performs an unusual national role. It receives reports of strange lights and objects from across Germany and neighbouring countries, then tries to match them with ordinary causes such as planets, [satellites]({{ 'satellites/' | relative_url }}), aircraft, balloons, drones, meteors and event lighting. It is a private volunteer operation, not an official German [investigation]({{ 'investigation-629971/' | relative_url }}) agency or academic research institute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hfv-ev.de/ufos-ueber-dem-odenwald/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hfv-ev.de">[hfv-ev.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hfv-ev.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF Os über dem Odenwald?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs über dem Odenwald?December 6, 2022 — Seit den 90er Jahren betreibt Köhler die weit über Deutschland hinaus bekannte UFO-Meldestelle...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2022</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Hesse_19bb21_cenap_reporting_cent_223f86-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for CENAP" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

CENAP matters to [Hesse]({{ 'why-hesses-ufo-story-is-mostly-about/' | relative_url }}) because the state is not merely a source of sightings: it is also where many German reports are interpreted and publicly explained. The organisation has helped establish a strongly sceptical model of civilian UFO investigation in which “unidentified” describes a witness’s initial uncertainty, not evidence of an extraterrestrial craft. Its success in identifying recurring visual effects has made it highly visible, but its confidence, informal structure and sometimes combative public style have also divided German UFO researchers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrales_Erforschungsnetz_au%C3%9Fergew%C3%B6hnlicher_Himmelsph%C3%A4nomene" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span></span></span>

## From Mannheim to Lützelbach

CENAP grew out of the personal experiences of Hansjürgen Köhler and Werner Walter. The two began investigating unusual aerial reports in the Mannheim area during the 1970s, after Walter had experienced a sighting that he initially found difficult to explain. Their early group developed into CENAP in 1976, although some modern accounts use 1973—the year of the formative sighting and earliest organising activity—as its starting point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrales_Erforschungsnetz_au%C3%9Fergew%C3%B6hnlicher_Himmelsph%C3%A4nomene" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span></span></span>

That origin is important because CENAP was not founded by people who simply wished to ridicule UFO witnesses. Its founders were initially attracted by the possibility that some reports might represent something genuinely extraordinary. Repeated investigation moved them towards a different conclusion: the same kinds of familiar objects and perceptual mistakes appeared again and again, while persuasive evidence for alien vehicles did not. Walter eventually became one of Germany’s best-known critics of extraterrestrial interpretations of UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Walter_%28Ufologe%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Werner Walter (Ufologe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Werner Walter (Ufologe</span></span></span>

For decades, the public face of the organisation was closely connected with Mannheim and Walter’s telephone hotline. After Walter withdrew from active work in 2014 and died in 2016, Köhler increasingly carried the reporting service forward. The centre was subsequently operated from his home in Breitenbrunn, part of the municipality of Lützelbach in southern Hesse. A call or email concerning a puzzling light seen hundreds of kilometres away could therefore end up being assessed in a small Odenwald community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fr.de/rhein-main/darmstadt/ufos-deutschland-cenap-ufo-meldestelle-melden-hunderte-sichtungen-keine-ausserirdischen-91257283.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fr.de">[FR.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufo-Meldestelle im Odenwald verzeichnet Hunderte...26 Jan 2022 — Im Schnitt gehen jährlich 300 bis 400 Meldungen aus dem deutschsprachig...</span></span></span>

This move gave Hesse a distinctive place in German UFO history. Lützelbach is not a government monitoring station filled with radar screens, laboratories or a large investigative staff. It is the base of a small, volunteer-led reporting network whose influence comes from experience, accumulated comparison material, accessibility to witnesses and frequent contact with journalists. CENAP’s national profile is consequently much larger than its physical organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrales_Erforschungsnetz_au%C3%9Fergew%C3%B6hnlicher_Himmelsph%C3%A4nomene" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span></span></span>

## How reports are tested

CENAP’s basic task is to reconstruct what was visible from a particular place at a particular time. Köhler asks witnesses for the date, exact time, location, viewing direction and a description of the object’s movement and appearance. Photographs and videos can help, but an image without reliable timing, orientation or location data may provide less information than witnesses assume.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ardsounds.de/episode/urn%3Aard%3Asection%3A87bb59a2d2129759/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ardsounds.de">[ARD Sounds]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ardsounds.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ardsounds.de.</span></span></span>

Investigators then compare the report with known possibilities. The practical checks may include:

* astronomical objects such as Venus, Jupiter, the Moon or bright stars;
* satellite and space-station passes;
* newly launched Starlink groups moving in a line;
* meteors, re-entering space debris or rocket activity;
* aircraft, helicopters and drones;
* balloons, including illuminated or miniature hot-air balloons;
* [searchlights]({{ 'searchlights-ad50fc/' | relative_url }}) and powerful event beams reflected from clouds;
* photographic effects, camera shake, digital zoom and reflections.

The purpose is not merely to name something that looks vaguely similar. A useful identification should fit the reported time, position, direction of travel, duration and visual behaviour. A proposed planet cannot explain an object moving rapidly across the whole sky, for example, while an aircraft explanation becomes weaker if flight direction and timing do not match. CENAP’s long experience is valuable chiefly because many apparently novel reports reproduce patterns encountered before.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrales_Erforschungsnetz_au%C3%9Fergew%C3%B6hnlicher_Himmelsph%C3%A4nomene" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span></span></span>

### The value of comparison cases

A single light in a dark sky offers few clues about its distance or size. Witnesses may sincerely describe a nearby object when they have actually seen a bright planet far beyond the atmosphere, or interpret a group of independently moving lanterns as a structured formation. CENAP approaches such cases through what is sometimes called a stimulus-response model: investigators seek the real stimulus that could have produced the reported perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrales_Erforschungsnetz_au%C3%9Fergew%C3%B6hnlicher_Himmelsph%C3%A4nomene" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span></span></span>

Its archive strengthens this work. The organisation published the *CENAP Report* in print and later online for several decades, documenting current cases, historical claims and recurring misidentifications. Hundreds of issues and associated records were subsequently digitised, while the present CENAP website maintains case files, report databases and reference material showing common aerial stimuli. The archive is useful not because every old conclusion must be accepted, but because it allows new photographs and descriptions to be compared with earlier examples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/wissenschaft/article188160697/Mysterioese-Flugobjekte-Ufo-Meldestelle-stellt-ihr-Archiv-online.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[welt.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTMysteriöse Flugobjekte: „Ufo-Meldestelle“ stellt ihr Archiv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DIE WELTMysteriöse Flugobjekte: „Ufo-Meldestelle“ stellt ihr Archiv</span></span></span>

This pattern recognition is particularly effective during reporting waves. When an unfamiliar technology becomes widely visible, many people encounter it at roughly the same time. Separate witnesses then submit similar accounts, making the cause easier to identify. CENAP can often recognise such a wave after the first few well-documented reports and use the known explanation to assess later submissions.

## Satellites and drones changed the workload

CENAP’s recent record totals do not show that extraordinary craft are appearing more frequently. They show how changes in the visible sky create new kinds of uncertainty. The centre received 1,084 reports during 2024 from Germany, Austria and Switzerland, compared with a stated usual annual range of roughly 600 to 800. Starlink satellite formations were identified as a major driver of that increase.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.handelsblatt.com/dpa/erscheinungen-am-himmel-rekordzahl-von-ufo-sichtungen-bei-meldestelle-eingegangen/30161262.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: handelsblatt.com">[handelsblatt.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">handelsblatt.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on handelsblatt.com.</span></span></span>

Starlink trains are unusually effective UFO stimuli. Soon after launch, a group of satellites can appear as a regularly spaced chain of bright points moving silently across the sky. To someone who does not know that a launch has occurred, the formation may look controlled, enormous or unlike conventional aviation. As the satellites spread into their operational orbits, the obvious “string” becomes less pronounced, but individual satellites can still brighten or fade according to their angle to the Sun.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ffh.de/nachrichten/top-meldungen/421824-rekord-so-viele-ufo-sichtungen-in-luetzelbach-gemeldet-wie-noch-nie.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ffh.de">[HIT RADIO FFH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ffh.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">HIT RADIO FFHUFO-Sichtungen über Deutschland in Lützelbach gemeldet</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HIT RADIO FFHUFO-Sichtungen über Deutschland in Lützelbach gemeldet</span></span></span>

The centre reported another record in 2025: 1,348 submissions. According to CENAP’s assessment, none of those cases remained genuinely unidentified after examination. Frequently reported causes included bright planets and stars, satellites, aircraft and drones. That conclusion should be read precisely. It means that CENAP believed it had found adequate conventional explanations for the reports it received; it is not an independently audited finding that every unusual aerial event over Germany during the year was identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swr3.de/aktuell/nachrichten/ufo-sichtungen-deutschland-alien-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swr3.de">[swr3.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swr3.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mehr Raumschiffe? Das steckt hinter den UFO-Sichtungen!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mehr Raumschiffe? Das steckt hinter den UFO-Sichtungen!</span></span></span>

Drones present a different problem from satellites. They can hover, turn sharply, carry unusual lighting and operate at heights or locations that make them difficult to recognise. Their real manoeuvrability means that features once treated as especially strange—stationary flight, sudden changes of direction or movement away from normal aircraft routes—are no longer automatically unusual. Köhler has described drones as one of the most common modern explanations submitted to the centre.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swr.de/swraktuell-radio/ufo-meldestelle-cenap-in-den-meisten-faellen-sind-es-drohnen-100.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swr.de">[SWR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO-Meldestelle CENAP: &quot;In den meisten Fällen sind es...Zum Internationalen UFO-Tag gibt der CENAP-Leiter und Gründer in SWR Aktuell...</span></span></span>

These shifts demonstrate CENAP’s wider value. Its records indirectly chart the changing visual environment: event spotlights and illuminated balloons generated one generation of reports, while satellite constellations and readily available drones generate another. A rise in sightings may therefore reflect technological change, media attention or clear viewing conditions rather than a rise in unexplained objects.

## What CENAP can establish

CENAP is strongest when a report contains enough information to test a specific explanation. A precise observation time can be compared with a satellite pass or astronomical position. A known viewing direction may distinguish Venus from an aircraft. Several reports along a path can support a meteor or re-entry explanation. An intact video may reveal flashing patterns, movement relative to stars or camera artefacts that are absent from a cropped still image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ardsounds.de/episode/urn%3Aard%3Asection%3A87bb59a2d2129759/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ardsounds.de">[ARD Sounds]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ardsounds.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ardsounds.de.</span></span></span>

It is also effective when the reported phenomenon belongs to a familiar class. Bright planets, skyward beams on clouds and freshly deployed satellite trains have recognisable characteristics. Once the relevant conditions are known, an explanation may be much more secure than the witness’s first impression suggests. The experience can still have been startling; identifying its cause does not imply that the observer was dishonest or foolish.

More difficult cases are usually those with poorer evidence. Reports may arrive days or years later, after exact times and directions have been forgotten. A mobile-phone camera may turn a tiny point of light into a pulsing disc because of autofocus, digital magnification and hand movement. An object may disappear behind a building or cloud before the witness can establish its path. In such circumstances, investigators may be unable to reproduce every detail even when an ordinary explanation is probable.

That distinction separates three conclusions that are often blurred together:

**Explained** means that a conventional source fits the important details of the observation.

**Insufficiently documented** means that too little reliable information survives to test the main possibilities.

**[Unresolved]({{ 'unresolved/' | relative_url }})** means that available evidence has been examined but no satisfactory identification has been reached.

CENAP’s public statements tend to emphasise explained cases, and its recent annual summaries have reported exceptionally high identification rates. Readers should nevertheless avoid treating a missing explanation as proof of an exotic craft—or treating a suggested explanation as certain when the original data are incomplete.

## Why CENAP’s conclusions divide opinion

The main strength of CENAP is also the source of its controversy. Its investigators begin from decades of evidence that most civilian UFO reports have ordinary causes. That prior experience prevents sensational interpretations of ambiguous lights and gives the organisation a large reference library of recurring mistakes. However, critics argue that a strong expectation of conventional explanation can become a bias if investigators settle on a familiar answer before testing all the reported details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrales_Erforschungsnetz_au%C3%9Fergew%C3%B6hnlicher_Himmelsph%C3%A4nomene" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span></span></span>

Other German UFO organisations have promoted more formalised procedures, including structured questionnaires, field investigations, image analysis and documented case databases. The Society for the Study of the UFO Phenomenon, or GEP, distinguishes between identified cases, cases lacking enough data and a smaller unresolved category. That approach provides a useful contrast with CENAP’s highly identification-focused public reporting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesellschaft_zur_Erforschung_des_UFO-Ph%C3%A4nomens" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gesellschaft zur Erforschung des UFO-Phänomens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gesellschaft zur Erforschung des UFO-Phänomens</span></span></span>

Criticism of CENAP has also concerned tone and treatment of witnesses. Rival researchers and some callers have alleged that reports were dismissed too quickly or that witnesses did not feel taken seriously. German UFO groups have at times publicly distanced themselves from CENAP’s methods and polemical style. These disputes do not by themselves disprove CENAP’s identifications, but they matter because trust affects whether witnesses disclose uncertainty, provide complete information or remain willing to answer follow-up questions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrales_Erforschungsnetz_au%C3%9Fergew%C3%B6hnlicher_Himmelsph%C3%A4nomene" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centrales Erforschungsnetz außergewöhnlicher Himmelsphänomene</span></span></span>

Its organisational status creates another limitation. CENAP is a small private network rather than a statutory body with published investigative standards, external oversight or guaranteed long-term funding. Annual totals are counts of reports received by CENAP, not a complete census of German aerial observations. They are influenced by media coverage, public awareness of the hotline, duplicate reports and the geographical reach of the service. The totals also include submissions from beyond Germany and must not be presented as Hessian sighting figures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.handelsblatt.com/dpa/erscheinungen-am-himmel-rekordzahl-von-ufo-sichtungen-bei-meldestelle-eingegangen/30161262.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: handelsblatt.com">[Handelsblatt]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">handelsblatt.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on handelsblatt.com.</span></span></span>

A fair assessment therefore lies between two extremes. CENAP should not be treated as an official authority whose verdict automatically closes every case. Nor should its explanations be rejected merely because they are sceptical. The proper question is whether each proposed identification matches the available facts and can be checked independently.

## Hesse’s national clearing house

CENAP’s lasting significance is less about proving or disproving one famous Hessian incident than about changing how German sighting reports are discussed. From its Odenwald base, it acts as a clearing house between witnesses, astronomy, aviation, space activity and the media. It translates a frightened or puzzled description into testable questions: Where was the observer? Which direction were they facing? What was visible there at that time? Did others report the same track? Does the image show an object, or the behaviour of the camera?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hfv-ev.de/ufos-ueber-dem-odenwald/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hfv-ev.de">[hfv-ev.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hfv-ev.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF Os über dem Odenwald?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs über dem Odenwald?December 6, 2022 — Seit den 90er Jahren betreibt Köhler die weit über Deutschland hinaus bekannte UFO-Meldestelle...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2022</span></span></span>

The centre’s work also reveals why UFO history cannot be understood as a simple catalogue of unexplained objects. Reports are shaped by the technology overhead, the observer’s expectations, the quality of the record and the availability of someone who recognises the pattern. Starlink did not merely add satellites to the sky; it added a new visual experience that thousands of people had never been taught to identify. Consumer drones similarly altered what a light can plausibly do at low altitude.

CENAP has been particularly successful at explaining that “UFO” is a temporary description rather than a final category. A report can begin as unidentified and cease to be so after checking. Its archive, accumulated experience and willingness to answer ordinary witnesses have made Lützelbach one of the most influential places in Germany’s civilian UFO landscape.

The organisation’s record is nevertheless best used critically. Its explanations are most persuasive when supported by precise timing, positional data and a close match between the proposed source and the witness account. They are less secure when based on incomplete recollection or broad resemblance. Seen in that balanced way, CENAP’s role in Hesse is not to issue the last word on every unusual light, but to demonstrate how far careful comparison can reduce mystery—and how much uncertainty remains when the original evidence is weak.
