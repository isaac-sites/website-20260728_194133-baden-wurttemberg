## Introduction

A daylight bolide is one of the strongest natural explanations available for the strange event reported near Rastpfuhl, Saarbrücken, on 1 April 1826. A large meteoroid breaking up in the atmosphere could account for extraordinary speed, violent thunder-like reports, several apparent fragments and a grey trail distorted by wind. Modern fireballs show that all of these effects can occur together, even in full daylight.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Saarland_af09d5_meteor_bolide_explan_b4df8b-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Meteor Theory" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The explanation is not complete, however. Johannes Becker reportedly heard the disturbance before seeing a grey, sheet-like form apparently rush towards him at very low altitude, fall nearby, remain quiet for about a minute and then move away amid renewed noise. No light, fire, scorching or meteorite fragments were found. Those details do not describe a straightforward bolide. The safest conclusion is therefore that a meteor or bolide could explain part of the Rastpfuhl experience—especially its speed and explosive sounds—but cannot be treated as a confirmed solution to [Saarland]({{ 'saarlands-ufo-story-between-mystery-and/' | relative_url }})’s 1826 mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

## Which details support a meteor explanation

The meteor theory begins with the event’s suddenness. Becker said the sky was mostly clear and still when cracking and rolling rapidly increased from the south-west. Moments later, something appeared with “lightning speed”. This is broadly compatible with a fireball: meteoroids enter the atmosphere at roughly 11 to 72 kilometres per second, and bright examples may cross a large part of the visible sky in only a few seconds. A bolide is a particularly bright fireball that ends in an explosive flash or pronounced fragmentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

The repeated reports of thunder, cracking and rolling also support an energetic atmospheric event. A large meteoroid moving at hypersonic speed produces shock waves along its flight path. Sudden fragmentation can release additional energy, creating one or more explosive reports that may be heard over a wide area. Modern meteor research treats such shock waves as a normal, although not universal, feature of deeply penetrating fireballs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07842" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Physics of Meteor Generated Shock Waves in the Earth&#x27;s Atmosphere</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Physics of Meteor Generated Shock Waves in the Earth&#x27;s Atmosphere - A Review...</span></span></span>

Fragmentation could also help explain why Becker described more than one visible form. His first comparison was with two parallel grey pieces of sheet metal. Shortly afterwards, the object appeared rolled like a cylinder and then spread out like a large linen sheet. A breaking meteoroid can create separate luminous fragments, clouds of dust and an elongated trail. From a poor viewing angle, especially without a clear sense of distance, these components may seem to change shape or move together as one object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

The grey-white colour is not by itself an argument against a bolide. A daylight fireball does not always present itself as the familiar bright streak seen at night. Fireballs can leave non-luminous smoke or dust trails below about 80 kilometres, and these may appear pale, dark or grey against the daytime sky. Upper-level winds can bend and spread a trail, producing folds, broad patches or sheet-like forms after the bright meteor has disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.amsmeteors.org/fireballs/faqf/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amsmeteors.org">[American Meteor Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">American Meteor Society Fireball FAQs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteor SocietyFireball FAQs - American Meteor Society...</span></span></span>

A particularly important point is that lack of recovered material does not rule out atmospheric fragmentation. The 2019 Flensburg bolide, also seen over Germany in daylight, began as an estimated two-to-three-metre body weighing between 10,000 and 20,000 kilograms. It fragmented heavily at heights of 37 to 46 kilometres, yet only one meteorite was recovered. In the 2015 Romanian superbolide, a body of roughly 4,500 kilograms disintegrated mainly into small fragments and dust, and searches found no meteorites. Large visible events can therefore leave little or nothing that is readily discovered on the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03968" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv[1702.03968] The January 7, 2015, superbolide over Romania and structural diversity of meter-sized asteroids...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 7, 2015</span></span></span>

These comparisons make a limited bolide reconstruction possible. A bright object may have crossed the Saarbrücken area, fragmented beyond Becker’s true line of sight and left a grey dust trail. Its shock waves could have produced the cracking and rolling heard by several [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-b354a2/' | relative_url }}). Becker, frightened and lacking reliable distance cues, may then have interpreted the distorted trail, drifting fragments or nearby debris as a solid object descending close to the ground.

## Which details do not fit neatly

The greatest difficulty is the reported order of sound and sight. Becker apparently heard thunder-like rolling, stopped, looked upwards and only then saw the grey object approaching. Ordinary meteor shock waves reach the ground well after the light because sound travels far more slowly. The American Meteor Society advises that sonic booms are commonly heard about one-and-a-half to four minutes after a fireball’s visual explosion. A witness would normally see the meteor first and hear it later, as happened dramatically at Chelyabinsk in 2013.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[grewi.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

The sequence could be reconciled only by adding assumptions. Becker might have missed the original flash, heard a delayed shock wave and then seen a persistent trail. Alternatively, different sections of a long flight path may have produced sounds and visible effects at different moments. Neither suggestion is impossible, but the surviving account does not say that Becker saw a stationary trail. It describes something rapidly approaching, rolling, spreading on or near the ground and later departing.

Claims that some fireballs produce sounds simultaneously with their appearance do not solve the problem convincingly. Witnesses have occasionally reported immediate hissing, sizzling or popping noises, often called electrophonic sounds. Their mechanism remains disputed, and they differ from the overwhelming thunder and rolling described at Rastpfuhl. Loud explosive reports are much more readily explained as delayed pressure waves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.amsmeteors.org/fireballs/faqf/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amsmeteors.org">[amsmeteors.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">American Meteor Society Fireball FAQs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteor SocietyFireball FAQs - American Meteor Society...</span></span></span>

The apparent altitude is another major obstacle. Becker estimated that the object flew only six or seven feet above the ground, passed within a few dozen paces and landed about seven paces away. A meteoroid still travelling at “lightning speed” cannot behave that way without producing severe local effects. Meteorites that survive atmospheric entry cease glowing several kilometres above the surface and continue in a slower, usually invisible phase known as dark flight. They do not normally race visibly at head height, flatten like cloth and then rise again.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

The physical inspection also weakened the idea of a nearby impact. Pastor Köllner visited the supposed site, questioned witnesses and examined ground that was said not to have been disturbed by others. He found no burning, scorched grass, damaged young trees or meteoritic stones. [Chladni]({{ 'chladni/' | relative_url }}) acknowledged that some meteoric events produced no recoverable stones and suggested that Becker might have badly underestimated the object’s distance. That optical-error proposal is essential to any bolide theory: the reported close encounter cannot be accepted literally while also being explained as a conventional high-altitude fireball.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

Most problematic of all is the reported minute-long pause followed by renewed movement. Becker said that after the grey form apparently fell, complete silence lasted for about a minute. Thunder and cracking then resumed, seeming to travel towards Heinrich König’s brickworks, followed by a powerful whirlwind-like rush. A bolide may fragment in stages, while delayed shock waves can arrive as separate bangs or rolling noise. Yet a meteor cannot land, remain still and take off again. This part of the narrative would have to represent misperceived distance, a drifting trail, wind-blown material or the merging of two different phenomena in a frightened witness’s account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

## How modern fireball cases compare

Modern events demonstrate how surprising a genuine bolide can look and sound. The Chelyabinsk meteor crossed the morning sky for more than 30 seconds, left a trail hundreds of kilometres long and broke apart about 25 kilometres above the ground. Its blast damaged buildings and injured people, mainly through shattered glass. Videos also showed the crucial delay between the brilliant flight and the arrival of the shock wave. This is strong evidence that a meteor can generate the scale of noise reported around Rastpfuhl, but it also highlights the timing problem in Becker’s account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/earth-matters/2013/02/15/meteor-fragments-blaze-over-the-ural-mountains/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Meteor Fragments Blaze Over the Ural Mountains</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceMeteor Fragments Blaze Over the Ural Mountains - NASA Science...</span></span></span>

The 2015 Romanian superbolide offers a closer comparison for unusual visible forms. It reached peak brightness at an altitude of about 43 kilometres, rapidly disintegrated into tiny fragments and dust, and left a stationary luminous trail visible for several seconds. Only one small fragment continued farther down. Such fragmentation could easily be described inconsistently by observers at different locations, particularly if clouds of material were seen against a bright sky. It nevertheless remained a high-altitude luminous event, not a grey body manoeuvring near the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03968" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv[1702.03968] The January 7, 2015, superbolide over Romania and structural diversity of meter-sized asteroids...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 7, 2015</span></span></span>

The Flensburg fall shows why absence of debris is weak negative evidence unless a reliable trajectory and search area are available. Despite cameras, video records and modern calculations, only a tiny proportion of the original body was recovered. In 1826 there were no calibrated observations, no photographs, no weather radar and no mapped fall zone. If an object fragmented tens of kilometres from the place Becker believed it had landed, investigators searching the immediate ground would have had little chance of finding anything.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02177" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv[2101.02177] Trajectory and orbit of the unique carbonaceous meteorite Flensburg...</span></span></span>

These modern cases also show what evidence is missing from the Saarbrücken incident. A secure bolide identification normally depends on several independent lines of information: a luminous flight path reported from widely separated locations, consistent timing, a terminal flash, delayed booms, camera or satellite data, infrasound, radar detections or a calculated meteorite fall area. The 1826 record has several witnesses to the noise but apparently only one detailed observer of the strange grey form. The original administrative statements are missing, preventing modern investigators from comparing each account or reconstructing a trajectory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[grewi.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

## The most defensible verdict

A meteor or fragmenting bolide remains a serious explanation because it naturally accounts for three central features: extraordinary apparent speed, explosive rolling sounds and multiple or changing aerial forms. A daylight dust trail could also appear grey rather than fiery and might spread into a broad, sheet-like shape. No recovered meteorite is required, since even well-observed modern bolides may disintegrate almost completely.

It does not explain the entire testimony without substantial reinterpretation. The sound appears too early, the perceived altitude is impossibly low, no luminous phase was reported and the supposed object seemed to pause and move again. Treating these details as errors of distance, sequence and perception may be reasonable, especially in a frightening event, but it cannot be proved from the surviving record.

Chladni himself did not simply classify the incident as a fireball. Although he was a pioneer of meteorite science, he and mineralogist Johann Jacob Nöggerath thought something resembling a whirlwind might be more likely. Chladni left open the possibility of an exploding meteoric mass and explicitly suggested that Becker could have judged its distance incorrectly. His cautious position still fits the evidence better than either absolute rejection or confident identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.grewi.de/saarbruecken-1826-deutschlands-aelteste-ufo-akte-weiterhin-verschollen20200528/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: grewi.de">[Grewi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">grewi.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">saarbruecken 1826 deutschlands aelteste ufo akte weiterhin verschollen20200528</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saarbrücken 1826: Deutschlands älteste UFO-Akte weiterhin verschollen...</span></span></span>

Within Saarland’s UFO history, the meteor theory therefore weakens any claim that the 1826 report necessarily involved an exotic craft. It provides credible mechanisms for much of the event and shows how an unfamiliar natural phenomenon could generate extraordinary testimony. Yet it does not fully close the case. The appropriate classification is **plausibly meteor-related but unresolved**, with the grey sheet-like appearance and reported near-ground behaviour remaining the hardest elements to reconcile.
