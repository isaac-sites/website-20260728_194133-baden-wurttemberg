## Introduction

The Greifswald recordings are unusually valuable by UFO-case standards because they do not depend on one witness or one camera. On 24 August 1990, several people around Greifswald, the Baltic coast and Rügen filmed or photographed two loose formations of bright lights from different positions. The material confirms that a real, prolonged and widely visible event occurred. It also shows that the lights changed brightness, drifted, descended and disappeared in stages.

What the recordings do **not** establish is equally important. Most images contain isolated points of light against a nearly featureless evening sky. Without a visible horizon, a known object beside the lights, reliable camera calibration or synchronised original footage, distance and size are difficult to determine. Multiple viewpoints strengthen the case as a documented observation, but they do not by themselves prove that the lights were solid craft. Later investigation has generally made military illumination or target flares the strongest explanation, although disputes remain over exact timing, reconstruction and the absence of a definitive firing record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[gwup.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">e. ## The surviving films and photographs

Accounts differ slightly over the final total, partly because copies, extracted stills and separate sequences have sometimes been counted differently. The MUFON-CES investigation, which argued that the case remained anomalous, reported six video recordings and eleven photographs from witnesses who were not all acquainted. Named camera operators included Jürgen Luchterhand near Greifswald, Ludmilla Ivanova and her husband in the city, Valery Vinogradov in the Eldena area, and Irmgard and Ingo Kaiser at Lauterbach harbour on Rügen. Other summaries commonly refer to four principal amateur films, which appear to be the clearest and most frequently circulated sequences rather than the complete visual archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[uapglobe.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

The best-known footage shows clusters of bright, pale or reddish lights arranged in a ring, arc, bunch or Y-like pattern. Individual lights brighten and fade at different times, while the group as a whole appears nearly stationary or moves only slowly. Some sequences show slight internal changes: one point seems to separate from the others, another appears to ignite near a formation, and the lights eventually go out one by one. These details made the material more persuasive than a single still photograph, because duration and changing behaviour can be assessed rather than inferred from one frozen frame.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

The photographs contribute less motion information but sometimes provide useful foreground references. One reported image by Vinogradov included lights from an apartment building roughly 150 metres away, giving analysts at least one fixed object within the frame. Even so, the building was not at the same distance as the sky lights and therefore could not supply their range or physical dimensions. It mainly helped establish camera orientation and angular position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/36348166/Ludwiger-Best-UFO-cases-in-Europe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Luchterhand, who lives in Berlin, was driving with his family about 6 km south of Greifswald&#x27;s nuclear power plant when he spotted the li...</span></span></span>

The audio on some video recordings is also evidentially useful. Luchterhand’s spontaneous commentary records surprise and uncertainty rather than a polished later claim. He reportedly stated while filming that he did not normally believe in “UFOs and such nonsense” but had never seen anything comparable. That does not identify the lights, but it supports the view that at least some principal witnesses were genuinely puzzled at the time rather than staging a performance for publicity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

## What the cameras establish

The visual record settles several basic questions more firmly than witness memory alone could.

First, the event was not a private misperception. Different cameras recorded broadly similar formations during the same period, and witnesses were spread across Greifswald, Usedom, Rügen and other coastal locations. This independent replication makes explanations based on hallucination, camera fault, lens reflection or a local hoax highly implausible. Whatever the source, there were bright physical lights in the distant sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

Second, the lights persisted long enough to be observed and recorded deliberately. Reports place the main sequence at approximately 20:28 to 20:57, although not every witness saw the full event and some accounts include later reappearances. The individual films are generally much shorter than the total reported duration. That distinction matters: no single continuous recording appears to document one unchanged group burning for half an hour. A sequence of separate flare releases could therefore produce a long overall incident even if each individual light lasted only several minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.openpr.de/news/581015/Unidentifizierte-Flugobjekte-ueber-der-Ostsee-DEGUFO-e-V-bittet-um-Ihre-Mithilfe.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openpr.de">[openPR.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openpr.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">open PR.de Unidentifizierte Flugobjekte über der Ostsee</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentifizierte Flugobjekte über der Ostsee - DEGUFO e.V. bittet um Ihre Mithilfe...</span></span></span>

Third, the films record slow collective movement rather than the unmistakable high-speed flight of a structured object. The apparent formations preserve a rough pattern but are not rigid. Their components shift slightly, fade independently and seem to descend. Those characteristics are compatible with several bright sources drifting under parachutes. Werner Walter’s sceptical analysis also claimed that one recording showed the formation moving with the wind and that another revealed smoke trails and flare-like burning beneath the light sources. These claims are significant, though access to the original numbered films has not always been equal, which has contributed to the continuing dispute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[GWUP e. V.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">e. V.Das UFO-Phänomen von Greifswald</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GWUP e. V.Das UFO-Phänomen von Greifswald - Ein deutscher Klassiker &#124; Pseudo- und Parawissenschaften &#124; GWUP e. Finally, the images show luminous points, not visible machines. There are no clearly resolved hulls, wings, surfaces, windows or structural connections between the lights. Descriptions of “spheres” refer primarily to the round appearance of overexposed light sources on video, not to a photographed three-dimensional body. A bright unresolved source normally spreads across several pixels or analogue video lines and appears larger than its true angular size, especially when focus, exposure and tape generation are uncertain.

## What multiple viewpoints add

Several viewpoints can, in principle, solve the central weakness of a distant-light case. When observers at known locations film the same object at the same moment, their lines of sight can be compared. Where those lines intersect provides an estimated position; distance can then be combined with angular size to calculate physical size. This is triangulation, and MUFON-CES investigators argued that the Greifswald material allowed such a reconstruction.

Their published interpretation placed the lights roughly 30 kilometres from Greifswald and about 14 kilometres from Trassenheide. From the measured image size, they estimated individual luminous objects at approximately 12 metres across, with a claimed error margin of about ten per cent. They treated these values as evidence against ordinary flares and as support for genuinely large objects over the Baltic area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

Those results sound precise, but their reliability depends entirely on the quality of the underlying inputs. Useful triangulation requires:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* exact camera positions and elevations;
* accurate viewing directions;
* confirmed identification of the same light in each recording;
* closely matched or synchronised times;
* original focal-length and zoom information;
* correction for camera movement, lens distortion and image cropping.

</div>

The publicly available material does not make it easy for an independent reader to verify all those conditions. The videos were made on domestic analogue equipment, often handheld, and copies circulated through television programmes and later digital uploads. If two frames were not recorded simultaneously, a drifting flare could move between observations and create a false intersection. A small error in azimuth also produces a large distance error when the lines of sight meet at a shallow angle.

The investigators’ estimated 12-metre diameter presents an additional problem. A glowing patch on analogue video is not necessarily the source’s physical edge. Saturation, blooming, atmospheric haze and videotape duplication enlarge bright points. Unless the light is resolved rather than overexposed, converting its apparent disc directly into metres can substantially overstate its size. The multi-camera material is therefore useful for locating the general sector of sky, but the very specific dimensions should be treated as model-dependent rather than directly measured facts.

The later flare investigation also used multiple witness directions, but reached a different conclusion. Its author plotted the lines of sight from different observation points and argued that they converged within the former Baltic firing area known as Air Firing Zone II, east of Rügen. The same report linked that zone with Warsaw Pact training involving parachute-supported pyrotechnic lights used as aerial targets. In this interpretation, the geographical agreement between cameras strengthens the flare explanation rather than the unknown-object case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.das-ufo-phaenomen.de/app/download/5790295537/Greifswald_Abschlussbericht.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: das-ufo-phaenomen.de">[Das UFO-Phänomen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">das-ufo-phaenomen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Das UFO-Phänomen</span></span></span>

## Witness testimony beyond the images

The witnesses add information that the cameras could not capture clearly, but their statements vary in evidential weight.

The strongest testimony concerns broad features repeated by many observers: two formations, several bright components, little or no audible sound, slow movement, changing brightness and disappearance over many minutes. These details agree reasonably well with the visual record. Reports from groups, including adults and children on Rügen, further confirm that the display was conspicuous and visible over a wide area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

More dramatic claims are harder to verify. Some witnesses described smaller lights leaving a formation and returning, a light approaching at extreme speed, or the entire group disappearing and later reforming elsewhere. A pilot witness reportedly estimated one approaching light at several times the speed of sound. Such speed estimates are intrinsically weak without known distance. A point of light crossing a small angular distance can appear very fast, but its true velocity cannot be calculated unless its range is established independently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

The impression that small objects were being “passed” between formations may also have arisen from separate lights brightening and fading along similar sight lines. Against a dark background, the eye tends to group neighbouring points into a single structure and interpret changes between them as coordinated motion. This does not mean witnesses invented the behaviour; it means that a genuine pattern of independent lights can be perceived as one organised object.

Testimony from people familiar with local military activity cuts in the opposite direction. A former East German military officer, Lüder Stock, described lights drifting beneath large parachutes and identified them as practice targets. Ferry captain Erwin Kollath and other residents also said that the televised display resembled illumination used during exercises. Their familiarity with the region gives this evidence weight, although Stock later acknowledged that he had not directly seen the vessel he assumed had launched them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

This division is important. The case does not pit “credible witnesses” against “sceptics”. Credible witnesses reported what they saw, while other credible and locally experienced observers supplied a conventional identification. The disagreement concerns interpretation, distance and mechanism, not whether lights were present.

## Why the images fit parachute flares

The leading conventional explanation is that the clusters were pyrotechnic lights deployed as military targets or illumination devices during training over the Baltic. A technical diagram reproduced in a later investigation shows an SAB-type bomb releasing multiple burning units, each descending beneath its own parachute. A single container could therefore create a cluster resembling the seven-light formations in the videos.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.das-ufo-phaenomen.de/app/download/5790295537/Greifswald_Abschlussbericht.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: das-ufo-phaenomen.de">[Das UFO-Phänomen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">das-ufo-phaenomen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Das UFO-Phänomen</span></span></span>

Several visible features support this reading:

**Slow descent.** Parachutes reduce the fall rate, making lights appear to hover when seen from tens of kilometres away.

**Independent movement.** Separate parachutes drift differently within the same wind field, preserving a loose group while gradually changing its shape.

**Sequential extinction.** Pyrotechnic units do not necessarily ignite or burn out at precisely the same moment.

**Lack of sound.** Distant military activity over the Baltic could be visually prominent yet acoustically inaudible from Greifswald and Rügen.

**Repeated formations.** More than one deployment could extend the total episode and create the impression that a formation vanished and later returned.

**Flare-like brightness and smoke.** Sceptical investigators reported visible flickering, bright burning beneath some lights and smoke trails in less frequently shown footage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[GWUP e. The geographical reconstruction also places the viewing sector within or near a known military training area. A later report cited evidence that Air Firing Zone II was being used during August 1990 and reproduced a briefing-room photograph showing the zone reserved during that week. It also quoted former military personnel who said Warsaw Pact forces had used parachute-supported pyrotechnic targets in the area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.das-ufo-phaenomen.de/app/download/5790295537/Greifswald_Abschlussbericht.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: das-ufo-phaenomen.de">[Das UFO-Phänomen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">das-ufo-phaenomen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Das UFO-Phänomen</span></span></span>

That evidence substantially strengthens the flare case, but it is not a perfect documentary chain. No surviving order has been publicly produced that names the exact unit, munition and launch time for 24 August. Some relevant military records were reportedly unavailable or destroyed. The safest conclusion is therefore that the footage is highly consistent with known training devices used in the correct area, rather than that every operational detail has been proven beyond dispute.

## What the images cannot establish

The Greifswald material is often described as unusually strong evidence, and in one sense that is correct: it is strong evidence for a real multi-witness aerial-light event. It is much weaker evidence for a large unknown craft.

The central limitations are structural.

### No dependable scale

A light against the sky has no built-in ruler. Apparent diameter on tape can be caused by focus, exposure, atmospheric scattering and electronic blooming. Claims that the lights were several metres wide depend on assumed distance and on treating the recorded luminous patch as the true boundary of an object.

### No clearly resolved structure

The points do not reveal material surfaces connecting them. A formation of seven lights may be seven independent sources rather than lamps mounted on one vehicle. The slight relative movement of the points arguably favours independent sources.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Mecklenburg-Vorpomme_d975e6_greifswald_visual_ev_0d2257-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Visual Evidence illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Mecklenburg-Vorpomme_d975e6_greifswald_visual_ev_0d2257-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Mecklenburg-Vorpomme_d975e6_greifswald_visual_ev_0d2257-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

### Incomplete continuity

The best-known clips are short segments from a longer reported event. They do not provide an uninterrupted, time-coded record from first appearance to final disappearance. This makes it difficult to decide whether one set of lights lasted exceptionally long or several sets appeared in succession.

### Uneven access to originals

Much public discussion relies on television edits, still frames, compressed web copies or descriptions of numbered films held by investigators. Editing can remove horizons, obscure time order and exaggerate apparent motion. Without original tapes, camera specifications and a transparent frame-by-frame comparison, later analysts cannot fully reproduce early photogrammetric claims.

### Witness estimates are not measurements

Descriptions such as “supersonic”, “huge” or “close” express genuine impressions but cannot substitute for known range. Human observers are poor at judging the distance of isolated lights at night or twilight, particularly over water.

## How later investigation changed the case

The early reputation of the Greifswald recordings rested on abundance: many witnesses, several cameras and a striking formation. In the first years after German reunification, official information was difficult to obtain, and television programmes could present the images as a major unresolved event without a settled military context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.n-tv.de/wissen/Es-gibt-keinen-ungeloesten-Fall-article5670326.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: n-tv.de">[n-tv.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">n-tv.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Werner Walter, Ufo-Jäger: &quot;Es gibt keinen ungelösten Fall</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Werner Walter, Ufo-Jäger: &quot;Es gibt keinen ungelösten Fall</span></span></span>

MUFON-CES strengthened the case in one respect by collecting recordings, identifying camera locations and attempting geometric analysis. Its work showed why multiple viewpoints matter and prevented the event from being dismissed as a single ambiguous clip. It also maintained that the flare hypothesis failed to explain claimed high-speed movements, reappearance and calculated size.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/greifswald-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Greifswald Lights (24 August 1990) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP Globe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1990</span></span></span>

Subsequent sceptical work weakened the extraordinary interpretation more substantially. It identified a suitable known mechanism, testimony from people familiar with military pyrotechnics, a relevant training zone, evidence of exercises during the same period and film characteristics consistent with burning, drifting flare clusters. The report’s reconstruction of witness directions placed the phenomenon in that military area rather than over the nuclear installation or directly above Greifswald.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.das-ufo-phaenomen.de/app/download/5790295537/Greifswald_Abschlussbericht.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: das-ufo-phaenomen.de">[Das UFO-Phänomen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">das-ufo-phaenomen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Das UFO-Phänomen</span></span></span>

The remaining uncertainty is narrow rather than unlimited. It concerns the exact exercise, launching force, munition model and interpretation of a few disputed movements. It does not require an exotic explanation. In evidential terms, the recordings have moved from “compelling images of unknown objects” towards “well-documented images of lights with a strong, though incompletely documented, military explanation”.

## Overall assessment

The Greifswald visual archive is one of the most important pieces of UFO evidence associated with [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern]({{ 'mecklenburg-vorpommern/' | relative_url }}) because it demonstrates the difference between **corroboration** and **identification**. Multiple films and photographs corroborate the presence, timing and broad appearance of the lights. They greatly reduce the chance of fraud, camera artefact or individual error.

They do not, however, provide reliable scale, solid structure or extraordinary performance. The most unusual witness interpretations depend on uncertain distance and visual grouping, while the slower, clearer behaviour in the recordings matches separate luminous sources descending and drifting. Later evidence connecting the observation sector with military training and parachute-supported pyrotechnics gives the flare explanation the strongest overall fit.

The case is therefore visually impressive but not visually decisive. Its recordings are valuable chiefly because they preserve a real regional event from several viewpoints—and because, when examined carefully, they show how even abundant imagery can remain ambiguous when the cameras capture light but not distance, scale or context.
