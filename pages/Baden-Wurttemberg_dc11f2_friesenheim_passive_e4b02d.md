## Introduction

The passive-radar claim from Friesenheim-Oberweier is one of Baden-Württemberg’s most unusual recent UFO cases, but it does not amount to radar confirmation of an extraordinary craft. At about 5:34 am on 20 September 2024, a woman returning from a night shift reported a bright, wave-like formation that appeared for only a few seconds in the southern sky. A privately operated radio receiver roughly one kilometre away recorded a short signal at approximately the same time.

That coincidence makes the case more interesting than a sighting supported only by memory. It also creates the central dispute. Investigators from the Society for the Investigation of the UFO Phenomenon, known as GEP, treated the signal as independent support for the visual report. Critics argue that the equipment recorded a radio-frequency echo rather than an image, direction or position of the object. The evidence therefore supports the presence of an unexplained brief event, but not a secure conclusion that the witness and receiver detected the same thing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/14949908" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[zenodo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Case Dataset 20240920 B &#124; Zenodo...</span></span></span>

## The September 2024 witness report

The principal witness was an employee of the Federal Police in Offenburg. According to the published GEP case account, she had just arrived home in Oberweier after a night shift when she heard a loud noise from the southern sky. She compared it to a metal chain being dragged over asphalt. Looking upwards, she saw a very bright white formation with a wavy outline and two vertically aligned points of light at one end.

The phenomenon reportedly vanished when the sound stopped. The witness estimated that the whole observation lasted no longer than five seconds. She was able to give a precise time because she checked her watch immediately afterwards. Her husband, who was inside the house, did not see the light but independently heard an unusual sound through an open balcony door. He described it differently, as resembling many small beads falling on a tiled floor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/14949908" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Case Dataset 20240920 B &#124; Zenodo...</span></span></span>

There are reasons to take the report seriously. The sighting was reported promptly, the investigator knew the witness’s identity, and the sound had limited corroboration from a second person. The witness also supplied a sketch rather than gradually developing a description through repeated media interviews.

Those strengths should not be overstated. There was only one visual witness, no photograph or video, and no measured bearing, elevation or angular size. The observation began only after the noise attracted attention, so the actual start of the phenomenon is unknown. Its extreme brevity left little opportunity to distinguish an unusual aircraft view, a meteor-related event, a transient reflection or another short-lived optical effect.

GEP classified the case as a “good UFO”, meaning that its investigators considered the report credible and resistant to an immediate conventional identification. That is an internal case assessment, not a finding by police, air-traffic control, a government radar service or an independent scientific panel. The investigation was also listed as being on hold rather than conclusively completed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-world%E2%80%99s-first-passive-radar-signal-confirms-visual-ufo-sighting.14046/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: World’s First: Passive Radar Signal Confirms visual UFO-Sighting &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

## What the passive-radar system recorded

The phrase “passive radar” can give a misleading impression of an airport-style screen showing a target moving across a map. The Friesenheim apparatus was a much simpler receiving station. It used a directional Yagi antenna, an Airspy R2 software-defined radio receiver and signal-display software, tuned to 143.050 MHz. The receiving antenna pointed broadly south-west and operated continuously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/14949908/preview/passive-radar-configuration.txt?include_deleted=0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Preview...</span></span></span>

The radio source was GRAVES, a powerful French space-surveillance radar transmitter near Dijon. Objects or ionised trails can reflect some of its signal towards receivers elsewhere in Europe. Aircraft, satellites, space debris and meteor trails are all capable of producing detectable echoes under suitable geometry. Amateur meteor observers commonly use the same 143.050 MHz transmission for this reason.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osservatoriocopernico.org/en/radioastronomy/sid/the-system/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: osservatoriocopernico.org">[osservatoriocopernico.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">osservatoriocopernico.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is important to note that the Meteor Scatter phenomenon can occur at any hour and is not influenced by weather conditions.... At the reported sighting time, the Oberweier receiver captured a short pattern on a waterfall display. Such a display places time on one axis and received frequency on the other; brightness represents signal strength. Frequency shifts and changing intensities may reveal that a reflecting target or ionised trail has altered the received radio signal.

The GEP account interpreted the trace as lasting slightly more than two seconds. Investigators considered its timing and apparent pattern sufficiently similar to the witness’s sketch to infer that the luminous phenomenon had produced the radio echo. They also argued that the trace suggested a briefly visible, essentially stationary object rather than something travelling steadily through the field of view. The underlying files, equipment details and case article were subsequently deposited in an open Zenodo dataset, an unusually useful act of transparency for a privately investigated UFO report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/14949908" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Case Dataset 20240920 B &#124; Zenodo...</span></span></span>

The recording is genuine evidence in the limited sense that the radio system registered a transient near the reported time. It is not merely a verbal recollection added years later. However, what that transient represents remains uncertain.

## Why the claimed match is disputed

The central weakness is that the waterfall trace is not a picture of the southern sky. Its curved or segmented appearance cannot straightforwardly be matched to the visible outline of an object. A wavy line in a frequency-time plot describes changing radio-frequency energy; it does not show a wavy physical body.

That distinction matters because the original presentation repeatedly compared the shape of the witness’s sketch with the radar display. Critics noted that a single receiver and narrow-band waterfall recording do not supply the measurements needed to reconstruct an object’s silhouette. The equipment did not independently record a photograph, precise azimuth, altitude, range or three-dimensional path.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-world%E2%80%99s-first-passive-radar-signal-confirms-visual-ufo-sighting.14046/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: World’s First: Passive Radar Signal Confirms visual UFO-Sighting &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

The antenna’s south-west orientation gives only broad sensitivity rather than a sharply bounded surveillance sector. GEP itself described the plotted limits of coverage as fluid. A reflected signal could therefore have originated from a much larger volume of airspace than a simple coverage diagram implies. Without a second or third synchronised station, the echo could not be triangulated to the witness’s line of sight. Basic forward-scatter systems can detect events but generally cannot determine their exact position without cross-bearing or comparable multi-station measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-world%E2%80%99s-first-passive-radar-signal-confirms-visual-ufo-sighting.14046/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[metabunk.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: World’s First: Passive Radar Signal Confirms visual UFO-Sighting &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

The equipment also used a 2 kHz bandwidth and one receiving channel. More advanced passive-radar systems compare direct and reflected signals, estimate delay and Doppler shift, use multiple antennas or receivers, and apply tracking algorithms to establish position and movement. Germany’s commercial and military passive-radar programmes illustrate how much additional processing and calibration are normally involved in dependable air-surveillance work. The Oberweier station was a useful radio-observation installation, but it was not equivalent to a certified air-traffic or defence radar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hensoldt.net/news/hensoldt-passive-radar-to-be-used-in-civil-aviation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hensoldt.net">[hensoldt.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hensoldt.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">passive radar to be used in civil aviation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HENSOLDT passive radar to be used in civil aviation2 Oct 2024 — HENSOLDT is working with DFS, the German air navigation service p...</span></span></span>

## The aircraft challenge

A particularly important criticism emerged when online analysts compared the event time with archived aircraft-tracking information. One reconstruction identified a DHL-operated Boeing 757 approaching or entering the broadly defined receiving area close to the moment of the radio trace. That does not prove that the aircraft caused the witness’s visual experience, but it challenges the early claim that no conventional aircraft was present in relevant airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-world%E2%80%99s-first-passive-radar-signal-confirms-visual-ufo-sighting.14046/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: World’s First: Passive Radar Signal Confirms visual UFO-Sighting &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

Several separate possibilities therefore have to be kept apart:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The aircraft may have produced the radio echo while an unrelated phenomenon produced the light and sound.
* The witness may have seen part of the aircraft or an optical effect associated with it, while the receiver detected the same aircraft.
* The timing may be coincidental, with neither the aircraft nor the radio echo explaining the sighting.
* The archived flight reconstruction may be incomplete or geographically imprecise enough not to resolve the issue.

</div>

Public aircraft-tracking websites depend on received transponder data and do not constitute a complete primary-radar record. Even so, an identifiable aircraft near the relevant time is a conventional candidate that requires detailed elimination. The published case material does not provide a documented comparison of the aircraft’s exact bearing from the witness, its altitude, lighting configuration, acoustic arrival time and expected GRAVES echo.

The sound presents an additional complication. A distant aircraft’s noise would normally continue rather than end abruptly after two to five seconds, although buildings, terrain, atmospheric refraction and the witness’s changing attention can strongly affect perceived duration. Conversely, a brief metallic or rattling sound might have originated locally and only appeared to come from the light. Because no audio recording or independent source was found, the sound cannot firmly join the visual and radio observations into one event.

## Could it have been a meteor?

A meteor or its ionised trail is another plausible category because GRAVES signals are routinely used for radio meteor detection. Meteors can produce bright, brief visual effects, and their trails can reflect VHF radio transmissions. On waterfall displays, such events may appear as short spots or lines with changing frequency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mcbainsite.co.uk/amateur-radio/meteor-scatter-graves-radar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mcbainsite.co.uk">[mcbainsite.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mcbainsite.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mc Bainsite Meteor Scatter, GRAVES Radar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mc Bainsite Meteor Scatter, GRAVES Radar</span></span></span>

The fit is imperfect. The witness did not describe a conventional straight meteor streak but a stationary-looking wave with two points. Audible meteor sounds are also difficult to evaluate: ordinary sonic effects generally arrive after a delay, while reports of sounds perceived at the same time as a bright meteor remain debated and require careful documentation. No matching widely reported fireball was identified in the immediate area and time.

GEP argued against lightning because the sky was reported as clear and because the radio apparatus was not designed to detect visible lightning itself. That reasoning is too categorical. The relevant question is not whether visible light enters the radio receiver, but whether lightning-related radio interference, an ionised path or another simultaneous reflecting target could create a trace. The available single-station recording does not by itself exclude all atmospheric or instrumental causes.

A meteor explanation therefore remains possible but unproven. It has the advantage of naturally linking a short visual event with a GRAVES reflection, yet it does not readily account for every reported feature.

## What would have made the correlation convincing?

The case would have been substantially stronger with independent measurements capable of showing that the radio echo came from the witness’s viewing direction. The most useful missing evidence includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* synchronised recordings from two or more separated receivers, allowing triangulation;
* an all-sky or fixed-direction camera showing the phenomenon at the same timestamp;
* calibrated antenna-pattern data and raw signal files sufficient for independent reconstruction;
* a complete comparison with aircraft, satellite and meteor records;
* measured witness azimuth and elevation rather than a general statement that the event was in the southern sky;
* audio or environmental recordings establishing whether the sound was local, airborne or atmospheric.

</div>

Modern ground-based UAP research proposals emphasise precisely this kind of multimodal approach: wide-field cameras, calibrated radio receivers, radar-derived range and movement, microphones and environmental instruments operating under a common clock. Multiple sensors are valuable not simply because they produce more data, but because they help distinguish a real correlation from two unrelated events occurring within the same time window.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

The Friesenheim case had only a partial version of that ideal. There was a visual account, a second person who heard a noise and an automatically stored radio trace. Yet the visual, acoustic and radio observations lacked the spatial measurements required to demonstrate a common source.

## Did passive radar confirm the sighting?

The fairest answer is **no, not in the strong sense implied by the early headlines**. The receiver confirmed that an unexplained radio-frequency reflection or disturbance occurred at approximately the reported time. It did not establish where the reflecting source was, what produced it, or whether it was the luminous formation seen by the witness.

The timing coincidence is still meaningful. Unlike most short UFO reports in Baden-Württemberg, the case includes privately collected instrumental data that existed independently of the witness’s later description. The publication of the configuration and data files also allows the claim to be examined rather than accepted solely on authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/14949908" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Case Dataset 20240920 B &#124; Zenodo...</span></span></span>

Later discussion weakened the claim of confirmation, however, by exposing three unresolved problems: the waterfall display was treated too much like an image of the object; the single receiver could not localise the echo; and a conventional aircraft may have occupied relevant airspace. None of those points conclusively solves the witness’s experience, but together they prevent the radio trace from functioning as decisive corroboration.

Within Baden-Württemberg’s UFO history, Friesenheim is best described as an **unresolved but weakly correlated multi-sensor case**. It is stronger than an unsupported light report, yet far weaker than a properly triangulated radar-visual event. Its lasting value lies less in proving an extraordinary object than in showing how easily an intriguing timestamp match can be mistaken for confirmation when the instruments do not measure position, shape and identity.
