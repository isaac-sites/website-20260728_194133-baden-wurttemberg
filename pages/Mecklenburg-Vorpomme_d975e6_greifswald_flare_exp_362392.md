## Introduction

Military illumination flares provide the strongest explanation for the famous lights filmed near Greifswald on 24 August 1990. Suspended beneath parachutes, such flares are designed to burn brightly while descending slowly. From many kilometres away, they can appear almost stationary, make no audible sound and drift into changing formations. Most importantly, some Eastern-bloc illumination devices released several separate burning elements at once, closely matching the repeated groups of seven lights seen over the Baltic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[gwup.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">das ufo phaenomen von greifswald ein deutscher klassiker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO-Phänomen von Greifswald - Ein deutscher...March 5, 2000 — Einer dieser Klassiker ereignete sich in Deutsch land, und zwar am Abend d...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 5, 2000</span></span></span>

The explanation is not proved by a surviving operational order that names the exact aircraft, munition and release time. That missing documentary link leaves room for dispute. ## How illumination flares behave

An illumination flare is not the small, fast-burning countermeasure commonly seen streaming behind combat aircraft. It is a much larger pyrotechnic device intended to light a target area or provide a visible aerial target. After release, a parachute slows the burning element so that it remains aloft for minutes rather than falling rapidly to the ground. Military examples have combined extremely bright output with burn times of several minutes; an American AN-M26 flare, for comparison, descended by parachute while burning for about three minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/196232/an-m26-parachute-flares/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalmuseum.af.mil">[Air Force Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalmuseum.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force MuseumAN-M26 Parachute Flares &gt; National Museum of the United States Air Force™ &gt; DisplayParachute flares provided illumination...</span></span></span>

This mechanism reproduces several features that made the Greifswald lights seem unusual.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.ufouap.net/en/cases/greifswald-lights" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufouap.net">[ufouap.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufouap.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greifswald lights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">greifswald lights</span></span></span>

**Slow descent can resemble hovering.** A distant flare may lose altitude steadily without appearing to move very far across a witness’s field of view. With no buildings, hills or other nearby reference points over the Baltic, observers could not reliably judge its distance, size or vertical speed.

**Parachutes allow gradual drift.** Each burning element is carried by the air around it. Small differences in deployment, parachute behaviour and local wind can alter the distance between neighbouring lights. A tidy cluster may therefore spread, contract or become uneven without any object deliberately manoeuvring. Aircraft-flare documentation explicitly treats descent rate and drift as important characteristics of illumination systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/af_a3/publication/afman11-2ac-130jv3/afman11-2ac-130jv3.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[E-Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pyrotechnic mixtures, particularly illuminating flare compositions, burn with intense heat... burning time, rate of descent, and drift o...</span></span></span>

**Extreme brightness conceals the supporting equipment.** A camera records the burning material as a glaring point or enlarged luminous disc, while the thin parachute and suspension lines remain invisible. Blooming in the video image can make each source look larger and more solid than it is.

**Silence is expected at long range.** The deployment aircraft, launch ship or accompanying fighters may have been far beyond the witnesses. Sound also weakens and arrives later than light. A bright object over the sea can consequently appear silent even when conventional military activity is taking place around it.

These effects are individually ordinary. Their combination is deceptive: a bright, long-lived point with no visible support, no obvious sound and only slight movement naturally gives the impression of something suspended under its own control.

## Why the Greifswald videos match flare drops

The most distinctive feature of the recordings is not simply that several lights were present. It is that they appeared in repeated, loosely grouped formations, commonly described as seven luminous points. That number matters because Eastern-bloc illumination bombs could disperse multiple pyrotechnic elements, each supported by its own parachute. Technical descriptions of the SAB-100 illumination bomb state that its flare elements separate after release and descend individually beneath parachutes. Later Greifswald research specifically connected seven-element flare packages with the formations visible in the films.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.digitalcombatsimulator.com/upload/iblock/f6d/DCS_FC3_Flight_Manual_EN.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalcombatsimulator.com">[Digital Combat Simulator]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalcombatsimulator.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Digital Combat Simulator FLAMING CLIFFS 37-15: The SAB-100 Illumination Bomb</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Each flare element is equipped with a parachute to decrease the rate of fall.... The time-delay fuse starts spinning after bomb release...</span></span></span>

A multi-element package would initially create a recognisable cluster. The elements would then separate as their parachutes opened and responded differently to the air. That sequence accounts for a pattern that appears organised at first but slowly changes shape. It does not require seven independent vehicles to coordinate their positions.

The location also supports the explanation. The lights were observed towards a part of the Baltic used as Warsaw Pact Air Firing Zone II, east of Rügen and north of Usedom. Historical reporting on the zone describes East German, Soviet and Czechoslovak aircraft practising against luminous aerial targets released at altitude. A documented 1983 accident involving a Czechoslovak MiG-21 occurred during an exercise against an illumination target in the same training zone, demonstrating that this was an established local military practice rather than a scenario invented after the UFO report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ostsee-zeitung.de/lokales/vorpommern-greifswald/usedom/das-raetsel-von-peenemuende-ist-geloest-es-war-eine-sowjetische-mig-WM6TLYTIOCX3E3KFD4NQYVRETU.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ostsee-zeitung.de">[OZ - Ostsee Zeitung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ostsee-zeitung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ostsee-zeitung.de.</span></span></span>

Later investigators also reported evidence that Czechoslovak forces used the zone around the relevant period and possessed the type of multi-element illumination equipment capable of producing the observed pattern. These claims are largely preserved through retrospective correspondence, specialist aviation discussions and sceptical investigations rather than a complete publicly available flight log. They strengthen the flare case, but should not be described as an unbroken official paper trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufo-information.de/index.php/aktuelles/weblog/282-greifswald-bericht-loest-reaktionen-aus" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo-information.de">[ufo-information.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo-information.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Information Greifswald Bericht löst Reaktionen aus</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Information Greifswald Bericht löst Reaktionen aus</span></span></span>

### Repeated formations explain the long sighting

The reported duration is often treated as the greatest obstacle to the flare theory. A single flare does not normally burn for half an hour. That objection assumes, however, that every light belonged to one simultaneous release.

A training operation could deploy several packages in succession. As one group descended and faded, another could appear above or nearby. Individual witnesses may then remember one continuous display, while recordings made at different places capture separate stages of the exercise. Later CENAP research argued that the longest duration commonly quoted for the case combines observations from different locations rather than documenting one unchanged formation lasting for the entire period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hjkc.de/158.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hjkc.de">[CENAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hjkc.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CENAPCENAP-Infoline 03</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Greifswald von solchen Leuchtbomben von... Luftschießzone-II an diesem Tag durch die tschechoslowakischen Luftstreitkräfte genutzt wurde...</span></span></span>

Successive deployment also explains why some lights disappeared while others appeared to remain, and why two similar clusters were reported. A sequence of seven-element packages can extend an event far beyond the burn time of any one element without requiring unusually long-lasting ammunition.

### Apparent motion need not be powered flight

Some witnesses described lights entering or leaving the formation, making rapid movements or changing position in ways that seemed deliberate. The films themselves are less decisive. They show luminous points against a distant sky, usually without a fixed horizon or enough stable reference detail to calculate range and speed.

Several ordinary effects can exaggerate apparent movement:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A handheld camera transfers its own motion to the lights.
* Zooming changes the apparent spacing between points.
* Wind carries separate parachutes at slightly different speeds.
* A newly ignited element may look as though it has entered an existing group.
* A fading element can seem to accelerate away or vanish abruptly.
* Aircraft, missiles or other lights associated with an exercise may cross the same line of sight.

</div>

Without triangulated positions from synchronised cameras, a bright point moving across the image cannot be converted reliably into a physical speed. The recordings document a real luminous display, but they do not establish the rapid, controlled motion expected of a solid craft.

## Why seven lights are especially important

Many proposed UFO explanations can account for one or two vague characteristics. Aircraft can explain movement, balloons can explain silence and astronomical objects can explain brightness. The flare theory is stronger because it accounts for the case’s unusual combination of features, including its most memorable numerical pattern.

A package that separates into seven burning units naturally produces seven points without requiring coincidence. Each unit can remain bright for several minutes, descend at a broadly similar rate and drift away from its neighbours. A second release produces another seven-point cluster. Later descriptions of comparable military illumination devices and photographs taken during Eastern European exercises reportedly showed arrangements closely resembling the Greifswald images.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flugzeugforum.de/threads/suche-infos-militaeruebung-in-der-lsz-ii-im-august-1990.72113/page-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flugzeugforum.de">[Flugzeug: Flugzeugforum.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flugzeugforum.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">page 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">page 2</span></span></span>

The formation was sometimes interpreted as evidence of a single structured object, with lights attached to an invisible body. Yet the relative positions do not remain rigid in the recordings. A real airframe would ordinarily preserve the geometry between its lights unless it was rotating dramatically. Separate parachute flares, by contrast, should slowly alter their spacing. The looseness of the pattern therefore favours independent luminous sources rather than lamps fixed to one craft.

This distinction is central to the case. The lights looked collectively meaningful because the human eye naturally connects nearby points into shapes. Once they are treated as separate objects released together, the apparent “formation” becomes evidence for the flare mechanism rather than evidence against it.

## The strongest objections to the explanation

The flare theory is persuasive, but its evidential gaps should not be concealed.

### No definitive operational record has surfaced

The clearest possible resolution would be a dated military document confirming that a named unit released a particular illumination device over Air Firing Zone II at the observed time. Public discussion has instead relied on indirect material: the established purpose of the zone, testimony from former military personnel, information about Czechoslovak use of the area and the visual resemblance between the lights and known flare packages.

Researchers critical of the explanation have noted that searches of surviving East German military records did not reveal an exercise entry that conclusively matches the event. They have also challenged shifting versions of the theory that variously attributed the lights to East German, Soviet or Czechoslovak forces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.exomagazin.tv/das-raetsel-der-greifswald-lichter/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: exomagazin.tv">[exomagazin.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">exomagazin.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Das Rätsel der Greifswald-Lichter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Das Rätsel der Greifswald-Lichter</span></span></span>

That uncertainty matters, but it does not make the mechanism implausible. August 1990 was a period of institutional disruption shortly before German reunification. Responsibility was divided among East German and allied Warsaw Pact forces, and records from foreign units were not necessarily preserved in East German archives. The absence of a located file is therefore weaker evidence than proof that no exercise occurred.

### Some reported movements sound too fast

Witnesses recalled sudden accelerations, lights crossing the formation and objects behaving unlike passive flares. These reports deserve recognition, especially where they were recorded close to the event. They are also difficult to test because distance was unknown and much of the detailed testimony was gathered or republished later.

A point that appears to travel a large angular distance may be a nearby aircraft rather than one of the distant cluster lights. Camera motion can create additional apparent speed, while fading and ignition can produce the impression that a light has jumped from one position to another. The surviving material does not provide the calibrated, continuous tracking needed to show that any individual luminous point performed a manoeuvre impossible for a flare.

### The display was said to last too long

This objection is substantial only if one fixed set of lights remained continuously visible for the full reported duration. The evidence is less clear. Timings differ between accounts, and observers were distributed across a wide coastal area. Two or more successive deployments can produce a much longer overall episode than one flare’s burn time. Military exercises also need not consist of a single release.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gwup.org/skeptiker-artikel/pseudo-parawissenschaften/das-ufo-phaenomen-von-greifswald-ein-deutscher-klassiker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwup.org">[gwup.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">das ufo phaenomen von greifswald ein deutscher klassiker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO-Phänomen von Greifswald - Ein deutscher...March 5, 2000 — Einer dieser Klassiker ereignete sich in Deutsch land, und zwar am Abend d...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 5, 2000</span></span></span>

The duration therefore weakens simplistic versions of the theory—such as one ordinary flare burning for half an hour—but does not seriously damage a sequence of multi-element flare drops.

### Witnesses did not report ground illumination or smoke

A flare bright enough to be visible over a great distance might be expected to light clouds, reveal smoke or illuminate the sea beneath it. Yet whether those effects would be noticeable depends on distance, atmospheric clarity, viewing angle, remaining daylight and camera exposure. A video adjusted for bright points can under-record faint smoke and reflected light.

The same distance that made the lights hard to identify also reduced the visibility of their surrounding effects. The absence of clearly recorded parachutes, smoke trails or illuminated water is therefore not surprising, although a high-quality close-range film showing none of them would have made the objection stronger.

## Why the military setting outweighs the mystery

The flare explanation does not rest solely on the fact that military lights can look strange. It connects a specific mechanism to a specific place with a documented history of exactly the relevant activity. Air Firing Zone II was used for aerial weapons training, including exercises involving luminous airborne targets. Parachute-supported devices produce slow, silent descents. Multi-element packages account for the seven-point clusters, while repeated releases account for the length of the episode.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ostsee-zeitung.de/lokales/vorpommern-greifswald/usedom/das-raetsel-von-peenemuende-ist-geloest-es-war-eine-sowjetische-mig-WM6TLYTIOCX3E3KFD4NQYVRETU.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ostsee-zeitung.de">[ostsee-zeitung.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ostsee-zeitung.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ostsee-zeitung.de.</span></span></span>

By comparison, the extraterrestrial or unknown-craft interpretation has no independent evidence of a vehicle. No structure is visible between the lights. No verified radar record has been publicly tied to their movement. No physical trace was recovered, and the videos do not provide distances from which extraordinary speed or size can be calculated.

This does not mean the witnesses imagined the event or that the recordings are worthless. They captured genuine lights whose source was not obvious from the shore. The mistake arose at the interpretation stage: separate, distant pyrotechnic sources were treated as if they formed one or more controlled objects.

## The most reasonable verdict

The [Greifswald case]({{ 'greifswald-case/' | relative_url }}) is more accurately described as **plausibly explained with an incomplete documentary record** than as either perfectly solved or genuinely inexplicable. The absence of a definitive mission log prevents absolute identification. However, explanations should be judged by how much of the evidence they cover without adding unsupported assumptions.

Military illumination flares account for:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the established Baltic training area;
* the slow downward movement;
* the lack of audible sound;
* the gradual change in spacing;
* the intense reddish-white appearance;
* the disappearance of individual lights;
* the repeated seven-point clusters;
* and the extended display through successive releases.

</div>

The main objections concern uncertain timings, disputed witness descriptions and the missing operational document. None demonstrates that the recorded lights behaved beyond the capabilities of parachute flares. Taken together, the visual and historical evidence makes a military exercise considerably more likely than an unknown aerial craft.

That assessment changes the significance of Greifswald within Mecklenburg-Vorpommern’s UFO history. The incident remains the state’s landmark modern sighting, but its value lies less in evidence for extraordinary technology than in showing how military activity can become a lasting UFO mystery when observers lack distance cues and official information arrives late, indirectly or not at all.
