## Introduction

Most civilian UFO reports in [Brandenburg]({{ 'why-brandenburgs-ufo-record-remains-so/' | relative_url }}) begin with a real light or object but an incorrect interpretation. Bright planets, approaching aircraft, satellite trains, balloons, meteors, drones and camera artefacts account for much of the pattern. This does not mean witnesses are dishonest or careless. It means that the night sky removes the ordinary clues people use to judge distance, size and speed.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Brandenburg_a1d94c_civilian_misidentifi_524c51-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Common Mistakes" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Brandenburg is particularly good at producing convincing mistakes. Its rural districts include exceptionally dark observing sites, while the state also surrounds Berlin and lies beneath busy civil air routes serving Berlin Brandenburg Airport. A distant light may therefore be unusually clear but almost impossible to place. In 2020 alone, the civilian investigation group CENAP received 47 reports from Brandenburg, yet that figure represents initially unidentified observations rather than 47 extraordinary craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moz.de/lokales/rathenow/ufo-ueber-brandenburg_-unbekannte-flugobjekte-leonard-ist-naechster-kandidat-61196141.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moz.de">[Moz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moz.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo über Brandenburg?: Unbekannte Flugobjekte</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MozUfo über Brandenburg?: Unbekannte Flugobjekte - Leonard ist nächster Kandidat &#124; moz.deDecember 1, 2021 — Im Jahr 2020 gab es aus dem B...</span></span></span>

The important question is not whether a witness “saw something”. Usually they did. The question is whether the surviving details distinguish an unusual object from an ordinary one seen under misleading conditions.

## The leading conventional explanations

The same small group of causes repeatedly appears in investigations of German civilian reports. CENAP’s figures illustrate the scale of the identification problem: it received 1,084 reports from Germany, Austria and Switzerland in 2024 and 1,348 in 2025. Reported explanations included planets, stars, Starlink satellites, meteors, balloons, drones, aircraft, rocket activity, space debris and optical effects. Venus, Jupiter and the bright star Sirius are among the most persistent sources of mistaken sightings. They can remain visible for long periods, appear exceptionally bright near the horizon and seem to change colour as their light passes through unstable layers of atmosphere. Several familiar witness descriptions follow naturally:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A “motionless light” remains over the same field or tree line for half an hour.
* The object seems to follow a car because its direction barely changes as the observer travels.
* A light flashes red, blue or green because atmospheric turbulence makes a low star scintillate.
* The object appears to rise, descend or dart when thin clouds cross in front of it or the witness shifts position.

The common error is to treat brightness as evidence of closeness. A planet is bright because it reflects sunlight, not because it is hovering a few kilometres away. Without a visible structure, shadow or background reference, the observer has no reliable way to assign it a distance.

### Aircraft that appear stationary

Brandenburg’s relationship with Berlin’s air traffic makes aircraft one of the most important explanations. German air-traffic control provides historical and current tracking of aircraft movements, including approaches and departures at Berlin Brandenburg Airport. Regular movements are supplemented by helicopters, light aircraft and occasional specialised operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dfs.de/homepage/en/environment/aircraft-noise/flight-paths-and-flight-procedures/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dfs.de">[DFS Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dfs.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">At the top right, you can set a filter for different...</span></span></span>

An aircraft flying almost directly towards an observer can show little sideways movement. Its landing lights may dominate while its navigation lights remain too faint to see. For several minutes it can therefore resemble a brilliant stationary object. Once the aircraft turns across the observer’s line of sight, it may suddenly seem to accelerate sideways or reveal several separate lights.

Aircraft can also appear to change shape. Two landing lights may be interpreted as a wide triangular craft; blinking navigation lights may seem to belong to separate objects; cloud or haze can enlarge a bright source into a glowing patch. At night, even trained pilots are warned that distance is deceptive because there may be no intervening landmarks and no known object against which to compare apparent size.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook/12_afh_ch11.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">12 afh ch11</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A lack of intervening references on the ground and the inability to compare the size...Read more...</span></span></span>

Unusual-looking flight paths are not automatically unexplained either. Berlin Brandenburg Airport conducts periodic night-time survey flights to test its instrument landing systems. Such aircraft must fly prescribed patterns that may involve repeated approaches, turns or passes at hours when residents do not expect normal traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://corporate.berlin-airport.de/en/company-media/corporate-news/2026-04-13-vermessungsfluege.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: corporate.berlin-airport.de">[Berlin Airport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">corporate.berlin-airport.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">As usual, the survey flights take place at night, as air traffic does not allow this during the day and the ILS cannot be used operationa...</span></span></span>

### Satellites and the “string of pearls”

A single satellite usually looks like a steady star moving smoothly across the sky. It does not normally blink, change direction or leave a conventional aircraft trail. The most striking modern variation is a newly launched group of Starlink satellites. Before the satellites spread into their operational orbits, they may cross the sky in a straight, evenly spaced procession often described as a train or string of pearls.

This appearance is sufficiently unfamiliar that it has produced reports from both members of the public and professional pilots. More than 120 of CENAP’s 2025 reports concerned Starlink, according to its published annual figures. Dark rural skies make satellites easier to see, not less likely. Westhavelland’s officially promoted dark-sky area exists precisely because the region offers unusually good naked-eye observation. A witness there may see faint satellites, multiple objects and subtle changes in brightness that would disappear in Berlin’s urban glow.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sternenpark-westhavelland.de/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sternenpark-westhavelland.de">[Sternenpark Westhavelland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sternenpark-westhavelland.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sternenpark Westhavelland Der Sternenpark Westhavelland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sternenpark Westhavelland Der Sternenpark Westhavelland</span></span></span>

A satellite may appear to vanish when it enters the Earth’s shadow. To an observer unfamiliar with orbital illumination, this can look like an object switching off, dissolving or accelerating away.

### Meteors, fireballs and re-entering debris

A normal meteor lasts only a second or two. A bright fireball can last longer, fragment into several pieces, change colour and illuminate thin cloud. These features sometimes lead witnesses to report a burning aircraft or a group of objects travelling in formation.

Re-entering space debris is slower and can be still more dramatic. A rocket body or satellite breaking apart may create a procession of glowing fragments that travels across a large part of the sky. Because several pieces follow roughly the same path, the event can be interpreted as a fleet of lights or a single enormous structure with illuminated sections.

Duration is one of the best initial clues. A flash lasting one or two seconds is more consistent with a meteor than an aircraft. A broad cluster of fragments moving steadily for tens of seconds may suggest re-entry. An object visible in approximately the same position for many minutes is unlikely to be either.

### Balloons, lanterns and drones

Balloons are difficult to judge because they have no standard visual behaviour. A reflective foil balloon can flash brightly as it rotates. An illuminated party balloon may drift silently, stop appearing to move when it travels towards the observer, then change direction with winds at another altitude. Several balloons released together may spread into an apparently coordinated formation.

Drones create a newer layer of uncertainty. Small recreational models, industrial platforms and police drones may hover, reverse direction or carry lighting that does not resemble a conventional aircraft. Brandenburg’s aviation authority regulates unmanned aircraft, including their use within controlled airspace around Berlin Brandenburg Airport, confirming that drones are an established part of the region’s aerial environment rather than an exceptional possibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lubb.berlin-brandenburg.de/aufgaben/nutzung-des-luftraums/unbemannte-fluggeraete/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lubb.berlin-brandenburg.de">[BerlBrandenburg Aviation Authority]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lubb.berlin-brandenburg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berl Brandenburg Aviation Authority Unbemannte Luftfahrzeugsysteme</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berl Brandenburg Aviation Authority Unbemannte Luftfahrzeugsysteme</span></span></span>

Yet “drone” should not become a catch-all answer. A distant light cannot be identified as a drone merely because it seems low or manoeuvrable. Confirmation may require a clear outline, characteristic sound, repeated operation from a known location or supporting flight information. The label can otherwise conceal the same problem as “UFO”: the object remains unidentified, only under a more modern name.

## Why darkness distorts judgement

Human vision is good at recognising objects when there are buildings, roads, trees and other familiar references nearby. A point of light in an empty sky provides almost none of this information. The observer cannot tell whether it is a small nearby object, a large distant aircraft or an astronomical source millions of kilometres away.

Brandenburg’s darkest landscapes intensify this ambiguity. They reveal more of the sky, but fields, lakes and flat horizons may provide few visual references after nightfall. Berlin’s light dome, patches of cloud, mist and isolated ground lighting can create additional layers in which a light appears to sit even when it is far beyond them. Research mapping the transition from Berlin’s urban skyglow into rural Brandenburg found a pronounced brightness gradient and strong changes under cloud cover.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08258" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

Three perceptual mistakes are especially important.

**Apparent motion.** A small stationary light can seem to drift when stared at against a dark, featureless background. This is known as the autokinetic effect. Modern experimental work confirms that observers can perceive movement in a light that is not physically moving.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10965040/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

**Incorrect distance.** A witness may estimate that a light is “just above the trees”, although the trees merely lie along the same line of sight. Without binocular depth cues or a known-size object beside it, the distance estimate may be little more than an impression.

**Exaggerated speed.** Speed estimates depend on distance. A small nearby insect and a faraway aircraft can move through the same angle of vision at very different real speeds. Once the witness assumes the wrong distance, the calculated speed and size become unreliable as well.

These limitations explain why sincere accounts can contain apparently precise but mutually incompatible statements: an object was “the size of a bus”, “two kilometres away” and “moving silently at enormous speed”, even though none of those values was directly measured.

## Cameras can add objects that were never in the sky

A photograph or video is useful only when its limitations are understood. Phones are designed to create an attractive image automatically, not to record scientifically accurate measurements of faint lights. Digital zoom enlarges pixels rather than revealing genuine detail, while autofocus may repeatedly turn a point source into a pulsating disc, diamond or ring.

Internal reflections are another frequent source of confusion. A bright streetlamp, the Moon or an object just outside the frame can reflect between lens elements and create a second luminous spot. Research into smartphone photography describes these reflections as bright spots or “ghosts” produced within the lens system, often appearing in a geometrical relationship to the true light source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15046" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

Camera movement can transform a star or aircraft light into a zigzag trail. Long exposure and night modes may combine several frames, producing breaks, duplicated points or distorted paths when either the phone or the source moves. Out-of-focus insects and birds close to the lens can become large, translucent shapes with no recognisable wings or body.

CENAP reported that many submitted photographs in 2024 were identified as lens reflections or nearby insects and birds rendered out of focus. This is an important corrective to the assumption that photographic evidence is automatically stronger than testimony. In some cases, the witness saw nothing unusual at the time and noticed the supposed object only after inspecting the image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welt.de/255086178" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welt.de">[DIE WELT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIE WELTImmer mehr Deutsche glauben, Ufos zu sehen</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Insgesamt wurden 1.084 Sichtungen aus Deutschland, Österreich und der Schweiz gemeldet, verglichen mit dem üblichen Durchschnitt von 600...</span></span></span>

A strong photographic report therefore needs more than a cropped enlargement. Investigators benefit from the original file, an uncropped view, the direction in which the camera was pointed, the exact time, the phone model and a description of what was visible to the naked eye. A single image without this information may be impossible to resolve even when its cause is conventional.

## Common reporting mistakes

The most damaging weaknesses usually arise during the minutes immediately after a sighting. Witnesses may discuss the event with one another, alter their estimates while replaying a video, or post a compressed copy online before preserving the original.

The most useful civilian report records:

* the exact date and local time;
* the observation point and viewing direction;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* the object’s angular height above the horizon;
* total duration;
* movement relative to a building, tree, star or cloud;
* colour and whether the light was steady or flashing;
* weather and cloud conditions;
* sounds heard before, during and after the observation;
* photographs in their original form;
* separate accounts from witnesses before they compare memories.

</div>

Estimating angular size is more useful than saying an object was “huge”. A witness can hold a hand at arm’s length and compare the object with a fingertip, thumbnail or fist. This does not reveal the object’s real dimensions, but it preserves what was actually observed.

Another mistake is to begin with an extraordinary conclusion and eliminate alternatives selectively. A soundless light is not evidence against an aircraft when it may be many kilometres away. A sudden disappearance is not necessarily evidence of extreme acceleration when a satellite may have entered shadow or an aircraft light may have turned away. An apparently triangular arrangement does not prove a solid triangular craft unless the space between the lights was visibly structured or blocked background stars.

The reverse error also occurs: declaring every report to be a planet, drone or aircraft without checking times and geometry. A proper conventional explanation should match the direction, duration, apparent movement and visibility described. Merely naming a familiar object does not resolve the case.

## When a report remains genuinely unresolved

A report should remain unidentified when the evidence does not support a specific explanation, not because investigators have demonstrated extraordinary performance. In Brandenburg, as elsewhere, many unresolved cases are likely to be cases of insufficient information.

A report is weakly unresolved when it has only a brief recollection, an uncertain time, no direction, no original photograph and no independent witness. Investigators may be unable to test satellite passes, aircraft tracks or astronomical positions. The correct conclusion is then “not enough information”, rather than either “alien craft” or “definitely mundane”.

A stronger unresolved report would include several independent witnesses, precise timing and location, sustained observation, original imagery, a clear path against fixed references and unsuccessful checks against air traffic, satellites, astronomical objects, balloons, drones and known local activity. Even then, unidentified means that the available evidence has not produced a satisfactory match. It does not identify the object’s origin.

Later evidence can strengthen a claim if another observer supplies an independent recording from a different location, allowing position or altitude to be estimated. It can weaken a claim if flight tracking, satellite data or astronomical reconstruction reproduces the reported path. In many civilian cases, the decisive evidence is ordinary metadata rather than a dramatic new photograph.

Brandenburg’s sighting pattern is therefore best understood as an interaction between a visually rich sky and imperfect human observation. Its dark rural areas make faint objects conspicuous; its airport and regional air traffic supply complex moving lights; modern satellites and drones add unfamiliar behaviour; and phone cameras generate persuasive distortions. The small residue left after these possibilities are checked deserves careful documentation, but uncertainty alone is not evidence that something extraordinary crossed the state’s skies.
