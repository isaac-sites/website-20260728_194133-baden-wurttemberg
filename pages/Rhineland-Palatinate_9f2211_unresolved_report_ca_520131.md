## Introduction

An aerial report should not be called genuinely unresolved merely because the original witness could not identify what they saw. A useful classification asks three separate questions: was the object unknown only to the observer, did it remain unexplained after a meaningful investigation, or was it identified as a conventional object—such as a drone—whose operator remained unknown?



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Rhineland-Palatinate_9f2211_unresolved_report_ca_520131-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Case Categories" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That distinction matters especially in [Rhineland-Palatinate]({{ 'why-rhineland-palatinate-reports-so-many/' | relative_url }}). A brief light seen over the countryside, an archival pilot report near Ramstein and a detected drone entering restricted military airspace may all be described casually as “unidentified”, yet they represent very different evidence and require different responses. The first may need routine checking, the second careful historical assessment, and the third an immediate security investigation. Treating them as one phenomenon exaggerates weak UFO reports while obscuring genuine aviation and infrastructure risks.

## Unknown to the original witness

Most reports begin in the simplest category: **unidentified by the person who saw them**. This is a description of the witness’s knowledge at the time, not a conclusion about the object itself.

A witness may accurately report a bright light, an apparent formation or an object that seemed to move unusually. Even a sincere and observant person may lack the information needed to recognise a distant aircraft, satellite train, planet, meteor, balloon or drone. Night observations are particularly difficult because distance, size and speed cannot be estimated reliably without reference points. A stationary light may appear to move because the observer or surrounding clouds are moving; an aircraft coming almost directly towards the observer may appear motionless before seeming to accelerate sideways.

German civilian investigators repeatedly identify ordinary causes after comparing reports with astronomical positions, satellite passes, flight activity, weather and photographs. CENAP, the long-running German reporting centre, says that many reports reaching it involve planets, satellites, aircraft and other familiar objects that appeared unfamiliar under the conditions of observation. The large number of reports is therefore not the same as a large number of unexplained cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deutschland.de/en/topic/knowledge/ufos-over-germany-hansjuergen-koehler-and-cenap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deutschland.de">[Deutschland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deutschland.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs over Germany: An expert provides clarity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A hotline for UFO sightings. The correct initial label is usually **unidentified observation pending checks**, not “unexplained object”. That wording preserves the witness’s experience without giving the report more evidential weight than it has earned.

Investigators should first establish the basic facts:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the exact date, time and observing position;
* the direction and angular height of the object;
* how long it was visible;
* whether it moved relative to buildings, stars or the horizon;
* weather, cloud and visibility conditions;
* whether photographs retain original metadata;
* whether other witnesses were together or genuinely independent;
* and whether aircraft, satellites, astronomical objects or public events match the observation.

</div>

Missing information is itself important. Project Blue Book, the historical United States Air Force UFO programme, distinguished reports lacking essentials such as time, place, duration, weather or direction from cases that remained unidentified despite containing the information needed for analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/proj_b1.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113513-837" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">proj b1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">proj b1</span></span></span> The French space agency’s GEIPAN investigation service makes a similar distinction today: its category C covers reports that cannot be identified because the data are inadequate, whereas category D is reserved for phenomena not identified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/faq-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">data or information.Read more...</span></span></span>

This prevents a common error in UFO discussion: interpreting absence of an explanation as evidence of an extraordinary object. Sometimes no conclusion is possible because the sighting lasted five seconds, was reported months later and contains no reliable direction, image or comparison point. Such a report may remain open in an archive, but it is better described as **indeterminate through lack of evidence** than as a strong mystery.

## Unresolved after meaningful investigation

A stronger category is **unresolved after meaningful investigation**. To qualify, a report must contain enough detail for ordinary explanations to be tested, and those tests must actually have been carried out.

This does not require perfect evidence. Historical cases rarely provide modern radar files, calibrated photographs and multiple instrument readings. It does require more than a witness statement followed by the phrase “no explanation was found”. Investigators should be able to show which possibilities were considered, what records were checked, why plausible matches failed and which uncertainties remained.

A useful unresolved case would normally have several of the following features:

* precise time and location data;
* a sufficiently long observation to record behaviour;
* independent witnesses separated from one another;
* original photographs or video rather than compressed copies;
* radar, [air-traffic]({{ 'air-traffic-35a486/' | relative_url }}), radio or other sensor information;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an identifiable chain of custody for records;
* checks against aircraft, satellites, balloons, astronomy and weather;
* and an investigation conducted while records and memories were still available.

</div>

NASA’s independent UAP study warned that analysis is often hindered by poor sensor calibration, missing metadata, a lack of multiple measurements and the absence of baseline data showing what ordinary traffic looks like in the same environment. It recommended systematic collection through multiple well-calibrated sensors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Independent Study Team Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportSeptember 13, 2023 — At present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 13, 2023</span></span></span> This is directly relevant to Rhineland-Palatinate, where military flights, civil air routes, satellites and local drone use create a crowded background against which apparently unusual movement must be assessed.

“Unresolved” should therefore be treated as a provisional evidence classification, not an explanation. It means that the available information supports investigation but does not justify a confident identification. It does not by itself establish advanced technology, hostile activity or an extraterrestrial origin. Project Blue Book ultimately left 701 of 12,618 reports unidentified, yet the Air Force stated that it had found no evidence that those cases demonstrated technology beyond contemporary scientific knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/press/press-releases/2020/nr20-19" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nr20 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives50 Years Ago: Government stops investigating UFOs20 Nov 2019 — Project Blue Book&#x27;s termination on December 17, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 17, 1969</span></span></span>

### The Ramstein report as a borderline example

The 26 March 1962 pilot sighting associated with Ramstein Air Base illustrates why archival labels require careful reading. The report entered Project Blue Book and was classified as unidentified. That makes it more significant within Rhineland-Palatinate’s UFO history than an uninvestigated newspaper anecdote, because it survives in an official case system whose records are now held by the US National Archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span></span></span>

Nevertheless, the observation was extremely brief. The pilot reportedly saw a fast object for only several seconds and described changing impressions of its shape. With no publicly established supporting radar track, photograph or prolonged multi-witness observation, the case cannot sustain precise claims about dimensions, speed or propulsion. Its unresolved status means that investigators did not establish a satisfactory match from the information available; it does not convert a fleeting visual estimate into proof of an exotic craft.

The fairest modern classification is therefore **historically unresolved but evidentially limited**. It deserves preservation and comparison with other aviation reports, while remaining weaker than a case supported by independent sensors. Later retellings should not upgrade its status by adding certainty absent from the original file.

## Confirmed conventional objects with unknown operators

A third category is often confused with UFO cases but belongs primarily to airspace security: **a conventional object has been identified, but its operator, purpose or launch point is unknown**.

This applies when observers or sensors establish that the object was a drone, aircraft or balloon, even though authorities cannot determine who controlled it. The object is no longer unidentified in the ordinary physical sense. The unresolved issue is attribution.

That distinction is crucial around Ramstein Air Base. Ramstein expressly prohibits personal drone use on the installation because unauthorised flights can threaten aircraft operations, public safety and security resources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramstein.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3857472/ramstein-ab-is-a-no-drone-zone/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramstein.af.mil">[Ramstein Air Base]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramstein.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ramstein Air Base Ramstein AB is a no drone zone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ramstein Air Base Ramstein AB is a no drone zone</span></span></span> Reports in December 2024 concerned suspected drones over or near Ramstein and other sensitive German sites. Public reporting indicated that the objects were treated as possible unmanned aircraft, while their operators and intentions had not been publicly established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.twz.com/air/u-s-air-force-confirms-drone-sightings-over-ramstein-air-base-in-germany" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: twz.com">[TWZ]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">twz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">u s air force confirms drone sightings over ramstein air base in germany</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">u s air force confirms drone sightings over ramstein air base in germany</span></span></span>

Calling such an incident simply a “mysterious UFO sighting” would be misleading. It could erase the strongest part of the evidence—that authorities had reason to classify the objects as drones—while encouraging speculation about their physical nature. The appropriate description is **confirmed or strongly assessed drone activity, attribution unresolved**, with confidence levels stated where necessary.

The same separation should be maintained between three questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Detection:** Was something reliably observed by people or sensors?
2. **Identification:** What class of object was it?
3. **Attribution:** Who operated it, and for what purpose?

</div>

An investigation may answer the first two and fail on the third. German parliamentary material on hybrid threats notes that sanctions or other responses depend on attributing an incident, a process that can require extensive and lengthy investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/012/2101231.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dserver.bundestag.de">[Bundestag]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dserver.bundestag.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bundestag.de.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2025</span></span></span> Failure to identify an operator therefore does not return a confirmed drone to the category of physically unexplained phenomena.

This classification also guards against premature accusations. A drone near a military installation may be flown by a careless hobbyist, a commercial operator, a journalist, a criminal group or a state-linked intelligence actor. Location and behaviour can justify a serious security response, but they do not by themselves prove espionage or foreign control.

## A practical classification for Rhineland-Palatinate

For public archives, local reporting and future case summaries, a five-level system would make the state’s records easier to interpret.

**1. Identified.**

The evidence supports a specific explanation such as an aircraft, satellite, astronomical object, balloon, meteor, drone or optical artefact.

**2. Probably identified.**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ntified due to lack of data or information.Read more...</span></span></span>

One conventional explanation fits the time, direction and appearance better than alternatives, but a small evidential gap prevents complete certainty. This is similar to GEIPAN’s distinction between perfectly and probably identified reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/faq-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">data or information.Read more...</span></span></span>

**3. Indeterminate because of insufficient information.**

The report cannot be tested properly because essential details, original media or timely records are missing. This category should not be described as a strong unexplained case.

**4. Unresolved after investigation.**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ntified due to lack of data or information.Read more...</span></span></span>

The report contains adequate information, plausible conventional explanations have been meaningfully examined, and no explanation fits with reasonable confidence. The remaining uncertainty and the limits of the investigation should be stated explicitly.

**5. Conventional object, attribution unresolved.**

The object class—most commonly a drone—is known or strongly supported, but the operator or purpose remains unknown. This is primarily an aviation, policing or national-security matter rather than evidence of an unknown type of craft.

These categories should be revisable. New flight records may identify an old observation; original video may reveal a camera artefact; several independent reports may strengthen a case previously judged too weak. GEIPAN explicitly allows cases to be reconsidered when new information becomes available, a useful model for historical material from Rhineland-Palatinate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ntified due to lack of data or information.Read more...</span></span></span>

## What should appear in a public case file

A public-facing classification is most useful when readers can see why it was assigned. Each case entry should separate observation from interpretation and include a concise audit trail.

The file should state:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Reported event:** what the witness or sensor recorded, without embellishment.
* **Evidence retained:** testimony, original images, radar data, correspondence or official records.
* **Checks completed:** astronomy, satellite passes, flight activity, weather, drone information and duplicate reports.
* **Best explanation:** including a confidence level.
* **Remaining gap:** exactly what prevents a firmer conclusion.
* **Current category:** identified, probable, insufficient information, unresolved after investigation, or known object with unknown operator.
* **Revision history:** whether later information strengthened or weakened the original claim.

</div>

Language should also reflect evidence quality. “The witness estimated Mach 2” is different from “radar measured Mach 2”. “No aircraft was identified in the available file” is different from “there were no aircraft in the area”. “Investigators could not determine the drone operator” is different from “the drone was untraceable”. These small distinctions prevent uncertain statements from hardening into supposed facts as stories are repeated.

## The category is not the conclusion

The most responsible use of “unresolved” is narrow and temporary. It should indicate that a report survived a defined level of inquiry, not that every ordinary explanation has been ruled out or that an extraordinary explanation has been established.

For Rhineland-Palatinate, this approach keeps three very different parts of the record in proportion. Common civilian sightings can be assessed without dismissing honest witnesses. The 1962 Ramstein report can retain its legitimate archival interest without being presented as stronger than its evidence. Modern [drone incursions]({{ 'drone-incursions/' | relative_url }}) can be treated as concrete security problems even where attribution remains uncertain.

Clear categories do not remove mystery from the subject. They show where the mystery actually lies: in a witness’s initial uncertainty, in missing evidence, in a stubbornly unresolved observation, or in the identity and intentions of someone operating a recognisable aircraft.
